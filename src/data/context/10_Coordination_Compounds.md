### 1.1 Coordination Entity & Central Metal Atom/Ion

- **Coordination Entity:** A coordination entity constitutes a central metal atom or ion bonded to a fixed number of ions or neutral molecules (ligands) arranged in a definite spatial geometry.

- **Central Metal Atom/Ion:** Acts as an electron-pair acceptor (Lewis acid). Usually a transition metal element possessing vacant $(n-1)d$, $ns$, $np$, and $nd$ orbitals of suitable energies.

- **Coordination Sphere:** The central metal and its directly coordinated ligands enclosed within square brackets $[\dots]$. The species within the coordination sphere do not ionise in aqueous solution.

- **Ionisation Sphere / Counter Ions:** The ionisable species written outside the square brackets that balance the electrical charge of the coordination entity.

### 1.2 Ligands, Classification & Denticity

**A ligand** is an ion or neutral molecule containing at least one lone pair of electrons capable of being donated to the central metal atom/ion via a coordinate covalent (dative) bond (Lewis base).

**Classification by Denticity (Number of Donor Atoms):**

#### 1. Unidentate / Monodentate Ligands
Bind through a single donor atom:
- **Neutral:** $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (aqua), $\text{NH}_3$ (ammine), $\text{CO}$ (carbonyl), $\text{NO}$ (nitrosyl), $\text{C}_5\text{H}_5\text{N}$ (pyridine, py).
- **Anionic:** $\text{Cl}^-$ (chlorido), $\text{F}^-$ (fluorido), $\text{Br}^-$ (bromido), $\text{CN}^-$ (cyanido), $\text{OH}^-$ (hydroxido), $\text{NO}_2^-$ (nitrito-N), $\text{ONO}^-$ (nitrito-O).

#### 2. Didentate / Bidentate Ligands
Possess two donor atoms capable of simultaneously coordinating to the metal ion:
- **Ethylenediamine (ethane-1,2-diamine, en):** Neutral, symmetrical, coordinates via two nitrogen donor atoms ($\text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{NH}_2$).
- **Oxalate ion ($\text{ox}^{2-}$ / $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$):** Anionic ($-2$), symmetrical, coordinates via two negatively charged oxygen atoms.
- **Glycinato ion ($\text{gly}^-$):** Anionic ($-1$), unsymmetrical, coordinates via one nitrogen ($\text{NH}_2$) and one carboxylate oxygen ($\text{COO}^-$).
- **Dimethylglyoximato ion ($\text{DMG}^-$):** Anionic ($-1$), coordinates via two nitrogen atoms; forms a distinctive hydrogen-bonded red complex $[\text{Ni}(\text{DMG})_2]$.
- **2,2'-Bipyridine (bipy) and 1,10-Phenanthroline (phen):** Neutral aromatic bidentate ligands.

#### 3. Polydentate Ligands
Possess three or more donor atoms per ligand molecule:
- **Tridentate:** Diethylenetriamine (dien, 3 N-donors).
- **Tetr dentate:** Triethylenetetramine (trien, 4 N-donors).
- **Hexadentate:** Ethylenediaminetetraacetate ion ($[\text{EDTA}]^{4-}$), coordinates through two tertiary amine nitrogen atoms and four carboxylate oxygen atoms.

**Ambidentate Ligands:** Monodentate ligands possessing two different potential donor atoms, capable of coordinating through either one at a time:
- $\text{NO}_2^-$: Coordinates through nitrogen ($\text{M}-\text{NO}_2$, nitrito-N / nitro) or through oxygen ($\text{M}-\text{ONO}$, nitrito-O).
- $\text{SCN}^-$: Coordinates through sulfur ($\text{M}-\text{SCN}$, thiocyanato-S) or through nitrogen ($\text{M}-\text{NCS}$, isothiocyanato-N).
- $\text{CN}^-$: Coordinates through carbon ($\text{M}-\text{CN}$, cyanido-C) or through nitrogen ($\text{M}-\text{NC}$, isocyanido-N).
- $\text{S}_2\text{O}_3^{2-}$: Coordinates through sulfur or oxygen.

**Chelation & The Chelate Effect:**
- When a di- or polydentate ligand binds to a central metal ion to form a closed ring structure (usually 5- or 6-membered), the ligand is termed a chelating ligand and the resulting complex is a chelate.
- **Chelate Effect:** Chelated complexes exhibit significantly enhanced thermodynamic and kinetic stability compared to analogous complexes containing only monodentate ligands.
- **Thermodynamic Origin:** Entropy driven ($\Delta S^\circ > 0$), as displacement of multiple monodentate solvent molecules by a single polydentate chelate increases the net number of independent particles in solution.
- **Macrocyclic Effect:** Chelates with pre-organized cyclic ligands (e.g., porphyrin rings, crown ethers) exhibit stability even higher than open-chain chelates.

### 1.3 Coordination Number (C.N.) vs. Oxidation State (O.S.)

- **Coordination Number (C.N.):** The total number of ligand donor atoms directly bonded to the central metal atom/ion:
  $$\text{C.N.} = \sum_{i} (\text{Number of ligands}_i \times \text{Denticity}_i)$$
  - Example: In $[\text{Co}(\text{en})_3]^{3+}$, $\text{C.N.} = 3 \times 2 = 6$.
  - Common coordination numbers in transition metal complexes are 4 and 6.

- **Oxidation State (O.S.):** The residual electrical charge the central metal atom would carry if all coordinated ligands were removed along with the electron pairs shared with them:
  $$\text{Charge on Complex} = \text{O.S. of Metal} + \sum (\text{Ligand Charges})$$

- **Double Salt vs. Coordination Complex:**
  - **Double Salt:** Molecular addition compound that dissociates completely into its individual simple ions when dissolved in water (e.g., Mohr's salt $\text{FeSO}_4\cdot(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4\cdot6\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{2+} + 2\text{NH}_4^+ + 2\text{SO}_4^{2-} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$; Potash alum $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4\cdot\text{Al}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3\cdot24\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
  - **Coordination Complex:** Retains its structural identity in aqueous solution; the coordination entity does not dissociate into constituent simple ions (e.g., $\text{K}_4[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]$ yields $4\text{K}^+$ and $[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{4-}$, but no free $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ or $\text{CN}^-$ ions).

### 1.4 Homoleptic vs. Heteroleptic & Kinetic Lability vs. Inertness

- **Homoleptic Complexes:** Central metal is bonded to only one type of donor ligand (e.g., $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$, $[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{4-}$).

- **Heteroleptic Complexes:** Central metal is coordinated to more than one type of ligand (e.g., $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]^+$, $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_2\text{Cl}_2]$).

- **Labile Complexes:** Undergo rapid ligand substitution reactions within seconds (e.g., $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$).

- **Inert Complexes:** Undergo very slow ligand substitution, requiring hours or days (e.g., $[\text{Cr}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}$, $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$).

- **Electronic Criteria for Inertness:** Complexes with $d^3$ or low-spin $d^6$ configurations (filled or half-filled stable $t_{2g}$ subshells) are kinetically inert.

- **Critical JEE Distinction:** Thermodynamic stability (governed by formation constant $\beta$) is fundamentally distinct from kinetic inertness/lability. For instance, $[\text{Ni}(\text{CN})_4]^{2-}$ has a massive formation constant (thermodynamically exceptionally stable), yet exchanges cyanide with $^{14}\text{CN}^-$ instantaneously (kinetically labile).

### 1.5 Systematic IUPAC Nomenclature Rules

1. **Order of Naming:** The cation is named first, followed by the anion, regardless of whether the complex entity is cationic, neutral, or anionic.

2. **Inside the Coordination Entity:** Ligands are named in strictly alphabetical order before the central metal atom/ion.
   - **Multiplicative Prefixes:** Multiplying prefixes (di-, tri-, tetra-, penta-, hexa-) and structural prefixes (cis-, trans-, fac-, mer-) are completely ignored when determining alphabetical order.
   - **Complex Ligands:** Ligands containing numerical prefixes or complex names utilize bis- (2), tris- (3), tetrakis- (4), pentakis- (5) with the ligand name placed inside parentheses (e.g., tris(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III)).

3. **Anionic Ligand Endings:** Terminate with the suffix '-ido' (or '-ato', '-ito'):
   - $\text{Cl}^-$: chlorido; $\text{CN}^-$: cyanido; $\text{OH}^-$: hydroxido; $\text{O}^{2-}$: oxido; $\text{O}_2^{2-}$: peroxido; $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$: oxalato; $\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$: acetato.

4. **Neutral Ligand Conventions:** Retain their common names, with four specific mandatory exceptions:
   - $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: aqua
   - $\text{NH}_3$: ammine (strictly spelled with double 'm')
   - $\text{CO}$: carbonyl
   - $\text{NO}$: nitrosyl

5. **Naming of Central Metal:**
   - **In Cationic or Neutral Complexes:** Metal retains its standard elemental English name (e.g., cobalt, iron, platinum, copper).
   - **In Anionic Complexes:** Metal name takes the Latin stem ending with the suffix '-ate':
     - Iron $\rightarrow$ ferrate
     - Copper $\rightarrow$ cuprate
     - Silver $\rightarrow$ argentate
     - Gold $\rightarrow$ aurate
     - Lead $\rightarrow$ plumbate
     - Tin $\rightarrow$ stannate
     - Cobalt $\rightarrow$ cobaltate; Platinum $\rightarrow$ platinate; Aluminum $\rightarrow$ aluminate; Zinc $\rightarrow$ zincate.

6. **Oxidation State:** Designated by Roman numerals in round parentheses immediately following the metal name without any intervening space (e.g., iron(II), cobalt(III)). If the oxidation state is zero, it is denoted as (0).

### 2.1 Werner's Fundamental Postulates

Alfred Werner (Nobel Prize 1893) established the structural foundation of coordination chemistry:

7. **Primary Valency (Principal Valency):**
   - Ionisable in polar solvents.
   - Satisfied exclusively by negative ions (anions).
   - Corresponds directly to the formal oxidation state of the central metal.
   - Non-directional in nature.

8. **Secondary Valency (Auxiliary Valency):**
   - Non-ionisable under standard conditions.
   - Satisfied by neutral molecules or anions.
   - Corresponds directly to the coordination number of the central metal.
   - Highly directional in three-dimensional space, determining the definite stereochemical geometry of the coordination entity.

### 2.2 Classical Cobalt(III) Ammines Series Analytics

Werner investigated the series $\text{CoCl}_3\cdot n\text{NH}_3$ ($n = 6, 5, 4, 3$) by measuring electrical conductance and quantitative precipitation with excess aqueous silver nitrate ($\text{AgNO}_3$):

| Complex Formulation | Modern Structural Formula | Colour | Mol AgCl Precipitated per Mol Complex | Total Ions Produced | Electrolytic Type | Electrical Conductance |
|-------------------|--------------------------|--------|--------------------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| $\text{CoCl}_3\cdot 6\text{NH}_3$ | $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]\text{Cl}_3$ | Yellow-Orange | $3\text{ mol AgCl}$ | 4 | $1:3$ electrolyte | Highest |
| $\text{CoCl}_3\cdot 5\text{NH}_3$ | $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{Cl}]\text{Cl}_2$ | Purple | $2\text{ mol AgCl}$ | 3 | $1:2$ electrolyte | Intermediate |
| $\text{CoCl}_3\cdot 4\text{NH}_3$ | $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]\text{Cl}$ | Green (trans) / Violet (cis) | $1\text{ mol AgCl}$ | 2 | $1:1$ electrolyte | Low |
| $\text{CoCl}_3\cdot 3\text{NH}_3$ | $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_3\text{Cl}_3]$ | Blue-Green | $0\text{ mol AgCl}$ | 0 | Non-electrolyte | Zero / Negligible |

- **Critical Insight on Dual Character:** Chloride ions residing inside the coordination sphere satisfy both primary valency (contributing to $+3$ oxidation state) and secondary valency (occupying coordination positions). Coordinated chloride ions cannot be precipitated by $\text{AgNO}_3$ at room temperature.

### 3.1 Sidgwick's Electronic Model & Formula

Sidgwick proposed that complex stability is maximized when the central metal atom/ion acquires the stable closed-shell electronic configuration of the nearest noble gas ($Kr: 36$, $Xe: 54$, $Rn: 86$):

$$\text{EAN} = Z - (\text{O.S.}) + 2 \times (\text{C.N.})$$

where:
- $Z$ is the atomic number of the central transition metal.
- $\text{O.S.}$ is the oxidation state of the metal ion.
- $\text{C.N.}$ is the coordination number (assuming monodentate ligands donating 2 electrons each). For polydentate ligands, add the total number of donated electrons.

### 3.2 High-Yield Exam EAN Calculations

- **Potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II), $\text{K}_4[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]$:**
  $$\text{Fe}(Z = 26), \text{ O.S.} = +2, \text{ C.N.} = 6 \implies \text{EAN} = 26 - 2 + (2 \times 6) = 36 \quad (\text{Kr configuration, Highly Stable})$$

- **Nickel tetracarbonyl, $[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4]$:**
  $$\text{Ni}(Z = 28), \text{ O.S.} = 0, \text{ C.N.} = 4 \implies \text{EAN} = 28 - 0 + (2 \times 4) = 36 \quad (\text{Kr configuration})$$

- **Hexaamminecobalt(III) ion, $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$:**
  $$\text{Co}(Z = 27), \text{ O.S.} = +3, \text{ C.N.} = 6 \implies \text{EAN} = 27 - 3 + (2 \times 6) = 36 \quad (\text{Kr configuration})$$

- **Decacarbonyldimanganese(0), $\text{Mn}2(\text{CO}){10}$:**
  - For each Mn ($Z = 25$): $\text{O.S.} = 0$, five terminal $\text{CO}$ ligands donate $5 \times 2 = 10$ electrons, plus 1 electron shared via the central $\text{Mn}-\text{Mn}$ metal-metal covalent single bond:
  $$\text{EAN} = 25 - 0 + 10 + 1 = 36 \quad (\text{Kr configuration})$$

- **Vanadium hexacarbonyl, $[\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]$:**
  $$\text{V}(Z = 23), \text{ O.S.} = 0, \text{ C.N.} = 6 \implies \text{EAN} = 23 - 0 + 12 = 35 \quad (\text{17-electron radical})$$
  - Trap: $[\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]$ possesses 35 electrons, fails the EAN rule, is paramagnetic, and readily acts as a powerful single-electron oxidizing agent to form the stable 36-electron $[\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]^-$ anion.

### 4.1 Principles & Postulates

Proposed by Linus Pauling, VBT accounts for bonding, geometry, and magnetic behaviour via atomic orbital hybridization:

9. The central metal ion provides vacant hybrid orbitals equal to its coordination number.
10. Under the influence of strong field ligands, electrons in the $(n-1)d$ subshell may be forced to pair up against Hund's rule, vacating inner $d$-orbitals.
11. Ligands donate lone pairs into these vacant hybrid orbitals to establish directional coordinate bonds.


#### $sp^3$ Hybridization (Tetrahedral)
Occurs when inner $d$-orbitals are unavailable for bonding (e.g., $d^{10}$ systems or weak field ligands unable to pair $d^8$ electrons).

- **Example: Tetrachloridonickelate(II), $[\text{NiCl}_4]^{2-}$:**
  - $\text{Ni}^{2+} (3d^8)$: Weak field $\text{Cl}^-$ ligands do not force pairing. Electron configuration remains $3d_{xy}^2 3d_{yz}^2 3d_{zx}^2 3d_{x^2-y^2}^1 3d_{z^2}^1$.
  - Vacant $4s$ and three $4p$ orbitals hybridize to form $sp^3$ hybrid orbitals.
  - Geometry: Tetrahedral; $n = 2$ unpaired electrons $\implies$ Paramagnetic ($\mu = \sqrt{2(4)} = \sqrt{8} \approx 2.83\text{ BM}$).

- **Example: Nickel tetracarbonyl, $[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4]$:**
  - $\text{Ni}^0 (3d^8 4s^2)$: Strong $\text{CO}$ ligand forces the two $4s$ electrons into the $3d$ subshell, completing the $3d^{10}$ configuration.
  - Empty $4s$ and $4p$ orbitals form $sp^3$ hybrids.
  - Geometry: Tetrahedral; $n = 0$ unpaired electrons $\implies$ Diamagnetic!

#### $dsp^2$ Hybridization (Square Planar)
Occurs when strong field ligands force pairing in $d^8$ configurations, vacating the inner $3d_{x^2-y^2}$ orbital.

- **Example: Tetracyanidonickelate(II), $[\text{Ni}(\text{CN})_4]^{2-}$:**
  - $\text{Ni}^{2+} (3d^8)$: Strong $\text{CN}^-$ forces pairing into four $3d$ orbitals. The vacant $3d_{x^2-y^2}$, $4s$, and two $4p$ orbitals hybridize into $dsp^2$.
  - Geometry: Square planar; $n = 0$ unpaired electrons $\implies$ Diamagnetic!

- **4d and 5d Metal Invariant:** Complexes of $\text{Pt}^{2+}$ and $\text{Pd}^{2+}$ (e.g., $[\text{PtCl}_4]^{2-}, [\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$) are invariably square planar ($dsp^2$) and diamagnetic, regardless of whether ligands are halogens or strong field ligands, due to very large ligand-field splitting.

- **The $[\text{Cu}(\text{NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$ Case:** $\text{Cu}^{2+} (3d^9)$. Has 1 unpaired electron ($n = 1, \mu \approx 1.73\text{ BM}$). Exhibits square planar geometry ($dsp^2$) through Jahn-Teller tetragonal distortion or promotion of the odd electron to $4p_z$.


#### Inner Orbital Octahedral Complexes ($d^2sp^3$)
Involve $(n-1)d$, $ns$, and $np$ orbitals. Termed low-spin, spin-paired, or covalent complexes.

- **Example: Hexaamminecobalt(III), $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$:**
  - $\text{Co}^{3+} (3d^6)$: $\text{NH}_3$ acts as a strong field ligand for $\text{Co}^{3+}$, forcing complete electron pairing in the $3d$ subshell ($t_{2g}^6$). Two vacant $3d$, one $4s$, and three $4p$ orbitals form $d^2sp^3$ hybrids.
  - Geometry: Octahedral; $n = 0 \implies$ Diamagnetic.

- **Example: Hexacyanidoferrate(II), $[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]^{4-}$:** $\text{Fe}^{2+} (3d^6)$, low spin, $d^2sp^3$, diamagnetic ($n = 0$).

#### Outer Orbital Octahedral Complexes ($sp^3d^2$)
Involve $ns$, $np$, and outer $nd$ orbitals. Termed high-spin, spin-free, or ionic complexes.

- **Example: Hexafluoridocobaltate(III), $[\text{CoF}_6]^{3-}$:**
  - $\text{Co}^{3+} (3d^6)$: Weak field $\text{F}^-$ cannot force pairing. Two outer $4d$ orbitals are used along with $4s$ and $4p$ to form $sp^3d^2$ hybrids.
  - Geometry: Octahedral; $n = 4$ unpaired electrons $\implies$ Highly paramagnetic ($\mu = \sqrt{4(6)} = \sqrt{24} \approx 4.90\text{ BM}$).

- **Example: Hexafluoroferrate(III), $[\text{FeF}_6]^{3-}$:** $\text{Fe}^{3+} (3d^5)$, high spin, $sp^3d^2$, $n = 5$ unpaired electrons ($\mu = \sqrt{35} \approx 5.92\text{ BM}$).

### 4.4 Spin-Only Magnetic Moment Formula

$$\mu_s = \sqrt{n(n + 2)} \quad \text{B.M. (Bohr Magnetons)}$$

where $n$ is the number of unpaired electrons:

- $n = 1 \implies \mu_s = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.73\text{ BM}$
- $n = 2 \implies \mu_s = \sqrt{8} \approx 2.83\text{ BM}$
- $n = 3 \implies \mu_s = \sqrt{15} \approx 3.87\text{ BM}$
- $n = 4 \implies \mu_s = \sqrt{24} \approx 4.90\text{ BM}$
- $n = 5 \implies \mu_s = \sqrt{35} \approx 5.92\text{ BM}$

### 5.1 Physical Model & Orbital Splitting

CFT treats the interaction between metal ion and ligands as purely electrostatic (point charges for anions, point dipoles for neutral ligands). The five degenerate $d$-orbitals of the free gaseous metal ion experience non-uniform electrostatic repulsion from approaching ligands, causing splitting into distinct energy levels.

### 5.2 Octahedral Crystal Field Splitting ($\Delta_o$)

In an octahedral field, six ligands approach along the $\pm x, \pm y, \pm z$ Cartesian axes.

- Orbitals directed along the axes ($d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}$, belonging to the $e_g$ set) experience strong repulsion and are elevated in energy by $+0.6\Delta_o$ ($+\frac{3}{5}\Delta_o$) above the barycenter.
- Orbitals directed between the axes ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{zx}$, belonging to the $t_{2g}$ set) experience lower repulsion and are lowered in energy by $-0.4\Delta_o$ ($-\frac{2}{5}\Delta_o$) below the barycenter.

### 5.3 Tetrahedral Crystal Field Splitting ($\Delta_t$)

In a tetrahedral field, four ligands approach alternate corners of a circumscribed cube (between axes).

- Orbitals directed between axes ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{zx}$, belonging to the $t_2$ set) experience greater repulsion and rise by $+0.4\Delta_t$.
- Orbitals directed along axes ($d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}$, belonging to the $e$ set) are stabilized by $-0.6\Delta_t$.
- Splitting is inverted compared to octahedral fields and lacks the 'g' subscript due to absence of an inversion center.
- **Mathematical Relation:** $$\Delta_t = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$$
- **Universal Consequence:** Because $\Delta_t$ is always substantially smaller than the pairing energy ($\Delta_t < P$), tetrahedral complexes are strictly HIGH-SPIN (no low-spin tetrahedral complexes exist).

### 5.4 The Spectrochemical Series

Experimentally determined order of ligand field splitting power:
$$\text{I}^- < \text{Br}^- < \text{S}^{2-} < \text{SCN}^- < \text{Cl}^- < \text{NO}_3^- < \text{F}^- < \text{OH}^- < \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} < \text{H}_2\text{O} < \text{NCS}^- < \text{edta}^{4-} < \text{NH}_3 < \text{en} < \text{bpy} < \text{phen} < \text{NO}_2^- < \text{CN}^- < \text{CO}$$

- Halide donors and oxygen donors are predominantly weak-field ligands.
- Nitrogen donors, cyanide, and carbonyl are strong-field ligands.

### 5.5 Electron Configurations & CFSE Calculations

- **Pairing Energy ($P$):** The electrostatic energy cost required to force two electrons to occupy the same spatial orbital with paired spins.
- **If $\Delta_o < P$ (Weak field ligand):** High-spin configuration is formed; electrons occupy $e_g$ before pairing in $t_{2g}$.
- **If $\Delta_o > P$ (Strong field ligand):** Low-spin configuration is formed; complete pairing occurs in $t_{2g}$ before $e_g$ is populated.
- **CFSE Formula for Octahedral Complexes:**
  $$\text{CFSE} = \left[-0.4\cdot n(t_{2g}) + 0.6\cdot n(e_g)\right]\Delta_o + mP$$
  where $m$ is the net increase in electron pairs relative to the free ion.

#### Mechanism 1: $d-d$ Transitions
- When visible light shines on a transition metal complex with partially filled $d$-orbitals, an electron absorbs energy equal to $\Delta_o$ and transitions from $t_{2g}$ to $e_g$. The observed colour of the complex is the complementary colour of the absorbed wavelength.
- **Absence of Colour:** Complexes with $d^0$ (e.g., $\text{Sc}^{3+}, \text{Ti}^{4+}$) or $d^{10}$ (e.g., $\text{Zn}^{2+}, \text{Cu}^+, \text{Cd}^{2+}$) configurations cannot undergo $d-d$ transitions and are invariably white or colourless in absence of charge transfer.
- **Anhydrous vs. Hydrated:** Anhydrous $\text{CuSO}_4$ is colourless (no ligand field), whereas hydrated $\text{CuSO}_4\cdot 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is vibrant blue due to $d-d$ transitions in $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_4]^{2+}$.

#### Mechanism 2: Charge-Transfer Spectra (LMCT / MLCT)
- Produces exceptionally high molar extinction coefficients (intensities $\sim 1000\times$ stronger than Laporte-forbidden $d-d$ transitions).
- **Ligand-to-Metal Charge Transfer (LMCT):** Electron density transfers transiently from ligand molecular orbitals into empty metal $d$-orbitals.
- **High-Yield Exam Examples:**
  - **Potassium permanganate, $\text{KMnO}_4$:** Intense purple/magenta colour in $\text{Mn}^{7+} (d^0)$ arises from $\text{O}^{2-} \rightarrow \text{Mn}^{7+}$ LMCT.
  - **Potassium dichromate, $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$:** Bright orange colour in $\text{Cr}^{6+} (d^0)$ arises from $\text{O}^{2-} \rightarrow \text{Cr}^{6+}$ LMCT.
  - **Mercuric iodide, $\text{HgI}_2$:** Brilliant scarlet red colour.
  - **Ferric thiocyanate, $[\text{Fe}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{SCN})]^{2+}$:** Intense blood-red colour.

### 5.7 Jahn-Teller Distortion

- **Theorem:** Any non-linear molecular system in a degenerate electronic state is unstable and will undergo geometrical distortion that lowers its symmetry and removes the electronic degeneracy.
- **Strongly Active Cases:** Asymmetrical filling of the $e_g$ set (which points directly at ligands).
  - High-spin $d^4$ ($t_{2g}^3 e_g^1$, e.g., $\text{Cr}^{2+}$)
  - Low-spin $d^7$ ($t_{2g}^6 e_g^1$)
  - $d^9$ configuration ($t_{2g}^6 e_g^3$, e.g., $\text{Cu}^{2+}$)
- **Tetragonal Elongation in $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_22\text{O})6]^{2+}$:** The $d{z^2}$ orbital holds two electrons while $d{x^2-y^2}$ holds one. The greater screening along the $z$-axis causes the two axial $\text{Cu}-\text{O}$ bonds to elongate significantly compared to the four shorter equatorial bonds.

### 6.1 Classification Tree

Isomerism in coordination compounds divides into:

12. **Structural Isomerism:** Distinct connectivity of bonds.
   - Ionisation Isomerism
   - Solvate / Hydrate Isomerism
   - Linkage Isomerism
   - Coordination Isomerism
   - Coordination Position Isomerism
   - Polymerisation Isomerism

13. **Stereoisomerism:** Identical bond connectivity, distinct spatial orientation.
   - Geometrical Isomerism (cis/trans, fac/mer)
   - Optical Isomerism (Chiral enantiomers, d/l pairs)


#### 4. Ionisation Isomerism
- Arises from the mutual exchange of a coordinated ligand with an ionisable counter ion outside the sphere.
- **Diagnostic Test:** $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{Br}]\text{SO}_4$ (red-violet) yields a white precipitate of $\text{BaSO}_4$ upon addition of $\text{BaCl}_2$, but gives no reaction with $\text{AgNO}_3$. Its isomer $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{SO}_4]\text{Br}$ (red) yields a pale yellow precipitate of $\text{AgBr}$ with $\text{AgNO}_3$, but gives no precipitate with $\text{BaCl}_2$.

#### 5. Solvate / Hydrate Isomerism
- Isomers differ in whether water molecules act as coordinated ligands within the inner sphere or as crystal lattice solvent outside.
- **Classical Chromium Hydrates:**
  - $[\text{Cr}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]\text{Cl}_3$: Violet; all $3\text{ Cl}^-$ ionise (precipitates $3\text{ mol AgCl}$); $0\text{ mol H}_2\text{O}$ lost over conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.
  - $[\text{Cr}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_5\text{Cl}]\text{Cl}_2\cdot\text{H}_2\text{O}$: Blue-green; $2\text{ Cl}^-$ ionise (precipitates $2\text{ mol AgCl}$); loses $1\text{ mol H}_2\text{O}$ over conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.
  - $[\text{Cr}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_4\text{Cl}_2]\text{Cl}\cdot2\text{H}_2\text{O}$: Dark green; $1\text{ Cl}^-$ ionises (precipitates $1\text{ mol AgCl}$); loses $2\text{ mol H}_2\text{O}$ over conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.

#### 6. Linkage Isomerism
- Arises in complexes containing ambidentate ligands ($\text{NO}_2^-, \text{SCN}^-, \text{CN}^-$).
- **Example:** $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5(\text{NO}_2)]\text{Cl}_2$ (nitro isomer, yellow, coordinated via N) and $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5(\text{ONO})]\text{Cl}_2$ (nitrito isomer, red, coordinated via O).

#### 7. Coordination Isomerism
- Occurs in compounds where both the cation and the anion are complex entities, through the interchange of ligands between the two metal centers.
- **Example:** $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6][\text{Cr}(\text{CN})_6]$ and $[\text{Cr}(\text{NH}_3)_6][\text{Co}(\text{CN})_6]$.
- **Example:** $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_4][\text{PtCl}_4]$ and $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_3\text{Cl}][\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)\text{Cl}_3]$.

#### 8. Coordination Position Isomerism
- Occurs in polynuclear bridged complexes with unequal distribution of terminal ligands on identical metal centers.

#### 9. Polymerisation Isomerism
- Not true isomerism; compounds possess identical empirical formula but differ in formula weight by integer multiples (e.g., $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_2\text{Cl}_2]$ and $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_4][\text{PtCl}_4]$).


#### 10. Geometrical Isomerism in Square Planar Complexes ($CN = 4$)
- **$[\text{M}a_2b_2]$:** Forms 2 geometrical isomers (cis and trans).
  - Example: $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_2\text{Cl}_2]$: cis-isomer (Cisplatin, anti-cancer drug) has dipole moment $\mu > 0$; trans-isomer (Transplatin, inactive) has symmetric cancellation with $\mu = 0$.

- **$[\text{M}a_2bc]$:** Forms 2 geometrical isomers (cis and trans with respect to $a$).

- **$[\text{M}abcd]$:** Forms 3 geometrical isomers (determined by fixing ligand $a$ and systematically placing $b, c, d$ trans to it). All 3 are achiral.

- **$[\text{M}(\text{AB})_2]$ with unsymmetrical bidentate ligand:** Forms 2 geometrical isomers (cis and trans, e.g., $[\text{Pt}(\text{gly})_2]$).

- **Fundamental Rule:** Tetrahedral complexes NEVER show geometrical isomerism because all vertices are mutually adjacent ($109.5^\circ$).

#### 11. Geometrical Isomerism in Octahedral Complexes ($CN = 6$)
- **$[\text{M}a_4b_2]$:** Forms 2 geometrical isomers:
  - cis-isomer (identical $b$ ligands at adjacent $90^\circ$ positions).
  - trans-isomer (identical $b$ ligands at opposite $180^\circ$ positions).
  - Example: $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]^+$.

- **$[\text{M}a_3b_3]$:** Forms 2 geometrical isomers:
  - **Facial (fac-isomer):** Three identical ligands occupy the corners of a single triangular face of the octahedron.
  - **Meridional (mer-isomer):** Three identical ligands occupy positions spanning an equatorial semicircle (meridian plane) passing through the metal center.
  - Example: $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_3(\text{NO}_2)_3]$.

- **$[\text{M}a_2b_2c_2]$:** Forms 5 geometrical isomers (one of which is chiral, giving a total of 6 stereoisomers).

- **Geometrical Invariance:** $[\text{M}a_6]$ and $[\text{M}a_5b]$ cannot exhibit geometrical isomerism.

#### 12. Optical Isomerism (Chirality in Complexes)
- A complex is optically active if it is non-superimposable on its mirror image (enantiomers: dextrorotatory $d$ and laevorotatory $l$).
- **Criterion:** The coordination entity must strictly lack both a plane of symmetry ($\sigma$) and a center of inversion ($i$).

- **High-Yield Chiral Archetypes:**
  - **$[\text{M}(\text{AA})_3]$ (Tris-chelate complexes):** e.g., $[\text{Co}(\text{en})_3]^{3+}, [\text{Fe}(\text{ox})_3]^{3-}, [\text{Cr}(\text{en})_3]^{3+}$. Highly symmetrical ($D_3$ point group) but chiral; always exists as a pair of non-superimposable enantiomers ($\Delta$ and $\Lambda$).
  - **Cis-$[\text{M}(\text{AA})_2b_2]$:** e.g., cis-$[\text{Co}(\text{en})_2\text{Cl}_2]^+$. Lacks a plane of symmetry $\implies$ Optically active (resolvable into $d$ and $l$ enantiomers).

- **Critical Trap ($\text{trans}-[\text{M}(\text{AA})_2b_2]$):**
  - $\text{trans}-[\text{Co}(\text{en})_2\text{Cl}_2]^+$ possesses a horizontal plane of symmetry and a center of inversion; it is superimposable on its mirror image $\implies$ STRICTLY OPTICALLY INACTIVE (achiral).

### 7.1 Classification of Organometallic Compounds

Organometallics contain at least one direct metal-carbon bond ($\text{M}-\text{C}$):

14. **$\sigma$-Bonded Organometallics:** Metal is bonded to carbon via standard covalent $\sigma$-bonds.
   - Grignard Reagent: $\text{R}-\text{Mg}-\text{X}$
   - Dialkylzinc (Frankland's Reagent): $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{Zn}$
   - Tetraethyllead (anti-knock additive): $(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_4\text{Pb}$

15. **$\pi$-Bonded Organometallics:** Metal coordinates to the delocalized $\pi$-electron system of unsaturated hydrocarbons.
   - **Hapticity ($\eta^n$):** The number of carbon atoms simultaneously bonded to the metal center.
   - **Zeise's Salt:** $\text{K}[\text{PtCl}_3(\eta^2-\text{C}_2\text{H}_4)]\cdot\text{H}_2\text{O}$:
     - First synthesized organometallic compound (1827).
     - The ethylene $\text{C}=\text{C}$ double bond axis coordinates strictly perpendicular to the square planar $\text{PtCl}_3$ plane.
   - **Ferrocene:** $\text{Fe}(\eta^5-\text{C}_5\text{H}_5)_2$ (bis(cyclopentadienyl)iron(II), sandwich structure).
   - **Dibenzenechromium:** $\text{Cr}(\eta^6-\text{C}_6\text{H}_6)_2$.

16. **Combined $\sigma$- and $\pi$-Bonded Organometallics (Metal Carbonyls):**
   - **Monomolecular:** $[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4]$ (tetrahedral), $[\text{Fe}(\text{CO})_5]$ (trigonal bipyramidal), $[\text{Cr}(\text{CO})_6]$ (octahedral).
   - **Polynuclear:** $\text{Mn}_2(\text{CO})_{10}$ (two square pyramidal units linked by a single $\text{Mn}-\text{Mn}$ bond), $\text{Co}_2(\text{CO})_8$ (exists in bridged and non-bridged forms).

### 7.2 Synergic Bonding Mechanism

The bond between transition metal and carbon monoxide possesses unique dual character:

17. **Ligand-to-Metal $\sigma$-Donation:**
   - The lone pair of electrons on the carbonyl carbon donates into an empty hybrid orbital of the transition metal, forming a $\text{M} \leftarrow \text{C}$ $\sigma$-dative bond.

18. **Metal-to-Ligand $\pi$-Backbonding:**
   - Filled non-bonding $d$-orbitals of the transition metal ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{zx}$) overlap with the empty $\pi^*$ antibonding molecular orbitals of $\text{CO}$, back-donating electron density from metal to ligand ($\text{M} \rightarrow \text{C}$ $\pi$-bond).

19. **The Synergic (Cooperative) Effect:**
   - $\sigma$-donation increases negative charge on the metal, facilitating stronger $\pi$-back-donation.
   - $\pi$-backbonding relieves excess electron density from the metal, strengthening the forward $\sigma$-bond.

### 7.3 Physical Consequences on Bond Orders & Vibrational Frequencies

- **$\text{M}-\text{C}$ Bond:** Bond order increases ($1 < \text{B.O.} < 2$); bond length shortens; bond dissociation energy increases.
- **$\text{C}-\text{O}$ Bond:** Population of $\pi^*$ antibonding orbitals decreases $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond order ($2 < \text{B.O.} < 3$); $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond length increases; bond strength decreases.
- **Infrared Carbonyl Stretching Frequency ($\nu_{\text{CO}}$):**
   - Free $\text{CO}$ gas: $\nu_{\text{CO}} \approx 2143\text{ cm}^{-1}$.
   - In neutral metal carbonyls: $\nu_{\text{CO}}$ drops to $\approx 2000\text{--}2060\text{ cm}^{-1}$.
   - **Isoelectronic Carbonyl Series Variation:**
     - Greater negative charge on complex $\implies$ Higher metal electron density $\implies$ Greater $\pi$-back-donation $\implies$ Weaker $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond $\implies$ Lower $\nu_{\text{CO}}$ stretching frequency:
     $$\text{Extent of } \pi\text{-backbonding: } [\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]^- > [\text{Cr}(\text{CO})_6] > [\text{Mn}(\text{CO})_6]^+$$
     $$\text{C}-\text{O Bond Order: } [\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]^- < [\text{Cr}(\text{CO})_6] < [\text{Mn}(\text{CO})_6]^+$$
     $$\text{C}-\text{O Stretching Frequency } (\nu{\text{CO}}): [\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]^- < [\text{Cr}(\text{CO})_6] < [\text{Mn}(\text{CO})_6]^+$$
     $$\text{M}-\text{C Bond Strength: } [\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]^- > [\text{Cr}(\text{CO})_6] > [\text{Mn}(\text{CO})_6]^+,$$

### 7.4 Industrial Catalytic Applications

20. **Wilkinson's Catalyst:**
   - Chloridotris(triphenylphosphine)rhodium(I), $[\text{Rh}(\text{PPh}_3)_3\text{Cl}]$.
   - Homogeneous hydrogenation catalyst for selectively reducing alkenes to alkanes at room temperature and atmospheric pressure.

21. **Ziegler-Natta Catalyst:**
   - Combination of titanium tetrachloride and triethylaluminum: $\text{TiCl}_4 + \text{Al}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_3$.
   - Heterogeneous coordination polymerization catalyst used for the stereospecific synthesis of linear, high-density polyethylene (HDPE) and polypropylene.

### 8.1 Stepwise vs. Overall Formation Constants

Complex formation in aqueous solution proceeds by stepwise equilibria:
$$\text{M} + \text{L} \rightleftharpoons \text{ML} \quad K_1 = \frac{[\text{ML}]}{[\text{M}][\text{L}]}$$
$$\text{ML} + \text{L} \rightleftharpoons \text{ML}_2 \quad K_2 = \frac{[\text{ML}2]}{[\text{ML}][\text{L}]}$$
$$\dots$$
$$\text{ML}{n-1} + \text{L} \rightleftharpoons \text{ML}_n \quad K_n = \frac{[\text{ML}n]}{[\text{ML}{n-1}][\text{L}]}$$

- **Overall Stability Constant ($\beta_n$):**
  $$\text{M} + n\text{L} \rightleftharpoons \text{ML}_n \quad \beta_n = \frac{[\text{ML}n]}{[\text{M}][\text{L}]^n} = K_1 \times K_2 \times \dots \times K_n$$
  $$\log \beta_n = \sum{i=1}^n \log K_i$$

- **Decreasing Stepwise Magnitude:** $K_1 > K_2 > K_3 > \dots > K_n$, caused by statistical factors (decreasing number of available coordination sites) and increasing steric hindrance among coordinating ligands.

- **Instability Constant (Dissociation Constant, $K_d$):** The equilibrium constant for the reverse dissociation of the complex entity:
  $$K_d = \frac{1}{\beta_n}$$

### 8.2 Determinants of Complex Stability

22. **Charge-to-Radius Ratio (Ionic Potential, $\phi = z/r$):** Higher formal oxidation state and smaller ionic radius generate a more intense positive electrostatic field, enhancing complex stability (e.g., $\text{Fe}^{3+} > \text{Fe}^{2+}$).

23. **Ligand Basicity:** Stronger Lewis bases (higher electron-pair donating ability) form thermodynamically more stable complexes.

24. **Chelate & Macrocyclic Effects:** Multidentate chelating ligands form far more stable complexes than monodentate analogues due to positive entropy of solvation.

25. **Irving-Williams Series:** For divalent $3d$ transition metal ions with any given ligand set, thermodynamic stability strictly follows the natural order:
   $$\text{Ba}^{2+} < \text{Sr}^{2+} < \text{Ca}^{2+} < \text{Mg}^{2+} < \text{Mn}^{2+} < \text{Fe}^{2+} < \text{Co}^{2+} < \text{Ni}^{2+} < \text{Cu}^{2+} > \text{Zn}^{2+}$$
   - The anomalous peak at $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ($d^9$) is directly caused by Jahn-Teller tetragonal distortion, which provides extra crystal field stabilization energy.

### 9.1 Qualitative & Quantitative Analytical Chemistry

26. **Confirmatory Test for $\text{Ni}^{2+}$:**
   - Addition of Dimethylglyoxime ($\text{DMG}$) to an ammoniacal solution of $\text{Ni}^{2+}$ yields a brilliant rosy-red precipitate of bis(dimethylglyoximato)nickel(II), $[\text{Ni}(\text{DMG})_2]$.
   - Structure: Square planar, diamagnetic, stabilized by two symmetrical intramolecular $\text{O}-\text{H}\cdots\text{O}$ hydrogen bonds forming four 5-membered rings.

27. **Detection of $\text{Fe}^{3+}$:**
   - Reaction with potassium thiocyanate ($\text{KSCN}$) forms an intense blood-red colored complex $[\text{Fe}(\text{SCN})(\text{H}_2\text{O})_5]^{2+}$.

28. **Detection of $\text{Cu}^{2+}$:**
   - Addition of excess ammonia generates a deep Prussian-blue solution due to the formation of $[\text{Cu}(\text{NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$.

29. **Water Hardness Determination:**
   - Total water hardness ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions) is estimated by complexometric titration with disodium EDTA ($\text{Na}_2\text{H}_2\text{EDTA}$) buffered at $\text{pH} = 10$, using Eriochrome Black-T (EBT) indicator (colour change: wine red to steel blue).

### 9.2 Metallurgy & Metal Extraction

30. **MacArthur-Forrest Cyanide Process for Gold and Silver:**
   - Extraction via complexation with cyanide in presence of oxygen:
     $$4\text{Au} + 8\text{CN}^- + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 4[\text{Au}(\text{CN})_2]^- + 4\text{OH}^-$$
     $$4\text{Ag} + 8\text{CN}^- + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 4[\text{Ag}(\text{CN})_2]^- + 4\text{OH}^-$$
   - Recovery by displacement with electropositive zinc dust:
     $$2[\text{Au}(\text{CN})_2]^- + \text{Zn} \rightarrow [\text{Zn}(\text{CN})_4]^{2-} + 2\text{Au}\downarrow$$

31. **Mond's Process for Nickel Refining:**
   - Impure nickel reacts with $\text{CO}$ at $330\text{--}350\text{ K}$ to produce volatile nickel tetracarbonyl:
     $$\text{Ni} + 4\text{CO} \xrightarrow{330-350\text{ K}} [\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4]\uparrow$$
   - The gaseous complex decomposes at $450\text{--}470\text{ K}$ to yield ultra-pure nickel:
     $$[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4] \xrightarrow{450-470\text{ K}} \text{Ni} + 4\text{CO}\uparrow$$

### 9.3 Biological & Medicinal Chemistry

32. **Biological Metalloproteins & Pigments:**
   - **Chlorophyll:** Coordination complex of Magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$) with a chlorin/porphyrin ring; essential for photosynthetic solar energy harvesting.
   - **Haemoglobin:** Coordination complex of Iron ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$) with a protoporphyrin-IX macrocycle; functions as the reversible oxygen carrier in blood.
   - **Vitamin $\text{B}_{12}$ (Cyanocobalamin):** Coordination complex of Cobalt ($\text{Co}^{3+}$) with a corrin macrocycle; vital for erythropoiesis and myelin synthesis.
   - **Carbonic Anhydrase & Carboxypeptidase-A:** Zinc ($\text{Zn}^{2+}$) metalloenzymes catalyzing biochemical hydration of $\text{CO}_2$ and peptide hydrolysis.

33. **Chemotherapeutic & Chelating Medicinal Agents:**
   - **Cisplatin ($\text{cis}-[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_2\text{Cl}_2]$):** Anti-cancer drug that binds to adjacent guanine bases in DNA, causing intrastrand cross-links that block cancer cell replication. Transplatin is therapeutically inactive.
   - **$\text{Ca}-\text{Na}_2\text{EDTA}$:** Chelating antidote used to treat clinical lead ($\text{Pb}^{2+}$) poisoning by forming a soluble, non-toxic lead chelate excreted in urine.
   - **D-Penicillamine:** Chelating agent for removing excess copper in Wilson's disease.
   - **Desferrioxamine-B:** Siderophore chelating agent for treating iron overload in thalassemia patients.

### 9.4 Photography & Electroplating

34. **Photographic Fixing:**
   - Unexposed silver halide emulsion on film is dissolved away by aqueous sodium thiosulfate (hypo), preventing blackening upon exposure to light:
     $$\text{AgBr} + 2\text{Na}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3 \rightarrow \text{Na}_3[\text{Ag}(\text{S}_2\text{O}_3)_2] + \text{NaBr}$$

35. **Electroplating:**
   - Smooth, uniform deposition of gold and silver is achieved using cyanide complexes ($[\text{Ag}(\text{CN})_2]^-$ and $[\text{Au}(\text{CN})_2]^-$) because controlled dissociation delivers a low, steady concentration of free metal cations.

## 13. High-Yield JEE Exam Traps & Problem Archetypes

- **Trap 1: Coordination Number vs. Oxidation State Discrepancy**
  - Coordination number depends strictly on donor atoms bonded, not charge. In $[\text{Fe}(\text{ox})_3]^{3-}$, $\text{C.N.} = 3 \times 2 = 6$, while $\text{O.S.} = +3$.

- **Trap 2: Secondary Valency vs. Precipitation Behavior**
  - Adding excess $\text{AgNO}_3$ precipitates ONLY outer-sphere counter ions. In $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{SO}_4]\text{Cl}$, $1\text{ mol AgCl}$ precipitates, but $0\text{ mol BaSO}_4$ forms upon adding $\text{BaCl}_2$.

- **Trap 3: Nickel Coordination Number 4 Geometry Invariance**
  - $[\text{Ni}(\text{CO})_4]$: $Ni(0) (3d^{10}) \implies sp^3$ tetrahedral, diamagnetic ($n = 0$).
  - $[\text{Ni}(\text{CN})_4]^{2-}$: $Ni(II) (3d^8) \implies dsp^2$ square planar, diamagnetic ($n = 0$).
  - $[\text{NiCl}_4]^{2-}$: $Ni(II) (3d^8) \implies sp^3$ tetrahedral, paramagnetic ($n = 2$).

- **Trap 4: Radical Nature of Vanadium Carbonyl**
  - $[\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]$ has $23 + 12 = 35$ electrons. It does NOT dimerize due to steric crowding of six $\text{CO}$ ligands around small vanadium; instead, it is a paramagnetic dark blue solid that easily reduces to $[\text{V}(\text{CO})_6]^-$.

- **Trap 5: The $\text{KMnO}_4$ and $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ Color Trap**
  - Both $\text{Mn}^{7+}$ in $\text{KMnO}_4$ and $\text{Cr}^{6+}$ in $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ are $d^0$ systems with zero $d$-electrons. Their intense coloration CANNOT be $d-d$ transitions; it is caused strictly by Ligand-to-Metal Charge Transfer (LMCT).

- **Trap 6: Optical Activity of $\text{trans}-[\text{Co}(\text{en})_2\text{Cl}_2]^+$**
  - Examiners frequently ask for the number of optical isomers of $[\text{Co}(\text{en})_2\text{Cl}_2]^+$. Total stereoisomers $= 3$ (trans-isomer + cis $d$-isomer + cis $l$-isomer). The trans-isomer has a horizontal plane of symmetry ($\sigma_h$) and is optically INACTIVE. Only the cis-isomer is chiral and optically active.

- **Trap 7: Geometrical Isomerism in Tetrahedral Systems**
  - Tetrahedral complexes $[\text{M}a_2b_2]$ and $[\text{M}abcd]$ NEVER show geometrical isomerism. However, $[\text{M}abcd]$ tetrahedral complexes lacking symmetry DO show optical isomerism.

- **Trap 8: Synergic Backbonding & Infrared Stretching Frequency**
  - As negative charge on a carbonyl complex increases, $\pi$-backbonding increases, $\text{M}-\text{C}$ bond shortens and strengthens, but $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond lengthens and weakens, causing $\nu_{\text{CO}}$ to DECREASE.
