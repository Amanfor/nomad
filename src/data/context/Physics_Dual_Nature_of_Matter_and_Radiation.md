1. Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter Waves
1.1 De-Broglie Hypothesis
- Since radiation exhibits dual nature (both wave and particle characteristics) and nature exhibits fundamental symmetry, material particles in motion must also possess wave-like properties.
- A moving material particle is accompanied by a pilot matter wave (de-Broglie wave) controlling its propagation.
- De-Broglie Wavelength Formula: $$\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv}$$ where:
   - $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$ (Planck's constant)
   - $p = mv$ is the linear momentum of the particle.
1.2 Matter Wave Expressions under Physical Conditions
2. In terms of Kinetic Energy ($E_k$ or $K$): Since $E_k = \frac{p^2}{2m} \implies p = \sqrt{2m E_k}$: $$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2m E_k}}$$
3. For a Charged Particle Accelerated through Potential Difference $V$: Since $E_k = qV$: $$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mqV}}$$
4. Specific Charged Particles:
   - Electron ($m_e = 9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}, q = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$): $$\lambda_e = \frac{12.27}{\sqrt{V}} , \text{Å} = \sqrt{\frac{150}{V}} , \text{Å} = \frac{1.227}{\sqrt{V}} , \text{nm}$$
   - Proton ($m_p \approx 1836 , m_e, q = e$): $$\lambda_p = \frac{0.286}{\sqrt{V}} , \text{Å}$$
   - Deuteron ($m_d \approx 2 , m_p, q = e$): $$\lambda_d = \frac{0.202}{\sqrt{V}} , \text{Å}$$
   - Alpha Particle ($m_\alpha \approx 4 , m_p, q = 2e$): $$\lambda_\alpha = \frac{0.101}{\sqrt{V}} , \text{Å}$$
5. Thermal Neutrons / Gas Molecules at Absolute Temperature $T$: Equipartition theorem gives average kinetic energy $E_k = \frac{3}{2} k_B T$: $$\lambda_{\text{thermal}} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3m k_B T}}$$

6. Cathode Rays and Specific Charge Determination
2.1 Properties of Cathode Rays
- Cathode rays are streams of fast-moving electrons emitted from the cathode in a low-pressure gas discharge tube ($\approx 0.01 \text{ mm of Hg}$).
- Travel in straight lines perpendicular to the cathode surface.
- Possess kinetic energy and exert mechanical pressure (rotate light paddle wheels).
- Deflected by both electric and magnetic fields (indicating negative electric charge).
- Produce X-rays when suddenly stopped by a target metal of high atomic weight and high melting point (e.g., tungsten).
2.2 Thomson’s Experiment for Specific Charge ($e/m$)
Using mutually perpendicular electric ($\vec{E}$) and magnetic ($\vec{B}$) cross-fields balanced against electrostatic deflection: $$\frac{e}{m} = \frac{E^2}{2 V B^2} = 1.7589 \times 10^{11} \text{ C}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$$

- Millikan’s oil drop experiment independently measured the elementary quantum charge: $$e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C} \implies m_e = \frac{e}{e/m} = 9.109 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}$$

7. Work Function and Electron Emission Mechanisms
3.1 Work Function ($W_0$ or $\phi$)
- Definition: The minimum amount of energy required to eject an electron from the surface of a metal against surface potential barriers.
- Expressed in electron-volts ($1 \text{ eV} = 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}$).
- Depends on the nature of the metal and surface impurities.
- Cesium ($\text{Cs}$) has the lowest work function ($\approx 2.14 \text{ eV}$), while Platinum ($\text{Pt}$) has one of the highest ($\approx 5.65 \text{ eV}$).
3.2 Types of Electron Emission
8. Thermionic Emission: Free electrons absorb thermal energy upon heating to overcome the surface barrier.
9. Field Emission (Cold Cathode): Strong electric field ($\sim 10^8 \text{ V/m}$) applied to the metal surface pulls electrons out via quantum tunneling.
10. Photoelectric Emission: Incident electromagnetic radiation of suitable frequency supplies quantum energy to release electrons.
11. Secondary Emission: High-velocity primary electrons striking a metal transfer momentum and knock out secondary electrons.

12. Quantum Photon Theory of Radiation
13. Energy Quanta: Radiation propagates in discrete packets of energy called photons: $$E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \approx \frac{12400}{\lambda , (\text{Å})} , \text{eV} = \frac{1240}{\lambda , (\text{nm})} , \text{eV}$$
14. Velocity: All photons travel through vacuum at the universal speed of light $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$, independent of reference frame.
15. Invariant Frequency: Frequency $\nu$ depends strictly on the source and does not change across different optical media; wavelength changes ($\lambda' = \lambda / \mu$).
16. Rest Mass and Relativistic Momentum:
   - Photon rest mass $m_0 = 0$.
   - Relativistic dynamic mass: $m = \frac{E}{c^2} = \frac{h\nu}{c^2}$.
   - Momentum: $$p = \frac{E}{c} = \frac{h\nu}{c} = \frac{h}{\lambda}$$
17. Radiation Force & Pressure ($P_{\text{rad}}$):
   - For total absorption on area $A$: Force $F = \frac{IA}{c}$, Pressure $P = \frac{I}{c}$.
   - For complete reflection ($r = 1$): Force $F = \frac{2IA}{c}$, Pressure $P = \frac{2I}{c}$.
   - For surface with reflectivity $r$: $P = \frac{I}{c}(1 + r)$.

18. Photoelectric Effect Laws and Characteristics
5.1 Laws of Photoelectric Emission
19. Rate of Emission: The number of photoelectrons emitted per second (saturation photoelectric current) is directly proportional to the intensity of incident light ($I \propto \text{lux}$).
20. Threshold Frequency ($\nu_0$): For every photosensitive material, there exists a definite minimum frequency $\nu_0$ below which no photoelectrons can be ejected, regardless of light intensity or illumination duration.
21. Max Kinetic Energy ($K_{\max}$): Above $\nu_0$, the maximum kinetic energy of emitted photoelectrons is strictly independent of light intensity and depends linearly on the frequency $\nu$ of incident radiation.
22. Instantaneous Emission: Photoelectric emission is an instantaneous quantum process; the time lag between photon arrival and electron ejection is less than $10^{-9} \text{ s}$.

23. Einstein’s Photoelectric Equation and Stopping Potential
6.1 Einstein’s Energy Conservation Equation
A single photon transfers its entire energy $h\nu$ to a single electron in an all-or-nothing interaction: $$h\nu = W_0 + K_{\max}$$ $$K_{\max} = \frac{1}{2} m v_{\max}^2 = h\nu - W_0 = h(\nu - \nu_0) = hc\left(\frac{1}{\lambda} - \frac{1}{\lambda_0}\right)$$ where:

- $\nu_0 = \frac{W_0}{h}$ is the threshold frequency.
- $\lambda_0 = \frac{hc}{W_0}$ is the threshold (cut-off) wavelength.
6.2 Stopping Potential ($V_0$)
The retarding potential required to reduce the photoelectric current to zero (stopping the fastest photoelectrons): $$e V_0 = K_{\max} = h\nu - W_0$$ $$V_0 = \left(\frac{h}{e}\right)\nu - \frac{W_0}{e} = \left(\frac{hc}{e}\right)\frac{1}{\lambda} - \frac{W_0}{e}$$

6.3 Diagnostic Graphical Signatures (High-Yield JEE Traps)
- Slope of $V_0$ vs $\nu$ graph: Universal constant $\frac{h}{e} \approx 4.14 \times 10^{-15} \text{ V}\cdot\text{s}$ (identical for all metals).
- Slope of $K_{\max}$ vs $\nu$ graph: Universal constant $h \approx 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$.
- Horizontal Intercept: Threshold frequency $\nu_0$.
- Vertical Intercept: Negative potential $-\frac{W_0}{e}$ (or energy $-W_0$).
- Photoelectric Current vs Intensity: Linear line passing through origin ($I_{\text{photo}} \propto \text{Intensity}$).
- Stopping Potential vs Intensity: Horizontal flat line (stopping potential is strictly independent of intensity).

24. Photoelectric Devices & Practical Classifications
25. Photo-emissive Cell: Uses an evacuated glass envelope with a curved cathode and central anode. Incident light liberates electrons across the vacuum gap; used in light meters and optical soundtrack decoders.
26. Photo-voltaic Cell: Generates an electromotive force (EMF) directly upon illumination without external battery bias across a semiconductor $p$-$n$ junction (solar cells).
27. Photo-conductive Cell: Utilizes intrinsic or extrinsic semiconductors whose electrical conductivity increases sharply upon photon absorption (e.g., $\text{CdS}$ light-dependent resistors / LDRs).
