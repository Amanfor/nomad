1. Structural Identification & Qualitative Functional Group Analysis
![Poc Qualitative Functional Group Tests Master Flowsheet](/media/poc_qualitative_functional_group_tests_master_flowsheet.webp) Description: Two-panel reference diagram for qualitative organic analysis and structural cleavage: (Panel A) Comprehensive functional group distinction matrix detailing diagnostic reagents, colorimetric observations, and selectivity criteria across alcohols, phenols, carboxylic acids, aldehydes, ketones, and amines; (Panel B) Structural identification roadmap showing degree of unsaturation calculation, catalytic hydrogenation pi-bond counting, and ozonolysis decision architecture comparing reductive and oxidative cleavage pathways.
1.1 Structural Elucidation Toolbox
1. Degree of Unsaturation (Double Bond Equivalent, DBE / DU)
For an organic molecule with general molecular formula $\text{C}_c\text{H}_h\text{N}_n\text{O}_o\text{X}_x$: $$\mathbf{\text{DU} = c + 1 - \frac{h - n + x}{2}}$$ (Oxygen and Sulphur are bivalent and do not affect the formula).


* Physical Interpretation:
   * $\text{DU} = 1 \implies$ Exactly $1$ double bond ($\text{C}=\text{C}$ or $\text{C}=\text{O}$) OR $1$ monocyclic ring.
   * $\text{DU} = 2 \implies$ Exactly $1$ triple bond ($\text{C}\equiv\text{C}$ or $\text{C}\equiv\text{N}$) OR $2$ double bonds OR $1$ ring $+ 1$ double bond OR bicyclic system.
   * $\text{DU} = 4 \implies$ Canonical indicator of a benzene ring ($1$ aromatic ring $+ 3\ \pi\text{ bonds}$).
2. Catalytic Hydrogenation ($\text{H}_2 / \text{Ni, Pt, Pd}$)
* Converts all non-aromatic carbon-carbon multiple bonds ($\text{C}=\text{C}, \text{C}\equiv\text{C}$) into single bonds at room temperature and pressure.
* Invariant: The carbon skeletal framework is strictly conserved.
* $\text{Moles of } \text{H}_2 \text{ consumed per mole of substrate} = \text{Total number of aliphatic } \pi \text{ bonds}$.
* Benzene rings are inert to hydrogenation at ambient temperature (requiring high temperature $150 - 200^\circ\text{C}$ and pressure).
3. Photochemical Monochlorination ($\text{Cl}_2 / h\nu$)
* Replaces an aliphatic $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bond with a $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bond via a free-radical mechanism.
* Rule: The total number of monochloro structural isomers equals the number of chemically non-equivalent (non-identical) hydrogen atoms present in the substrate.
* Aromatic ring $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bonds do not undergo free-radical chlorination.
4. Ozonolysis Cleavage Analytics
Ozonolysis cleaves multiple bonds to locate the exact position of unsaturation:


* Reductive Ozonolysis ($\text{O}_3$ followed by $\text{Zn}/\text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{S}$):
   * Preserves aldehydes from oxidation: $$\text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{R}' \xrightarrow{\text{O}_3, \text{Zn}/\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{R}-\text{CHO} + \text{R}'-\text{CHO}$$ $$\text{R}_2\text{C}=\text{CH}-\text{R}' \xrightarrow{\text{O}_3, \text{Zn}/\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{R}_2\text{C}=\text{O} + \text{R}'-\text{CHO}$$ $$\text{Terminal }=\text{CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{O}_3, \text{Zn}/\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{HCHO} \quad (\text{Formaldehyde})$$
   * Internal alkynes yield $\alpha$-diketones: $$\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{R}' \xrightarrow{\text{O}_3, \text{Zn}/\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{R}-\text{CO}-\text{CO}-\text{R}'$$
* Oxidative Ozonolysis ($\text{O}_3$ followed by $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ or $\text{H}_2\text{O}$):
   * Aldehydic $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bonds are oxidized to carboxylic acids: $$\text{R}-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{R}' \xrightarrow{\text{O}_3, \text{H}_2\text{O}_2} \text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{R}'-\text{COOH}$$
   * Ketones remain unaffected: $\text{R}_2\text{C}=\text{O}$.
   * Terminal $=\text{CH}_2$ oxidizes to carbonic acid, rapidly decomposing to $\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$.
   * Alkynes undergo complete cleavage to carboxylic acids: $$\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{R}' \xrightarrow{\text{O}_3, \text{H}_2\text{O}_2} 2\text{R}-\text{COOH}$$

1.2 Tests for Unsaturation & Active Acidic Hydrogen
1. Unsaturation Detection
* Bromine Water Test ($\text{Br}_2 / \text{CCl}_4$ or $\text{Br}_2 / \text{H}_2\text{O}$):
   * Reddish-brown colour of bromine is rapidly discharged without evolution of $\text{HBr}$ gas.
   * Aromatic rings do not discharge bromine water under ambient conditions.
* Baeyer's Reagent Test:
   * Cold, dilute, neutral or alkaline $1\%\ \text{KMnO}_4$ solution.
   * Discharges purple permanganate color with precipitation of brown manganese dioxide ($\text{MnO}_2\downarrow$), forming syn-vicinal diols.
2. Terminal Alkyne Differentiation ($\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{H}$)
Terminal alkynes possess weakly acidic sp-hybridized hydrogen:


* Ammoniacal Cuprous Chloride ($\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{OH}$): $$\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + [\text{Cu}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+ \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CCu}\downarrow} \quad (\textbf{Red Precipitate}) + \text{NH}_4^+ + \text{NH}_3$$
* Ammoniacal Silver Nitrate (Tollens' Reagent): $$\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + [\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+ \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CAg}\downarrow} \quad (\textbf{White Precipitate}) + \text{NH}_4^+ + \text{NH}_3$$
* Sodium Metal Test: $$2\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} + 2\text{Na} \rightarrow 2\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{CNa} + \mathbf{\text{H}_2\uparrow}$$ Internal alkynes ($\text{R}-\text{C}\equiv\text{C}-\text{R}'$) give negative results in all three tests.

1.3 Systematic Functional Group Diagnostic Tests
1. Alcohols ($-\text{OH}$)
* Lucas Reagent Test ($\text{conc. HCl} + \text{anhydrous ZnCl}_2$): Forms insoluble alkyl chloride via an $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ carbocation mechanism:
   * $3^\circ$ Alcohols: Immediate cloudiness / oily turbidity at room temperature.
   * $2^\circ$ Alcohols: Turbidity appears within $5$ minutes.
   * $1^\circ$ Alcohols: No turbidity at room temperature; solution remains clear for $> 30\ \text{min}$ (turbidity only upon prolonged heating).
   * Exceptions: Allyl alcohol ($\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$) and benzyl alcohol ($\text{PhCH}_2\text{OH}$) form stable carbocations and produce immediate turbidity like $3^\circ$ alcohols. Phenols do not react.
* Victor Meyer Test (Red-Blue-White / Colorless Rule): Alcohol $\xrightarrow{\text{P} + \text{I}_2} \text{RI} \xrightarrow{\text{AgNO}_2} \text{RNO}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_2} \text{Intermediate} \xrightarrow{\text{NaOH}}$ Color:
   * $1^\circ$ Alcohol: Forms Nitrolic acid $\rightarrow$ Blood Red Color in alkali.
   * $2^\circ$ Alcohol: Forms Pseudonitrol $\rightarrow$ Deep Blue Color in alkali.
   * $3^\circ$ Alcohol: No alpha-hydrogen; unreactive toward $\text{HNO}_2 \rightarrow$ Colorless / White.
* Ceric Ammonium Nitrate (CAN) Test: Alcohols react with yellow CAN reagent $[(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{Ce}(\text{NO}_3)_6]$ to form a red or pink coordination complex: $$[(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{Ce}(\text{NO}_3)_6] + \text{ROH} \rightarrow [(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{Ce}(\text{OR})(\text{NO}_3)_5] + \text{HNO}_3$$
2. Phenols ($\text{Ar}-\text{OH}$)
* Neutral Ferric Chloride ($\text{FeCl}_3$) Test: Phenols react with neutral $\text{FeCl}_3$ to form intensely colored coordination complexes: $$6\text{PhOH} + \text{FeCl}_3 \rightarrow \mathbf{[\text{Fe}(\text{OPh})_6]^{3-}} \quad (\textbf{Violet / Purple / Green}) + 3\text{H}^+ + 3\text{HCl}$$ Enols also give a positive test; aliphatic alcohols give a negative test.
* Phthalein Dye Test: Phenol heated with phthalic anhydride and concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, then poured into dilute $\text{NaOH}$, produces phenolphthalein giving a brilliant pink coloration.
* Liebermann's Nitroso Reaction: Phenol treated with $\text{NaNO}_2$ and concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ gives a deep green/blue coloration. On dilution with water, it turns red; addition of excess alkali restores the deep blue color of the indophenol anion.
* Bromine Water Reaction: Phenol rapidly decolorizes aqueous bromine, forming an insoluble white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
3. Carbonyl Compounds (Aldehydes & Ketones)
* Brady's Reagent (2,4-DNP: 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine): Both aldehydes and ketones condense to form yellow, orange, or red crystalline hydrazones: $$>\text{C}=\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{NH}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_3(\text{NO}_2)_2 \rightarrow >\text{C}=\text{N}-\text{NH}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_3(\text{NO}_2)_2\downarrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
* Tollens' Reagent (Ammoniacal Silver Nitrate, $[\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+$): Oxidizes all aldehydes to carboxylate ions with deposition of a brilliant silver mirror or black precipitate of elemental silver: $$\text{R}-\text{CHO} + 2[\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+ + 3\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{R}-\text{COO}^- + \mathbf{2\text{Ag}\downarrow} + 4\text{NH}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$ Positive Respondents: Aliphatic aldehydes, aromatic aldehydes (benzaldehyde), formic acid ($\text{HCOOH}$), $\alpha$-hydroxy ketones (fructose), and terminal alkynes (white ppt). Ordinary ketones give a negative test.
* Fehling's & Benedict's Tests:
   * Fehling's Reagent: Solution A ($\text{CuSO}_4$) + Solution B (Alkaline sodium potassium tartrate / Rochelle salt).
   * Benedict's Reagent: Solution A ($\text{CuSO}_4$) + Solution B ($\text{Sodium citrate} + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$).
   * Reaction: Aliphatic aldehydes reduce blue $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ complex to a red precipitate of cuprous oxide ($\text{Cu}_2\text{O}\downarrow$): $$\text{R}-\text{CHO} + 2\text{Cu}^{2+} + 5\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{R}-\text{COO}^- + \mathbf{\text{Cu}_2\text{O}\downarrow} \quad (\textbf{Red}) + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
   * Critical Distinction: Aromatic aldehydes (e.g., benzaldehyde) DO NOT reduce Fehling's or Benedict's solutions! This serves as a vital diagnostic test to differentiate aliphatic from aromatic aldehydes.
* Schiff's Reagent: Rosaniline hydrochloride (magenta dye) decolorized by passing sulphur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$). Addition of aldehydes restores the characteristic pink/magenta coloration.
* Iodoform (Haloform) Test ($\text{I}_2 + \text{NaOH}$ or $\text{NaOI}$): Detects the presence of:
   1. Methyl carbonyl group: $\text{CH}_3-\text{C}(=\text{O})-$ (Acetaldehyde and all methyl ketones).
   2. Methyl carbinol group: $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{OH})-$ (Ethanol and all secondary methyl alcohols). $$\text{CH}_3-\text{CO}-\text{R} + 3\text{I}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{CHI}_3\downarrow} \quad (\textbf{Yellow crystalline precipitate}, \text{m.p. } 119^\circ\text{C}) + \text{RCOONa} + 3\text{NaI} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$$ Negative Respondents: Carboxylic acids, esters, amides, and $3^\circ$ alcohols do not give iodoform.
4. Carboxylic Acids ($-\text{COOH}$)
* Sodium Bicarbonate ($\text{NaHCO}_3$) Test: Carboxylic acids displace weaker carbonic acid from bicarbonate, producing brisk effervescence of carbon dioxide: $$\text{R}-\text{COOH} + \text{NaHCO}_3 \rightarrow \text{R}-\text{COONa} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \mathbf{\text{CO}_2\uparrow}$$
   * Thermodynamic $\text{p}K_a$ Criterion: A compound gives a positive $\text{NaHCO}_3$ test if and only if it is a stronger acid than $\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$ ($\text{p}K_a < 6.4$).
   * Positive: Carboxylic acids ($\text{p}K_a \approx 4 - 5$), sulphonic acids ($\text{ArSO}_3\text{H}$), picric acid ($2,4,6\text{-trinitrophenol}$, $\text{p}K_a \approx 0.38$), $2,4\text{-dinitrophenol}$, and squaric acid.
   * Negative: Ordinary phenol ($\text{p}K_a \approx 10$), cresols, nitrophenols with only one $-\text{NO}_2$ group, and alcohols.
5. Amines ($-\text{NH}_2, -\text{NHR}, -\text{NR}_2$)
* Carbylamine Test (Isocyanide Test for $1^\circ$ Amines): All primary amines (aliphatic or aromatic) heated with chloroform and alcoholic potassium hydroxide form an isocyanide (carbylamine) with an intensely nauseating, foul odor: $$\text{R}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{CHCl}_3 + 3\text{KOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathbf{\text{R}-\text{NC}} \quad (\textbf{Foul Smelling}) + 3\text{KCl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$$ Secondary and tertiary amines do not yield isocyanides.
* Hinsberg's Test (Benzenesulphonyl Chloride, $\text{PhSO}_2\text{Cl}$): Used to distinguish and separate mixtures of $1^\circ, 2^\circ, 3^\circ$ amines:
   * $1^\circ$ Amine: Forms $N$-alkylbenzenesulphonamide ($\text{PhSO}_2\text{NHR}$). The remaining hydrogen on nitrogen is strongly acidic due to the electron-withdrawing sulphonyl group, rendering it soluble in aqueous alkali ($\text{KOH}$). Acidification reprecipitates it.
   * $2^\circ$ Amine: Forms $N,N$-dialkylbenzenesulphonamide ($\text{PhSO}_2\text{NR}_2$). Lacks an acidic hydrogen; remains insoluble in aqueous alkali.
   * $3^\circ$ Amine: Has no replaceable hydrogen; does not react with benzenesulphonyl chloride. Remains insoluble in alkali but dissolves in dilute mineral acid.
* Nitrous Acid ($\text{HNO}_2$) & Azo Dye Test:
   * $1^\circ$ Aliphatic Amine: Forms highly unstable diazonium salt, decomposing immediately with evolution of $\text{N}_2$ gas and formation of alcohols: $$\text{R}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{HNO}_2 \rightarrow [\text{R}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-] \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{ROH} + \mathbf{\text{N}_2\uparrow} + \text{HCl}$$
   * $1^\circ$ Aromatic Amine (Aniline): Stable diazonium salt formed at ice-cold temperature ($0 - 5^\circ\text{C}$): $$\text{Ph}-\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{0 - 5^\circ\text{C}} \text{Ph}-\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$ Coupling with alkaline $\beta$-naphthol yields a brilliant scarlet/orange-red azo dye.
   * $2^\circ$ Amine (Aliphatic or Aromatic): Forms a yellow oily liquid of $N$-nitrosamine: $$\text{R}_2\text{NH} + \text{HNO}_2 \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{R}_2\text{N}-\text{NO}} \quad (\textbf{Yellow oily nitrosamine}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
   * $3^\circ$ Aliphatic Amine: Forms a water-soluble trialkylammonium nitrite salt ($\text{R}_3\text{NH}^+\text{NO}_2^-$).
6. Nitro Compounds ($-\text{NO}_2$)
* Mulliken-Barker Test: The nitro group is reduced with neutral reducing agent ($\text{Zn} + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$) to hydroxylamine ($\text{R}-\text{NHOH}$). Hydroxylamine readily reduces Tollens' reagent, producing a black precipitate or silver mirror: $$\text{R}-\text{NO}_2 + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{Zn}/\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}} \text{R}-\text{NHOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$ $$\text{R}-\text{NHOH} + 2[\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+ + 2\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{R}-\text{NO} + \mathbf{2\text{Ag}\downarrow} + 4\text{NH}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

2. Elemental Analysis: Lassaigne's Test & Quantitative Estimation
![Poc Elemental Analysis Lassaigne And Quantitative Formulas](/media/poc_elemental_analysis_lassaigne_and_quantitative_formulas.webp) Description: Two-panel reference diagram for elemental analysis and quantitative stoichiometry: (Panel A) Lassaigne's test flowsheet and chromatic precipitate structures for nitrogen, sulphur, halogens, and phosphorus; (Panel B) Master quantitative estimation formulas for Dumas, Kjeldahl, Liebig, and Carius methods, highlighting structural exclusions where Kjeldahl's method fails.
2.1 Qualitative Detection: Lassaigne's Test (Sodium Fusion Extract, SFE)
Because non-metallic elements ($\text{N, S, Halogens, P}$) are covalently bound to carbon, they do not ionize in solution. Fusion with metallic sodium breaks covalent bonds and converts elements into water-soluble ionic sodium salts: $$\text{Na} + \text{C} + \text{N} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaCN}$$ $$2\text{Na} + \text{S} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Na}_2\text{S}$$ $$\text{Na} + \text{X} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaX} \quad (\text{X} = \text{Cl, Br, I})$$ $$\text{Na} + \text{C} + \text{N} + \text{S} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaSCN} \quad (\text{if limited Na is used})$$
1. Test for Nitrogen
1. To the sodium fusion extract, add freshly prepared ferrous sulphate ($\text{FeSO}_4$) solution and heat in alkaline medium.
2. Acidify with concentrated sulphuric acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) and add a few drops of $\text{FeCl}_3$.
3. Formation of a Prussian blue coloration or precipitate of ferric ferrocyanide confirms Nitrogen: $$\text{FeSO}_4 + 2\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{Fe(OH)}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$$ $$\text{Fe(OH)}_2 + 6\text{NaCN} \rightarrow \text{Na}_4[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6] + 2\text{NaOH}$$ $$3\text{Na}_4[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6] + 4\text{FeCl}_3 \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{Fe}_4[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]_3} \quad (\textbf{Prussian Blue}) + 12\text{NaCl}$$
2. Test for Nitrogen and Sulphur Together
If both $\text{N}$ and $\text{S}$ are present in the compound and sodium is not in excess, sodium thiocyanate ($\text{NaSCN}$) forms. Addition of $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ yields an intense blood-red coloration: $$\text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{SCN}^- \rightarrow \mathbf{[\text{Fe}(\text{SCN})]^{2+}} \quad (\textbf{Blood Red Complex})$$ If excess sodium is used in fusion, thiocyanate decomposes into separate cyanide and sulphide: $$\text{NaSCN} + 2\text{Na} \rightarrow \text{NaCN} + \text{Na}_2\text{S}$$
3. Test for Sulphur
* Sodium Nitroprusside Test: Addition of sodium nitroprusside $\text{Na}_2[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_5\text{NO}]$ to SFE yields an intense violet / purple coloration: $$\text{Na}_2\text{S} + \text{Na}_2[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_5\text{NO}] \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{Na}_4[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_5\text{NOS}]} \quad (\textbf{Purple Complex})$$
* Lead Acetate Test: SFE acidified with acetic acid produces a black precipitate of lead sulphide: $$\text{Na}_2\text{S} + (\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_2\text{Pb} \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{PbS}\downarrow} \quad (\textbf{Black}) + 2\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$$
4. Test for Halogens
* Precautionary Boiling with Nitric Acid: Before adding $\text{AgNO}_3$, the extract must be boiled with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ to destroy cyanide and sulphide ions: $$\text{NaCN} + \text{HNO}_3 \rightarrow \text{NaNO}_3 + \text{HCN}\uparrow$$ $$\text{Na}_2\text{S} + 2\text{HNO}_3 \rightarrow 2\text{NaNO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{S}\uparrow$$ (If omitted, $\text{AgCN}$ [white] and $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$ [black] would precipitate and falsify the halogen test).
* Silver Nitrate Reactions:
   * Chloride ($\text{Cl}^-$): Curdy white precipitate ($\text{AgCl}\downarrow$), readily soluble in aqueous $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$.
   * Bromide ($\text{Br}^-$): Pale yellow precipitate ($\text{AgBr}\downarrow$), sparingly soluble in $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$.
   * Iodide ($\text{I}^-$): Canary yellow precipitate ($\text{AgI}\downarrow$), completely insoluble in $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$.
* Layer Test for Bromine & Iodine: SFE acidified with dilute $\text{HCl}$ and shaken with $\text{CCl}_4$ or $\text{CS}_2$ in the presence of chlorine water:
   * $\text{Br}_2$ imparts an orange-brown color to the organic layer.
   * $\text{I}_2$ imparts a violet color to the organic layer.
5. Test for Phosphorus
Fuse the organic compound with sodium peroxide ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$) to oxidize phosphorus to sodium phosphate ($\text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4$). Boil the extract with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ and add ammonium molybdate $[(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4]$. Formation of a canary yellow precipitate confirms phosphorus: $$\text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4 + 3\text{HNO}_3 \rightarrow \text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 3\text{NaNO}_3$$ $$\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 12(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4 + 21\text{HNO}_3 \rightarrow \mathbf{(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{PO}_4 \cdot 12\text{MoO}_3\downarrow} \quad (\textbf{Canary Yellow}) + 21\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

2.2 Quantitative Elemental Estimation Formulas
Let $w$ denote the mass of the organic compound taken in grams.
1. Estimation of Carbon & Hydrogen (Liebig's Combustion Method)
$$\mathbf{\% \text{C} = \frac{12}{44} \times \frac{\text{Mass of } \text{CO}_2 \text{ formed}}{w} \times 100\%}$$ $$\mathbf{\% \text{H} = \frac{2}{18} \times \frac{\text{Mass of } \text{H}_2\text{O} \text{ formed}}{w} \times 100\%}$$
2. Estimation of Nitrogen: Dumas Method
The nitrogenous compound is oxidized by dry cupric oxide ($\text{CuO}$) in a $\text{CO}2$ atmosphere. Nitrogen is converted to $\text{N}2$ gas and collected over aqueous $\text{KOH}$ in a nitrometer: $$P_1 = P{\text{observed}} - \text{Aqueous Tension at } T_1$$ $$V{\text{STP}} = \frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} \times \frac{273}{760} \quad (\text{in mL})$$ $$\mathbf{\% \text{N} = \frac{28}{22400} \times \frac{V_{\text{STP}}}{w} \times 100\% = \frac{V_{\text{STP}}}{8 \times w}\%}$$
3. Estimation of Nitrogen: Kjeldahl's Method
The compound is digested with concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ in the presence of $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$ (boiling point elevator) and $\text{CuSO}_4$ (catalyst), converting organic nitrogen quantitatively into ammonium sulphate: $$(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{NH}_3\uparrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$ The liberated ammonia is absorbed in a known excess volume of standard acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or $\text{HCl}$), and unreacted acid is back-titrated with standard $\text{NaOH}$: $$\mathbf{\% \text{N} = \frac{1.4 \times N \times V}{w}}$$ Where $N$ is the normality of standard acid, and $V$ is the volume (in mL) of acid consumed by ammonia.
4. Estimation of Halogens: Carius Method
The compound is heated with fuming $\text{HNO}_3$ and solid $\text{AgNO}_3$ in a sealed Carius tube: $$\mathbf{\% \text{Cl} = \frac{35.5}{143.5} \times \frac{\text{Mass of } \text{AgCl}}{w} \times 100\%}$$ $$\mathbf{\% \text{Br} = \frac{80}{188} \times \frac{\text{Mass of } \text{AgBr}}{w} \times 100\%}$$ $$\mathbf{\% \text{I} = \frac{127}{235} \times \frac{\text{Mass of } \text{AgI}}{w} \times 100\%}$$
5. Estimation of Sulphur: Carius Method
The compound is oxidized with fuming $\text{HNO}_3$ to sulphuric acid, precipitated with $\text{BaCl}_2$ as barium sulphate ($\text{BaSO}_4$): $$\mathbf{\% \text{S} = \frac{32}{233} \times \frac{\text{Mass of } \text{BaSO}_4}{w} \times 100\%}$$
6. Estimation of Phosphorus
Phosphorus is oxidized to phosphoric acid and weighed as magnesium pyrophosphate ($\text{Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7$): $$\mathbf{\% \text{P} = \frac{62}{222} \times \frac{\text{Mass of } \text{Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7}{w} \times 100\% = \frac{31}{111} \times \frac{\text{Mass of } \text{Mg}_2\text{P}_2\text{O}_7}{w} \times 100\%}$$

3. High-Yield Problem Archetypes & Structural JEE Traps
#
	Concept / Scenario
	Common Mistake / Trap
	Correct Chemical Principle
	1
	Kjeldahl's Method Applicability
	Applying Kjeldahl's formula to Nitrobenzene or Pyridine.
	Kjeldahl's method FAILS for: 1. Nitro compounds ($-\text{NO}_2$), 2. Azo compounds ($-\text{N}=\text{N}-$), and 3. Heterocyclic ring nitrogen (Pyridine, Quinoline). Nitrogen is NOT converted to $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$.
	2
	Halogen Test in SFE
	Adding $\text{AgNO}_3$ directly without boiling with $\text{HNO}_3$.
	$\text{NaCN}$ and $\text{Na}_2\text{S}$ present in SFE precipitate $\text{AgCN}$ (white) and $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$ (black), giving false halogen indications. Must boil with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ first.
	3
	Tollens' vs Fehling's with Benzaldehyde
	Assuming Benzaldehyde reduces Fehling's solution.
	Benzaldehyde ($\text{PhCHO}$) reduces Tollens' reagent, but DOES NOT reduce Fehling's or Benedict's solutions because it lacks $\alpha$-enolizable hydrogens.
	4
	Iodoform with $3^\circ$ Alcohols
	Assuming all compounds with $-\text{OH}$ and methyl groups give iodoform.
	Tert-butanol $[(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{OH}]$ cannot be oxidized to a carbonyl and yields negative iodoform. Only $\text{CH}_3\text{CH(OH)}-$ reacts.
	5
	NaHCO3 with Phenol
	Expecting phenol to give $\text{CO}_2$ with $\text{NaHCO}_3$.
	Ordinary phenol ($\text{p}K_a \approx 10$) is weaker than $\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$ ($\text{p}K_a \approx 6.4$). Only Picric acid and dinitrophenols give effervescence.
	6
	Beilstein Test Limitations
	Assuming green flame is unique to halogens.
	Fluorine does not give a green flame; Urea, thiourea, and certain pyridine derivatives also produce a false positive green flame.
	7
	Blood Red Lassaigne Coloration
	Assuming red color means absence of Nitrogen.
	Blood red color ($[\text{Fe}(\text{SCN})]^{2+}$) proves that both Nitrogen AND Sulphur are present together in the compound.
	8
	Aqueous Tension in Dumas Method
	Using observed barometric pressure directly in Dumas calculation.
	Water vapor pressure (aqueous tension) MUST be subtracted: $P_{\text{dry}} = P_{\text{observed}} - \text{Aqueous Tension}$.
	9
	Carbylamine with Secondary Amines
	Believing diethylamine gives carbylamine test.
	The carbylamine test is strictly selective for $1^\circ$ amines (aliphatic or aromatic). Secondary and tertiary amines are negative.
	10
	Hydrogenation of Benzene Ring
	Counting $3$ moles of $\text{H}_2$ consumed by benzene at room temperature.
	Aromatic $\pi$ bonds are resonance-stabilized and unreactive to catalytic hydrogenation at room temperature ($\text{H}_2 / \text{Ni}, 25^\circ\text{C}$).
