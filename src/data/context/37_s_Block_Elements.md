## 1. Group 1 Elements: Alkali Metals Physical & Periodic Invariants


### 1.1 Electronic Configurations & Atomic/Ionic Radii
* **General Electronic Configuration:** $[ \text{Noble Gas} ] ns^1$.
* The solitary valence $s$-electron is shielded effectively by inner electron shells, resulting in low effective nuclear charge ($Z_{\text{eff}}$), largest atomic and ionic radii in their respective periods, and minimal first ionization enthalpies ($IE_1$).
* **Radii Trend (Increases Down the Group):**
  $$\text{Atomic Radius: } \mathbf{\text{Li} < \text{Na} < \text{K} < \text{Rb} < \text{Cs}}$$
  $$\text{Ionic Radius: } \mathbf{\text{Li}^+ (76\text{ pm}) < \text{Na}^+ (102\text{ pm}) < \text{K}^+ (138\text{ pm}) < \text{Rb}^+ (152\text{ pm}) < \text{Cs}^+ (167\text{ pm})}$$


---


### 1.2 Density Trend & The Potassium Anomaly
Alkali metals are characterized by unusually low densities due to their large atomic volumes. They are light metals ($\text{Li}, \text{Na}, \text{K}$ are lighter than water):
* **Normal Expectation:** Density should increase down the group as atomic mass increases faster than atomic volume.
* **The High-Yield Inversion Anomaly:**
  $$\mathbf{\text{Density Order:}\quad \text{Li}\ (0.53) < \text{K}\ (0.86) < \text{Na}\ (0.97) < \text{Rb}\ (1.53) < \text{Cs}\ (1.90)\ \text{g/cm}^3}$$
* **Physical Origin:** Potassium ($\text{K}$) has an anomalous increase in atomic volume due to the presence of vacant, diffuse $3d$-subshell orbitals in the inner core, which expands the lattice volume disproportionately relative to its mass increase. Consequently, **potassium is lighter than sodium**.


---


### 1.3 Hydration Enthalpy, Hydrated Radii, & Ionic Mobility
Because hydration enthalpy is governed by electrostatic charge density:
$$\Delta H_{\text{hyd}} \propto \frac{z^2}{r}$$
* **Hydration Enthalpy Trend (Decreases Down Group):**
  $$\mathbf{\text{Li}^+\ (-506) \gg \text{Na}^+\ (-406) > \text{K}^+\ (-322) > \text{Rb}^+\ (-297) > \text{Cs}^+\ (-264)\ \text{kJ/mol}}$$
* **Hydrated Radii in Aqueous Solution:**
  Because $\text{Li}^+$ has the highest charge density, it binds the largest shell of water molecules:
  $$\mathbf{\text{Hydrated Radius:}\quad \text{Li}^+_{\text{aq}} > \text{Na}^+_{\text{aq}} > \text{K}^+_{\text{aq}} > \text{Rb}^+_{\text{aq}} > \text{Cs}^+_{\text{aq}}}$$
* **Ionic Mobility & Electrical Conductance in Water:**
  Larger hydrated spheres encounter higher frictional resistance moving through aqueous media:
  $$\mathbf{\text{Ionic Conductance / Mobility:}\quad \text{Cs}^+_{\text{aq}} > \text{Rb}^+_{\text{aq}} > \text{K}^+_{\text{aq}} > \text{Na}^+_{\text{aq}} > \text{Li}^+_{\text{aq}}}$$
* **Hydrate Salt Formation:** $\text{Li}^+$ is the only alkali metal cation that forms hydrated crystalline salts (e.g., $\text{LiCl} \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$); salts of $\text{Na}^+, \text{K}^+, \text{Rb}^+, \text{Cs}^+$ do not form solid hydrates.

### 1.5 Flame Photometry & Photoelectric Applications
* **Flame Emission Mechanism:** In a non-luminous Bunsen flame, thermal energy excites the loosely bound outer $s$-electron to higher energy levels. When the excited electron cascades back to the ground state, it emits radiation in the visible region of the electromagnetic spectrum:
  * **Lithium ($\text{Li}$):** Crimson Red ($670.8\text{ nm}$)
  * **Sodium ($\text{Na}$):** Golden Yellow ($589.2\text{ nm}$, Sodium D-lines)
  * **Potassium ($\text{K}$):** Violet / Lilac ($766.5\text{ nm}$)
  * **Rubidium ($\text{Rb}$):** Red Violet ($780.0\text{ nm}$)
  * **Caesium ($\text{Cs}$):** Sky Blue ($455.5\text{ nm}$)
* **Photoelectric Utility:** Potassium and Caesium possess ultra-low ionization enthalpies and low work functions ($\phi$). Shining visible light ejects photoelectrons effortlessly, making $\text{Cs}$ and $\text{K}$ ideal for photoelectric cells and solar sensors.


---


### 1.6 Reducing Power Thermodynamics: The Lithium Super-Reducing Anomaly
* **Observation:** In the gas phase, reducing power tracks ionization energy: $\text{Cs} > \text{Rb} > \text{K} > \text{Na} > \text{Li}$. However, in aqueous solution, **Lithium is the most powerful reducing agent in the entire Periodic Table** ($E^\circ = -3.05\text{ V}$):
  $$\mathbf{\text{Aqueous Reducing Power:}\quad \text{Li} > \text{Cs} > \text{Rb} > \text{K} > \text{Na}}$$
* **Thermodynamic Analysis via Born-Haber Cycle:**
  $$\text{M}(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta H_{\text{sub}}} \text{M}(g) \xrightarrow{IE_1} \text{M}^+(g) \xrightarrow{\Delta H_{\text{hyd}}} \text{M}^+(aq)$$
  $$\Delta H_{\text{total}} = \Delta H_{\text{sub}} + IE_1 + \Delta H_{\text{hyd}}$$
  * Although $\text{Li}$ has the highest sublimation enthalpy and highest ionization enthalpy among alkali metals, its minute ionic radius ($r_{\text{Li}^+} = 76\text{ pm}$) generates an **enormously exothermic hydration enthalpy** ($\Delta H_{\text{hyd}} = -506\text{ kJ/mol}$).
  * This colossal energy release overcompensates for the ionization cost, making $\Delta H_{\text{total}}$ deeply negative and driving $E^\circ$ to $-3.05\text{ V}$.

## 2. Chemical Reactivity of Alkali Metals


### 2.1 Reactivity with Atmospheric Gases: Oxide Combustion Hierarchy
When ignited in air or excess oxygen, alkali metals exhibit a sharp divergence in oxidation products:


| Alkali Metal | Principal Combustion Product | Trace / Minor Product | Formal Oxidation State of Oxygen | Magnetic Character |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Lithium ($\text{Li}$)** | **Normal Oxide ($\text{Li}_2\text{O}$)** | Peroxide ($\text{Li}_2\text{O}_2$) | $-2$ (Oxide ion $\text{O}^{2-}$) | Diamagnetic |
| **Sodium ($\text{Na}$)** | **Peroxide ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$)** | Normal Oxide ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}$) | $-1$ (Peroxide ion $\text{O}_2^{2-}$) | Diamagnetic |
| **Potassium ($\text{K}$)** | **Superoxide ($\text{KO}_2$)** | Peroxide ($\text{K}_2\text{O}_2$) | $-1/2$ (Superoxide ion $\text{O}_2^-$) | **Paramagnetic** |
| **Rubidium ($\text{Rb}$)** | **Superoxide ($\text{RbO}_2$)** | — | $-1/2$ (Superoxide ion $\text{O}_2^-$) | **Paramagnetic** |
| **Caesium ($\text{Cs}$)** | **Superoxide ($\text{CsO}_2$)** | — | $-1/2$ (Superoxide ion $\text{O}_2^-$) | **Paramagnetic** |


* **Lattice Energy Stabilization Rule:** A small cation ($\text{Li}^+$) stabilizes a small anion ($\text{O}^{2-}$), while large cations ($\text{K}^+, \text{Rb}^+, \text{Cs}^+$) with low positive charge density stabilize large, polarizable polyatomic anions ($\text{O}_2^{2-}, \text{O}_2^-$) via favorable lattice packing.
* **Hydrolysis Reactions of Oxides:**
  * Normal Oxide: $\text{Li}_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{LiOH}$
  * Peroxide: $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ (cold); decomposes to $\text{O}_2$ when warm.
  * Superoxide: $2\text{KO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{KOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2 + \text{O}_2\uparrow$
* **Air Purification in Submarines & Space Capsules:**
  $$\mathbf{4\text{KO}_2 + 2\text{CO}_2 \to 2\text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + 3\text{O}_2\uparrow}$$
  $$\mathbf{\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2 + \text{CO}_2 \to \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2\uparrow}$$
  Both $\text{KO}_2$ and $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$ absorb respiratory $\text{CO}_2$ and regenerate life-supporting $\text{O}_2$ gas simultaneously.


---


### 2.2 Direct Nitride Formation: The Lithium Anomaly
* When heated in dry air or pure nitrogen, **Lithium is the only alkali metal that reacts directly with $\text{N}_2$** to form a stable ionic nitride:
  $$6\text{Li}(s) + \text{N}_2(g) \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{Li}_3\text{N}(s) \quad (\text{Ruby-red crystalline solid})$$
  $$\text{Li}_3\text{N} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 3\text{LiOH} + \text{NH}_3\uparrow$$
* Other alkali metals ($\text{Na, K, Rb, Cs}$) do NOT form nitrides directly because their larger cations cannot stabilize the small, triply-charged nitride anion ($\text{N}^{3-}$).


---


### 2.3 Solutions in Liquid Ammonia & Ammoniated Electrons


![Oxide-Peroxide-Superoxide Hierarchy, Liquid Ammonia, and Solvay Process](/media/oxide_superoxide_hierarchy_liquid_ammonia_and_solvay_process.webp)
*Description: Two-panel reaction chemistry and industrial process flowsheet: (A) Oxygen reactivity spectrum of alkali metals (normal oxide, peroxide, superoxide) and dissolution behavior in liquid ammonia contrasting dilute blue paramagnetic solutions with concentrated bronze diamagnetic states; (B) Step-by-step chemical flowsheet of the Solvay Ammonia-Soda process for $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$ detailing carbonation, calcination, ammonia recovery via $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$, and explaining why potassium carbonate cannot be manufactured via Solvay synthesis, alongside the Castner-Kellner mercury-cell electrolysis of brine.*


* **Dissolution Phenomenon:** Alkali metals dissolve readily in liquid ammonia without hydrogen evolution to yield conducting solutions:
  $$\mathbf{\text{M} + (x + y)\text{NH}_3 \to [\text{M}(\text{NH}_3)_x]^+ + [e(\text{NH}_3)_y]^-}$$
* **Dilute Solutions ($< 3\text{ M}$):**
  * **Deep Blue Color:** Caused by light absorption in the red region ($\approx 1500\text{ nm}$) by free ammoniated electrons trapped in solvent cavities.
  * **Paramagnetic:** Presence of individual unpaired ammoniated electrons ($\mu \approx 1.73\text{ BM}$).
  * **High Electrical Conductivity:** Conduction occurs via both ammoniated cations and ammoniated electrons.
  * **Potent Reducing Agent:** Widely utilized in the Birch reduction of aromatic rings.
* **Concentrated Solutions ($> 3\text{ M}$):**
  * **Copper-Bronze Metallic Luster:** Ammoniated electrons coalesce and pair up.
  * **Diamagnetic:** Loss of paramagnetism due to electron spin-pairing: $2[e(\text{NH}_3)_y]^- \rightleftharpoons [e_2(\text{NH}_3)_{2y}]^{2-}$.
  * **Metallic Conduction:** Electrical conductivity approaches that of pure liquid metals.
* **Decomposition on Standing / Catalysis:**
  $$\text{M} + \text{NH}_3(l) \xrightarrow{\text{Fe catalyst}} \text{MNH}_2 (\text{Metal Amide}) + \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\uparrow$$

## 3. Thermal Stability & Decomposition of Oxo-Salts


### 3.1 Carbonates & Bicarbonates
* **Thermal Stability of Carbonates:**
  All alkali metal carbonates are thermally stable and do not decompose below $1000^\circ\text{C}$, **except $\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3$**:
  $$\mathbf{\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Li}_2\text{O}(s) + \text{CO}_2\uparrow}$$
  $$\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3, \text{K}_2\text{CO}_3, \text{Rb}_2\text{CO}_3, \text{Cs}_2\text{CO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{No Decomposition (Melts without decomposition)}$$
  * Polarization Explanation: $\text{Li}^+$ has high polarizing power and polarizes the large $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ anion, pulling electron density toward oxygen and facilitating cleavage into $\text{Li}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$.
* **Bicarbonates:**
  * $\text{NaHCO}_3, \text{KHCO}_3, \text{RbHCO}_3, \text{CsHCO}_3$ exist as stable crystalline solids.
  * $\text{LiHCO}_3$ is unstable and **exists only in aqueous solution**; attempting to isolate it yields $\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3, \text{H}_2\text{O},$ and $\text{CO}_2$.
  * Thermal decomposition of solid sodium bicarbonate:
    $$2\text{NaHCO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{> 100^\circ\text{C}} \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(g) + \text{CO}_2(g)\uparrow$$


---


### 3.2 Nitrates
Alkali metal nitrates decompose differently upon heating:
* **Sodium, Potassium, Rubidium, Caesium Nitrates:**
  $$2\text{MNO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{500^\circ\text{C}} 2\text{MNO}_2(s) (\text{Metal Nitrite}) + \text{O}_2(g)\uparrow$$
  At higher temperatures ($> 800^\circ\text{C}$):
  $$2\text{NaNO}_2 \xrightarrow{> 800^\circ\text{C}} \text{Na}_2\text{O} + \text{N}_2\uparrow + \frac{3}{2}\text{O}_2\uparrow$$
* **Lithium Nitrate Anomaly:**
  Behaves like magnesium nitrate (diagonal relationship) and decomposes to oxide and brown $\text{NO}_2$ gas:
  $$\mathbf{4\text{LiNO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{Li}_2\text{O}(s) + 4\text{NO}_2(g)\uparrow + \text{O}_2(g)\uparrow}$$

## 4. Industrial Compounds of Sodium


### 4.1 Sodium Hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$, Caustic Soda)
Manufactured commercially by the electrolysis of aqueous brine ($\text{NaCl}$) in the **Castner-Kellner Cell**:
* **Cathode:** Flowing liquid mercury ($\text{Hg}$).
* **Anode:** Graphite / Titanium rods.
* **Electrochemical Reactions:**
  $$\text{Anode: } 2\text{Cl}^-(aq) \to \text{Cl}_2(g)\uparrow + 2e^-$$
  $$\text{Cathode: } \text{Na}^+(aq) + e^- \xrightarrow{\text{Hg}} \text{Na-Hg} \quad (\text{Sodium Amalgam})$$
  *(Note: $\text{H}^+$ is not discharged at the mercury cathode because of its extremely high hydrogen overpotential on mercury).*
* **Decomposer Reaction:**
  $$2\text{Na-Hg} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{NaOH}(aq) + \text{H}_2(g)\uparrow + 2\text{Hg}(l)\quad (\text{Hg recycled})$$
* **High-Yield Chemical Reactions of $\text{NaOH}$:**
  1. **With Halogens ($\text{Cl}_2, \text{Br}_2, \text{I}_2$):**
     * Cold & Dilute $\text{NaOH}$:
       $$2\text{NaOH} + \text{Cl}_2 \to \text{NaCl} + \text{NaOCl} (\text{Sodium Hypochlorite}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
     * Hot & Concentrated $\text{NaOH}$:
       $$6\text{NaOH} + 3\text{Cl}_2 \to 5\text{NaCl} + \text{NaClO}_3 (\text{Sodium Chlorate}) + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  2. **With Fluorine ($\text{F}_2$):**
     * Cold & Dilute: $2\text{F}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \to 2\text{NaF} + \mathbf{\text{OF}_2\uparrow} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$
     * Hot & Concentrated: $2\text{F}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \to 4\text{NaF} + \mathbf{\text{O}_2\uparrow} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$
  3. **With White Phosphorus ($\text{P}_4$):**
     $$\text{P}_4 + 3\text{NaOH} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathbf{\text{PH}_3\uparrow} + 3\text{NaH}_2\text{PO}_2 \quad (\text{Sodium Hypophosphite})$$
  4. **With Elemental Sulfur ($\text{S}_8$):**
     $$3\text{S} + 6\text{NaOH} \to 2\text{Na}_2\text{S} + \text{Na}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3 (\text{Sodium Thiosulfate / Hypo}) + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  5. **With Amphoteric Metals ($\text{Zn, Al, Be, Sn, Pb}$):**
     $$\text{Zn} + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{Na}_2\text{ZnO}_2 (\text{Sodium Zincate}) + \text{H}_2\uparrow$$
     $$2\text{Al} + 2\text{NaOH} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{NaAlO}_2 (\text{Sodium Meta-aluminate}) + 3\text{H}_2\uparrow$$

### 4.2 Sodium Carbonate ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$, Washing Soda)
Manufactured via the **Solvay (Ammonia-Soda) Process**:
1. Saturation of concentrated brine with ammonia:
   $$\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \to \text{NH}_4\text{HCO}_3$$
2. Carbonation to precipitate sparingly soluble sodium bicarbonate:
   $$\mathbf{\text{NaCl} + \text{NH}_4\text{HCO}_3 \to \text{NaHCO}_3\downarrow + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}}$$
3. Calcination of $\text{NaHCO}_3$:
   $$2\text{NaHCO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3(s) + \text{CO}_2(g)\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}(g)$$
4. Ammonia Recovery:
   $$\text{CaCO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow$$
   $$\text{CaO} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Ca(OH)}_2$$
   $$2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \to 2\text{NH}_3\uparrow (\text{recycled}) + \mathbf{\text{CaCl}_2} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
* **By-Product:** Calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$).
* **Critical JEE Limitation:** Potassium carbonate ($\text{K}_2\text{CO}_3$) **CANNOT** be prepared by the Solvay process because potassium bicarbonate ($\text{KHCO}_3$) is too soluble in water to precipitate upon adding $\text{NH}_4\text{HCO}_3$ to $\text{KCl}$.
* **Efflorescence of Washing Soda:**
  $$\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{dry air}} \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O} (\text{Monohydrate}) + 9\text{H}_2\text{O}\uparrow$$
  $$\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{> 373\text{ K}} \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 (\text{Soda Ash, Anhydrous}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}\uparrow$$

## 5. Group 2 Elements: Alkaline Earth Metals


### 5.1 Physical & Periodic Trends
* **General Electronic Configuration:** $[ \text{Noble Gas} ] ns^2$.
* Compared to Group 1, Group 2 elements have smaller atomic radii, higher nuclear charge, higher melting and boiling points, higher densities, and greater hardness due to two valence electrons participating in metallic bonding.
* **Ionization Enthalpies:**
  * $IE_1(\text{Group 2}) > IE_1(\text{Group 1})$ (smaller size, higher $Z_{\text{eff}}$, stable full $s^2$ subshell).
  * $IE_2(\text{Group 2}) \ll IE_2(\text{Group 1})$ (removing a second electron from Group 2 leaves a noble gas core, whereas in Group 1 it breaks a stable noble gas octet).
* **Flame Colors:**
  * **Calcium ($\text{Ca}$):** Brick Red ($622\text{ nm}$)
  * **Strontium ($\text{Sr}$):** Crimson Red ($674\text{ nm}$)
  * **Barium ($\text{Ba}$):** Apple Green ($553\text{ nm}$)
  * **Beryllium ($\text{Be}$) and Magnesium ($\text{Mg}$):** **NO FLAME COLOR** because their atomic sizes are tiny and valence electrons are bound so tightly that flame temperatures cannot excite electrons to visible emission states.


---


### 5.2 Chemical Reactivity of Alkaline Earth Metals
1. **Reactivity with Air:**
   * $\text{Be}$ and $\text{Mg}$ are kinetically inert at room temperature due to the formation of a protective, impervious oxide film.
   * When ignited, both form normal oxides ($\text{MO}$) and nitrides ($\text{M}_3\text{N}_2$):
     $$3\text{Mg} + \text{N}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2 \quad (\text{Hydrolysis yields } 3\text{Mg(OH)}_2 + 2\text{NH}_3\uparrow)$$
   * $\text{Ba}$ forms barium peroxide ($\text{BaO}_2$).
2. **Reactivity with Water:**
   * $\text{Be}$ does not react with water or steam.
   * $\text{Mg}$ reacts only with hot water/steam: $\text{Mg} + \text{H}_2\text{O}(g) \to \text{MgO} + \text{H}_2\uparrow$.
   * $\text{Ca, Sr, Ba}$ react readily with cold water to evolve $\text{H}_2$ and form hydroxides.
3. **Halides & Structural Polymorphism of $\text{BeCl}_2$:**
   * Beryllium chloride ($\text{BeCl}_2$) is covalent due to high polarizing power of $\text{Be}^{2+}$.
   * **In Solid State:** Forms an infinite polymeric chlorobridged chain $(\text{BeCl}_2)_n$ where each $\text{Be}$ atom is tetrahedrally coordinated via coordinate covalent bonds ($sp^3$ hybridized).
   * **In Vapor Phase ($< 1200\text{ K}$):** Exists as a chloro-bridged planar dimer $(\text{BeCl}_2)_2$ with $sp^2$ hybridized $\text{Be}$.
   * **Above $1200\text{ K}$:** Dissociates into a linear monomer $\text{Cl}-\text{Be}-\text{Cl}$ with $sp$ hybridized $\text{Be}$.
4. **Internal Hydrolysis of Hydrated Chlorides:**
   * $\text{MgCl}_2 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$ cannot be dehydrated by simple heating because it undergoes internal hydrolysis:
     $$\mathbf{\text{MgCl}_2 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{MgO} + 2\text{HCl}\uparrow + 5\text{H}_2\text{O}\uparrow}$$
   * To prepare anhydrous $\text{MgCl}_2$, it must be heated in a dry stream of $\text{HCl}$ gas, or prepared via:
     $$\text{MgO} + \text{C} + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{MgCl}_2 + \text{CO}\uparrow$$

## 6. Solubility & Thermal Stability Trajectories of Group 2 Salts


### 6.1 Solubility Trajectories (Lattice vs. Hydration Enthalpy Competition)
Solubility in water depends on the relative magnitudes of lattice enthalpy ($U$) and hydration enthalpy ($\Delta H_{\text{hyd}}$):
$$\Delta H_{\text{sol}} = U - |\Delta H_{\text{hyd}}|$$
* **Hydroxides ($\text{M(OH)}_2$):**
  $$\mathbf{\text{Solubility Order:}\quad \text{Be(OH)}_2 < \text{Mg(OH)}_2 < \text{Ca(OH)}_2 < \text{Sr(OH)}_2 < \text{Ba(OH)}_2\ (\text{Increases Down Group})}$$
  * Reason: $\text{OH}^-$ is a small anion. Lattice enthalpy ($U \propto \frac{1}{r_+ + r_-}$) decreases much faster than hydration enthalpy down the group, making dissolution progressively more exothermic.
  * Basic strength also increases: $\text{Be(OH)}_2$ (amphoteric) $\to \text{Mg(OH)}_2$ (weak base) $\to \text{Ba(OH)}_2$ (strong base).
* **Sulfates ($\text{MSO}_4$):**
  $$\mathbf{\text{Solubility Order:}\quad \text{BeSO}_4 > \text{MgSO}_4 \gg \text{CaSO}_4 > \text{SrSO}_4 > \text{BaSO}_4\ (\text{Decreases Down Group})}$$
  * Reason: $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ is a large anion. The lattice enthalpy remains almost constant down the group because $r_-$ dominates $(r_+ + r_-)$. However, $\Delta H_{\text{hyd}}$ of the cation drops rapidly as cation size increases. Thus, hydration enthalpy fails to overcome lattice energy, making $\text{BaSO}_4$ highly insoluble.
* **Carbonates ($\text{MCO}_3$):**
  $$\mathbf{\text{Solubility Order:}\quad \text{BeCO}_3 > \text{MgCO}_3 > \text{CaCO}_3 > \text{SrCO}_3 > \text{BaCO}_3\ (\text{Decreases Down Group})}$$


---


### 6.2 Thermal Stability Trajectories of Carbonates
Thermal stability increases down the group as cation size increases:
$$\mathbf{\text{Thermal Stability:}\quad \text{BeCO}_3 < \text{MgCO}_3 < \text{CaCO}_3 < \text{SrCO}_3 < \text{BaCO}_3}$$
* $\text{BeCO}_3$ is so unstable that it decomposes at room temperature and must be preserved under an atmosphere of $\text{CO}_2$:
  $$\text{BeCO}_3 \xrightarrow{25^\circ\text{C}} \text{BeO} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow \quad (T_{\text{decomp}} \approx 100\text{ K})$$
  $$\text{BaCO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{BaO} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow \quad (T_{\text{decomp}} \approx 1630\text{ K})$$

### 7.2 The Lithium - Magnesium Diagonal Parallels
Due to nearly identical ionic radii ($\text{Li}^+ = 76\text{ pm}, \text{Mg}^{2+} = 72\text{ pm}$) and similar polarizing power:
1. **Direct Nitride Formation:** Both combine directly with nitrogen upon heating: $\text{Li}_3\text{N}$ and $\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2$.
2. **Thermal Decomposition of Carbonates:** Both carbonates decompose on heating to give oxides and $\text{CO}_2$:
   $$\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Li}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow; \quad \text{MgCO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{MgO} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow$$
3. **Deliquescent Chlorides:** Both $\text{LiCl}$ and $\text{MgCl}_2$ are deliquescent, crystallize as hydrates ($\text{LiCl} \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{MgCl}_2 \cdot 8\text{H}_2\text{O}$), and are soluble in organic solvents (ethanol).
4. **Normal Oxides:** Both form only normal oxides ($\text{Li}_2\text{O}, \text{MgO}$) upon burning in excess air, not superoxides.
5. **Solubility of Fluorides & Phosphates:** Fluorides, carbonates, and phosphates of both elements are sparingly soluble in water.


---


### 7.3 The Beryllium - Aluminium Diagonal Parallels
Due to virtually identical charge/radius ratios ($\text{Be}^{2+} \approx 0.31 / 31\text{ pm}$, $\text{Al}^{3+} \approx 0.53 / 53\text{ pm}$):
1. **Amphoteric Oxides and Hydroxides:** Both $\text{BeO}$ and $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ dissolve in both strong acids and strong alkalis:
   $$\text{BeO} + 2\text{HCl} \to \text{BeCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}; \quad \text{BeO} + 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Na}_2[\text{Be(OH)}_4] \quad (\text{Sodium Beryllate})$$
   $$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 6\text{HCl} \to 2\text{AlCl}_3 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}; \quad \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 2\text{NaOH} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{Na}[\text{Al(OH)}_4] \quad (\text{Sodium Aluminate})$$
2. **Passivation by Nitric Acid:** Both metals are rendered passive by concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ due to the instantaneous formation of an impenetrable oxide film.
3. **Chloro-Bridged Polymeric Halides:** Both $\text{BeCl}_2$ and $\text{AlCl}_3$ form chloro-bridged dimeric or polymeric structures in the vapor/solid phase with coordinate bonds, dissolve in organic solvents, and act as potent Lewis acid catalysts in Friedel-Crafts alkylation.
4. **Carbide Hydrolysis (Methanides):**
   $$\mathbf{\text{Be}_2\text{C} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{Be(OH)}_2 + \text{CH}_4\uparrow}$$
   $$\mathbf{\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 4\text{Al(OH)}_3 + 3\text{CH}_4\uparrow}$$
   Both contain the methanide ion $\text{C}^{4-}$ and yield pure methane gas upon contact with water.

## 8. Industrial Compounds of Calcium


### 8.1 Quicklime ($\text{CaO}$) & Slaked Lime ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$)
1. **Quicklime ($\text{CaO}$):**
   * Prepared by calcining limestone in a rotary kiln at $1070-1270\text{ K}$:
     $$\text{CaCO}_3(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{CaO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g)\uparrow \quad (\text{Reversible; } \text{CO}_2 \text{ continuously removed})$$
   * **Slaking of Lime:** Addition of limited water to quicklime produces a hissing sound, immense heat evolution, and cracks the lumps into a fine white powder of slaked lime:
     $$\mathbf{\text{CaO}(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \to \text{Ca(OH)}_2(s) \quad (\Delta H = -63\text{ kJ/mol})}$$
   * Basic Slag Formation in Metallurgy:
     $$\text{CaO} + \text{SiO}_2 \to \text{CaSiO}_3 (\text{Calcium Silicate Slag})$$
     $$3\text{CaO} + \text{P}_4\text{O}_{10} \to \text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 (\text{Thomas Slag})$$
   * Carbide & Fertilizer Synthesis:
     $$\text{CaO} + 3\text{C} \xrightarrow{2000^\circ\text{C}} \text{CaC}_2 (\text{Calcium Carbide}) + \text{CO}\uparrow$$
     $$\text{CaC}_2 + \text{N}_2 \xrightarrow{1100^\circ\text{C}} \mathbf{\text{CaCN}_2 + \text{C}\quad (\text{Nitrolim Fertilizer})}$$
2. **Slaked Lime ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$):**
   * Aqueous suspension is called **Milk of Lime**; clear filtered solution is **Lime Water**.
   * **Lime Water Test for $\text{CO}_2$:**
     $$\text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{CO}_2 \to \text{CaCO}_3\downarrow (\text{Milkiness}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
     $$\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{CO}_2(\text{excess}) + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2(aq) \quad (\text{Milkiness disappears due to soluble bicarbonate})$$
   * **Bleaching Powder Formation:**
     $$2\text{Ca(OH)}_2 + 2\text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{dry, } 40^\circ\text{C}} \mathbf{\text{CaCl}_2 + \text{Ca(OCl)}_2 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{O}\quad (\text{Bleaching Powder})}$$

### 8.2 Plaster of Paris ($\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}$, POP)
* **Manufacture from Gypsum:**
  Prepared by heating gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$) in a kiln carefully controlled at **$393\text{ K}\ (120^\circ\text{C})$**:
  $$\mathbf{2(\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}) \xrightarrow{393\text{ K}} 2\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}\uparrow}$$
  $$\text{or}\quad \text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{393\text{ K}} \text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} + 1\frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}\uparrow$$
* **Dead Burnt Plaster Anomaly:**
  If heated above $393\text{ K}$ ($> 120^\circ\text{C}$), gypsum loses all water of crystallization to form anhydrous calcium sulfate:
  $$\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{> 393\text{ K}} \mathbf{\text{CaSO}_4 (\text{Dead Burnt Plaster})} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  Dead burnt plaster completely loses the ability to re-hydrate and set with water!
* **Setting Mechanism of POP:**
  When mixed with water, POP forms a plastic paste that sets into a hard, rigid mass in two stages:
  1. **Setting Stage (5-15 mins):** Hemihydrate transforms into an orthorhombic dihydrate.
  2. **Hardening Stage (hours):** Orthorhombic dihydrate recrystallizes into interlocking needles of monoclinic gypsum.
  * Setting is accompanied by a **$1\%$ volume expansion**, ensuring it fills every crevice of a mold (utilized in orthopedic surgical casts, dentistry, and sculptures).

## 10. High-Yield JEE Problem Archetypes & Trap Logs


### 10.1 Archetype 1: Action of Heat on Nitrates, Carbonates, & Oxalates
* **Nitrate Trap:**
  * Heating $\text{NaNO}_3 \to \text{NaNO}_2 + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2$ (No colored gas evolved).
  * Heating $\text{LiNO}_3 \to \text{Li}_2\text{O} + 2\mathbf{\text{NO}_2\uparrow}\ (\text{Brown gas}) + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2$.
  * Heating $\text{Mg(NO}_3)_2 \to \text{MgO} + 2\mathbf{\text{NO}_2\uparrow} + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2$.
* **Alkali Carbonate Trap:**
  * Only $\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3$ decomposes on heating to $\text{Li}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$.
  * $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ and $\text{K}_2\text{CO}_3$ do NOT decompose under ordinary Bunsen flame heating!


### 10.2 Archetype 2: Carbide Classification & Hydrolysis Products
* **Methanides (contain $\text{C}^{4-}$):**
  $$\text{Be}_2\text{C} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{Be(OH)}_2 + \mathbf{\text{CH}_4\uparrow}$$
  $$\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 4\text{Al(OH)}_3 + 3\mathbf{\text{CH}_4\uparrow}$$
* **Acetylides (contain $\text{C}_2^{2-}$):**
  $$\text{CaC}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \mathbf{\text{C}_2\text{H}_2\uparrow}\quad (\text{Ethyne / Acetylene})$$
  $$\text{BaC}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Ba(OH)}_2 + \mathbf{\text{C}_2\text{H}_2\uparrow}$$
* **Allylides (contain $\text{C}_3^{4-}$):**
  $$\text{Mg}_2\text{C}_3 + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{Mg(OH)}_2 + \mathbf{\text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}\uparrow}\quad (\text{Propyne / Allylene})$$


### 10.3 Archetype 3: Disproportionation with $\text{NaOH}$
* $\text{P}_4 + 3\text{NaOH} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{PH}_3\uparrow + 3\text{NaH}_2\text{PO}_2$. Phosphorus oxidizes from $0 \to +1$ (in $\text{H}_2\text{PO}_2^-$) and reduces from $0 \to -3$ (in $\text{PH}_3$).
* $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{NaOH}\ (\text{cold}) \to \text{NaCl} + \text{NaOCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Chlorine $0 \to -1$ and $0 \to +1$).
* $3\text{Cl}_2 + 6\text{NaOH}\ (\text{hot}) \to 5\text{NaCl} + \text{NaClO}_3 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Chlorine $0 \to -1$ and $0 \to +5$).
