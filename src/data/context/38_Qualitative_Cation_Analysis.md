### 1.1 The Master Analytical Group Classification Scheme
In qualitative inorganic analysis, basic radicals (cations) are classified into seven analytical groups (Group 0 to Group VI) based on the systematic differences in the solubility products ($K_{\text{sp}}$) of their insoluble chlorides, sulfides, hydroxides, carbonates, and phosphates:

| Analytical Group | Cations Included | Group Reagent | Precipitated Chemical Species | Precipitate Characteristics & Colors |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Group 0** | $\text{NH}_4^+$ | $\text{NaOH}$ or $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (warm) | Gaseous $\text{NH}_3$ evolved | Pungent gas, turns red litmus blue, dense white fumes with $\text{HCl}$ |
| **Group I** | $\text{Ag}^+, \text{Pb}^{2+}, \text{Hg}_2^{2+}$ | Dilute $\text{HCl}$ (Cold) | Chlorides: $\text{AgCl}, \text{PbCl}_2, \text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2$ | White precipitates ($\text{PbCl}_2$ uniquely soluble in boiling water) |
| **Group IIA***(Copper Group)* | $\text{Hg}^{2+}, \text{Pb}^{2+}, \text{Bi}^{3+}, \text{Cu}^{2+}, \text{Cd}^{2+}$ | $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ gas in presence of dilute $\text{HCl}$ ($0.3\text{ M}$) | Sulfides: $\text{HgS}, \text{PbS}, \text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3, \text{CuS}, \text{CdS}$ | Insoluble in Yellow Ammonium Sulfide (YAS); $\text{HgS, PbS, Bi}_2\text{S}_3, \text{CuS}$ are black; $\text{CdS}$ is canary yellow |
| **Group IIB***(Arsenic Group)* | $\text{As}^{3+}, \text{Sb}^{3+}, \text{Sn}^{2+}, \text{Sn}^{4+}$ | $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ gas in presence of dilute $\text{HCl}$ ($0.3\text{ M}$) | Sulfides: $\text{As}_2\text{S}_3, \text{Sb}_2\text{S}_3, \text{SnS}, \text{SnS}_2$ | Soluble in Yellow Ammonium Sulfide (YAS); $\text{As}_2\text{S}_3$ (yellow), $\text{Sb}_2\text{S}_3$ (orange), $\text{SnS}$ (brown), $\text{SnS}_2$ (yellow) |
| **Group III** | $\text{Fe}^{3+}, \text{Al}^{3+}, \text{Cr}^{3+}$ | Solid $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ + excess $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ (after boiling with conc. $\text{HNO}_3$) | Hydroxides: $\text{Fe(OH)}_3, \text{Al(OH)}_3, \text{Cr(OH)}_3$ | $\text{Fe(OH)}_3$ (reddish-brown), $\text{Al(OH)}_3$ (gelatinous white), $\text{Cr(OH)}_3$ (green) |
| **Group IV** | $\text{Zn}^{2+}, \text{Mn}^{2+}, \text{Ni}^{2+}, \text{Co}^{2+}$ | $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ gas in presence of $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ | Sulfides: $\text{ZnS}, \text{MnS}, \text{NiS}, \text{CoS}$ | $\text{ZnS}$ (dirty white), $\text{MnS}$ (buff / flesh), $\text{NiS}$ (black), $\text{CoS}$ (black) |
| **Group V** | $\text{Ba}^{2+}, \text{Sr}^{2+}, \text{Ca}^{2+}$ | $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{CO}_3$ in presence of $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ | Carbonates: $\text{BaCO}_3, \text{SrCO}_3, \text{CaCO}_3$ | All form white precipitates (separated via differential solubility in $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$) |
| **Group VI** | $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ | $\text{Na}_2\text{HPO}_4$ in presence of $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ | Phosphate: $\text{Mg(NH}_4)\text{PO}_4 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$ | White crystalline precipitate |

#### 1.3.1 Precipitation Condition: Ionic Product vs. Solubility Product
Precipitation of an electrolyte $\text{A}_x\text{B}_y \rightleftharpoons x\text{A}^{y+} + y\text{B}^{x-}$ occurs if and only if its ionic product ($Q_{\text{sp}}$) exceeds its thermodynamic solubility product ($K_{\text{sp}}$):
$$\mathbf{Q_{\text{sp}} = [\text{A}^{y+}]^x [\text{B}^{x-}]^y > K_{\text{sp}}}$$
- If $Q_{\text{sp}} < K_{\text{sp}}$: Unsaturated solution (no precipitate forms).
- If $Q_{\text{sp}} = K_{\text{sp}}$: Saturated equilibrium solution.
- If $Q_{\text{sp}} > K_{\text{sp}}$: Supersaturated solution (precipitation occurs until equilibrium is restored).

---

#### 1.3.2 Why Dilute $\text{HCl}$ is Chosen in Group I (Reagent Selectivity)
- **Why not dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$?**
  If dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ were used instead of $\text{HCl}$, Group V cations ($\text{Ba}^{2+}, \text{Sr}^{2+}, \text{Ca}^{2+}$) and $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ would precipitate prematurely as insoluble sulfates ($\text{BaSO}_4, \text{SrSO}_4, \text{CaSO}_4$), hopelessly corrupting group separation.
- **Why not dilute $\text{HNO}_3$?**
  Nitric acid forms highly water-soluble nitrate salts with all cations, resulting in zero precipitation. Furthermore, $\text{HNO}_3$ is a powerful oxidizing agent; residual $\text{HNO}_3$ carried into Group II would oxidize $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ to colloidal sulfur ($\text{H}_2\text{S} + 2\text{HNO}_3 \to 2\text{NO}_2 + \text{S}\downarrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$), producing milky white turbidity that obscures metal sulfide detection.

---

#### 1.3.3 Selective Sulfide Precipitation: Group II vs. Group IV
The dissociation of hydrogen sulfide, a weak diprotic acid, proceeds in two steps:
$$\text{H}_2\text{S}(aq) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{HS}^- \quad (K_{a1} \approx 10^{-7})$$
$$\text{HS}^- \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{S}^{2-} \quad (K_{a2} \approx 10^{-14})$$
$$\mathbf{K_a = K_{a1} \cdot K_{a2} = \frac{[\text{H}^+]^2 [\text{S}^{2-}]}{[\text{H}_2\text{S}]} \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-21}}$$
- **In Group II (Acidic Medium: $\text{H}_2\text{S} + \text{dil. HCl}$):**
  $\text{HCl}$ is a strong acid that completely ionizes: $\text{HCl} \to \text{H}^+ + \text{Cl}^-$.
  By the **Common Ion Effect**, the high concentration of $[\text{H}^+]$ ($\approx 0.3\text{ M}$) suppresses the ionization of $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ overwhelmingly to the left.
  $$[\text{S}^{2-}] = \frac{K_a [\text{H}_2\text{S}]}{[\text{H}^+]^2} \approx \frac{(10^{-21})(0.1)}{(0.3)^2} \approx \mathbf{10^{-22}\text{ M}}$$
  At this minuscule sulfide concentration, **only the cations with extremely low solubility products** ($K_{\text{sp}} \approx 10^{-30}$ to $10^{-53}$, i.e., Group II sulfides like $\text{HgS, CuS, PbS, CdS}$) exceed their $K_{\text{sp}}$ and precipitate.
  Group IV sulfides have significantly higher solubility products ($K_{\text{sp}} \approx 10^{-15}$ to $10^{-24}$ for $\text{ZnS, MnS, NiS, CoS}$), so their ionic product fails to reach $K_{\text{sp}}$, and they remain completely dissolved in the filtrate.
- **In Group IV (Basic Medium: $\text{H}_2\text{S} + \text{NH}_4\text{OH} + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$):**
  The added base $\text{OH}^-$ removes $\text{H}^+$ ions by forming water: $\text{H}^+ + \text{OH}^- \to \text{H}_2\text{O}$.
  By Le Chatelier's principle, the equilibrium shifts dramatically to the right, elevating $[\text{S}^{2-}]$ by ten orders of magnitude ($\approx 10^{-7}\text{ M}$).
  Consequently, the ionic products of $\text{Zn}^{2+}, \text{Mn}^{2+}, \text{Ni}^{2+}, \text{Co}^{2+}$ easily surpass their higher $K_{\text{sp}}$ values, causing quantitative precipitation as Group IV sulfides.

---

#### 1.3.4 Selective Hydroxide Precipitation: Group III vs. Group IV/V
- **Role of $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ before $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$:**
  Ammonium hydroxide is a weak base: $\text{NH}_4\text{OH} \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_4^+ + \text{OH}^-$.
  Adding solid ammonium chloride provides a massive concentration of the common ion $\text{NH}_4^+$ ($\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} \to \text{NH}_4^+ + \text{Cl}^-$).
  This common ion pushes the dissociation equilibrium back to the left, drastically depressing $[\text{OH}^-]$ to $\approx 1.5 \times 10^{-9}\text{ M}$.
  - Group III hydroxides have extraordinarily small solubility products:
    $$K_{\text{sp}}[\text{Fe(OH)}_3] \approx 3.8 \times 10^{-38}, \quad K_{\text{sp}}[\text{Al(OH)}_3] \approx 1.3 \times 10^{-33}, \quad K_{\text{sp}}[\text{Cr(OH)}_3] \approx 6.3 \times 10^{-31}$$
    Thus, $Q_{\text{sp}} = [\text{M}^{3+}][\text{OH}^-]^3 > K_{\text{sp}}$, and Group III cations precipitate completely.
  - In contrast, Group IV and V hydroxides have much higher solubility products (e.g., $K_{\text{sp}}[\text{Zn(OH)}_2] \approx 3 \times 10^{-17}$, $K_{\text{sp}}[\text{Mg(OH)}_2] \approx 1.8 \times 10^{-11}$). Suppressed $[\text{OH}^-]$ cannot exceed their $K_{\text{sp}}$, preventing their premature precipitation in Group III.
- **Why Boil with Concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ Before Group III?**
  In the original solution, iron may be present in the ferrous state ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$). The solubility product of $\text{Fe(OH)}_2$ is relatively large ($K_{\text{sp}} \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-14}$), so $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ **will NOT precipitate** with the suppressed $[\text{OH}^-]$ of $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{NH}_4\text{OH}$.
  Boiling with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ quantitatively oxidizes $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ to $\text{Fe}^{3+}$:
  $$\mathbf{6\text{Fe}^{2+} + 2\text{HNO}_3 + 6\text{H}^+ \xrightarrow{\Delta} 6\text{Fe}^{3+} + 2\text{NO}\uparrow + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
  Because $\text{Fe(OH)}_3$ has an ultra-low $K_{\text{sp}}$ ($3.8 \times 10^{-38}$), iron precipitates quantitatively as reddish-brown ferric hydroxide.

---

#### 1.3.5 Why $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ Does Not Precipitate in Group V
- Group V reagent is $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{CO}_3$ in the presence of $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ and $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$.
- The carbonate ion concentration $[\text{CO}_3^{2-}]$ is kept under strict control by the buffer:
  $$\text{NH}_4^+ + \text{CO}_3^{2-} \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_3 + \text{HCO}_3^-$$
- This suppressed $[\text{CO}_3^{2-}]$ is sufficient to exceed the low $K_{\text{sp}}$ of Group V carbonates ($K_{\text{sp}}[\text{BaCO}_3] \approx 5 \times 10^{-9}, K_{\text{sp}}[\text{SrCO}_3] \approx 1 \times 10^{-10}, K_{\text{sp}}[\text{CaCO}_3] \approx 4.8 \times 10^{-9}$), but is **insufficient to exceed the significantly higher $K_{\text{sp}}$ of magnesium carbonate** ($K_{\text{sp}}[\text{MgCO}_3] \approx 3.5 \times 10^{-5}$).
- Hence, $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ remains soluble and passes quantitatively into the Group VI filtrate.

### 2.1 Principle & Gaseous Evolution Tests
Because all ammonium salts are highly soluble in water, $\text{NH}_4^+$ cannot be precipitated by any common group reagent and is analyzed in the **original salt mixture** (zero-group analysis):
1. **Action of Strong Bases ($\text{NaOH}$ or $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$):**
   When heated with alkali, ammonia gas is evolved:
   $$\mathbf{\text{NH}_4^+ + \text{OH}^- \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NH}_3(g)\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   - Gas possesses a characteristic suffocating, pungent ammoniacal odor.
   - Turns moist red litmus paper blue.
   - Turns moist turmeric paper brown.
   - A glass rod dipped in concentrated $\text{HCl}$ brought near the mouth of the test tube produces **dense white fumes of ammonium chloride**:
     $$\text{NH}_3(g) + \text{HCl}(g) \to \mathbf{\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}(s)\quad (\text{Dense White Fumes})}$$
   - Blackens paper moistened with mercurous nitrate ($\text{Hg}_2(\text{NO}_3)_2$) due to the separation of finely divided metallic mercury:
     $$2\text{Hg}_2(\text{NO}_3)_2 + 4\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \mathbf{2\text{Hg}\downarrow (\text{Black})} + \text{Hg}_2\text{O} \cdot \text{Hg(NH}_2)\text{NO}_3\downarrow + 3\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3$$

### 2.2 Confirmatory Reaction: Nessler's Reagent Test

*Description: Two-panel inorganic coordination and qualitative confirmatory graphic: (A) Groups IV & V confirmatory tests and chelation mechanisms detailing the square-planar intramolecularly hydrogen-bonded rosy-red $\text{Ni(DMG)}_2$ complex, Vogel's royal blue thiocyanate test for cobalt $[\text{Co(SCN)}_4]^{2-}$, Rinmann's green test for zinc ($\text{CoO} \cdot \text{ZnO}$), permanganic acid oxidation of manganese ($\text{HMnO}_4$), and the acetic acid separation cascade of Group V carbonates ($\text{BaCrO}_4, \text{SrSO}_4, \text{CaC}_2\text{O}_4$); (B) Group VI ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$) magneson lake and ammonium phosphate precipitation, alongside Group 0 ($\text{NH}_4^+$) chemistry focusing on Nessler's reagent synthesis and the structure of the brown iodide of Millon's base.*

- **Preparation of Nessler's Reagent:**
  Mercuric chloride solution is treated dropwise with $\text{KI}$ solution until the initial scarlet-red precipitate of $\text{HgI}_2$ completely dissolves in excess $\text{KI}$, followed by adding concentrated $\text{KOH}$ or $\text{NaOH}$:
  $$\text{HgCl}_2 + 2\text{KI} \to \text{HgI}_2\downarrow (\text{Scarlet Red}) + 2\text{KCl}$$
  $$\text{HgI}_2 + 2\text{KI}(\text{excess}) \to \mathbf{\text{K}_2[\text{HgI}_4]\quad (\text{Potassium tetraiodomercurate(II)})}$$
  $$\text{Nessler's Reagent} = \mathbf{\text{K}_2[\text{HgI}_4] + \text{KOH} \text{ (Alkaline Medium)}}$$
- **Confirmatory Reaction with $\text{NH}_3$ / $\text{NH}_4^+$:**
  Passing ammonia gas or adding an ammonium salt solution to Nessler's reagent produces an instantaneous **brown precipitate** (or yellow-brown coloration for trace amounts) known as the **Iodide of Millon's Base**:
  $$\mathbf{2\text{K}_2[\text{HgI}_4] + \text{NH}_3 + 3\text{KOH} \to \text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{Hg}-\text{O}-\text{Hg}-\text{I}\downarrow (\text{Brown Ppt}) + 7\text{KI} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
  - Formula of Iodide of Millon's Base: $\mathbf{\text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{Hg}-\text{O}-\text{Hg}-\text{I}}$ or $[\text{Hg}_2\text{N}]\text{I} \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O}$.

### 2.3 Sodium Cobaltinitrite Test
Adding freshly prepared sodium hexanitrocobaltate(III) ($\text{Na}_3[\text{Co(NO}_2)_6]$) to a neutral or slightly acidic solution of an ammonium salt produces a **canary-yellow crystalline precipitate**:
$$\mathbf{3\text{NH}_4^+ + [\text{Co(NO}_2)_6]^{3-} \to (\text{NH}_4)_3[\text{Co(NO}_2)_6]\downarrow \quad (\text{Yellow Precipitate})}$$
*(Note: $\text{K}^+$ ions also give an identical yellow precipitate of $\text{K}_3[\text{Co(NO}_2)_6]$; hence $\text{K}^+$ must be absent).*

### 3.1 Initial Precipitation & Separation Strategy
Addition of cold dilute $\text{HCl}$ precipitates:
$$\text{Ag}^+ + \text{Cl}^- \to \mathbf{\text{AgCl}\downarrow\ (\text{Curdy White})}$$
$$\text{Pb}^{2+} + 2\text{Cl}^- \to \mathbf{\text{PbCl}_2\downarrow\ (\text{White Crystalline})}$$
$$\text{Hg}_2^{2+} + 2\text{Cl}^- \to \mathbf{\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2\downarrow\ (\text{Calomel, White})}$$
- **Separation of $\text{PbCl}_2$:** The precipitate mixture is boiled with distilled water. **$\text{PbCl}_2$ dissolves completely in hot water**, whereas $\text{AgCl}$ and $\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2$ remain completely insoluble.
- The hot suspension is filtered immediately:
  - **Filtrate:** Contains $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ ions (confirm with $\text{K}_2\text{CrO}_4, \text{KI},$ and $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$).
  - **Residue:** Contains $\text{AgCl}$ and $\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2$ (separated using aqueous ammonia).

### 3.2 Confirmatory Chemistry for Lead ($\text{Pb}^{2+}$)

2. **Recrystallization on Cooling:**
   As the hot $\text{PbCl}_2$ solution cools, lead chloride precipitates out as **brilliant, silky white needles**.
3. **Potassium Iodide Test — "The Golden Rain Reaction":**
   Adding $\text{KI}$ solution to the lead filtrate yields a bright yellow precipitate of lead iodide:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Pb}^{2+} + 2\text{I}^- \to \text{PbI}_2\downarrow \quad (\text{Yellow Precipitate})}$$

Upon slow, quiescent cooling, it crystallizes into **magnificent, sparkling golden spangles/hexagonal plates** resembling golden rain!
4. **Potassium Chromate Test:**
   Adding $\text{K}_2\text{CrO}_4$ solution produces a **bright yellow precipitate of lead chromate**:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Pb}^{2+} + \text{CrO}_4^{2-} \to \text{PbCrO}_4\downarrow \quad (\text{Chrome Yellow})}$$

     Lead chromate is insoluble in acetic acid, but dissolves in excess sodium hydroxide to form a clear yellow solution of sodium tetrahydroxoplumbate(II):
     $$\text{PbCrO}_4 + 4\text{NaOH} \rightleftharpoons \mathbf{\text{Na}_2[\text{Pb(OH)}_4] + \text{Na}_2\text{CrO}_4}$$

5. **Sulfate Test:**
   Adding dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ gives a white precipitate of $\text{PbSO}_4$:
   $$\text{Pb}^{2+} + \text{SO}_4^{2-} \to \mathbf{\text{PbSO}_4\downarrow\ (\text{White})}$$
   - $\text{PbSO}_4$ dissolves in ammonium acetate solution due to the formation of weakly ionized lead acetate complex:
     $$\text{PbSO}_4 + 2\text{CH}_3\text{COONH}_4 \to \text{Pb(CH}_3\text{COO})_2 + (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$$

### 3.3 Separation of Silver ($\text{Ag}^+$) & Mercurous ($\text{Hg}_2^{2+}$)
Adding warm aqueous ammonia ($\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$) to the residue containing $\text{AgCl}$ and $\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2$:
- **Action on Silver Chloride (Dissolution):**
  $\text{AgCl}$ dissolves completely by forming a stable, water-soluble coordination complex, **diamminesilver(I) chloride**:
  $$\mathbf{\text{AgCl}(s) + 2\text{NH}_3(aq) \rightleftharpoons [\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]^+ + \text{Cl}^-}$$
  - Confirmatory Acidification: When the clear ammoniacal filtrate is acidified with dilute $\text{HNO}_3$, the ammine complex is destroyed by neutralizing $\text{NH}_3$, and **curdy white $\text{AgCl}$ reprecipitates**:
    $$[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]^+ + \text{Cl}^- + 2\text{H}^+ \to \mathbf{\text{AgCl}\downarrow (\text{Curdy White})} + 2\text{NH}_4^+$$
  - Confirmatory Chromate Test: Treating the ammoniacal solution with neutral $\text{K}_2\text{CrO}_4$ and acetic acid precipitates **brick-red silver chromate**:
    $$2\text{Ag}^+ + \text{CrO}_4^{2-} \to \mathbf{\text{Ag}_2\text{CrO}_4\downarrow \quad (\text{Brick Red})}$$
- **Action on Mercurous Chloride (Disproportionation Blackening):**
  When ammonia is added to white calomel ($\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2$), the solid turns **jet black** due to redox auto-disproportionation:
  $$\mathbf{\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2(s) + 2\text{NH}_3(aq) \to \underbrace{\text{Hg}(l)}_{\text{Black Metallic}} + \underbrace{\text{Hg(NH}_2)\text{Cl}(s)}_{\text{White}} + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}(aq)}$$
  - The resulting intimate mixture of finely divided metallic mercury ($\text{Hg}$) and insoluble mercuric amidochloride ($\text{Hg(NH}_2)\text{Cl}$) appears intensely black.
  - Dissolution in Aqua Regia: The black precipitate dissolves in boiling aqua regia:
    $$2\text{Hg} + 2\text{HNO}_3 + 6\text{HCl} \to 2\text{HgCl}_2 + 2\text{NO}\uparrow + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
    $$\text{Hg(NH}_2)\text{Cl} + \text{HNO}_3 + 3\text{HCl} \to \text{HgCl}_2 + \text{N}_2\uparrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2\text{HCl}$$
  - Confirmation with Stannous Chloride ($\text{SnCl}_2$): Adding $\text{SnCl}_2$ dropwise to the resulting $\text{HgCl}_2$ solution yields a **silky white precipitate of $\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2$**, which turns **ash-grey/black** with excess $\text{SnCl}_2$ due to reduction to metallic mercury:
    $$2\text{HgCl}_2 + \text{SnCl}_2 \to \mathbf{\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2\downarrow (\text{Silky White})} + \text{SnCl}_4$$
    $$\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2 + \text{SnCl}_2 \to \mathbf{2\text{Hg}\downarrow (\text{Grey/Black})} + \text{SnCl}_4$$

### 4.1 Separation of Subgroups IIA and IIB using Yellow Ammonium Sulfide (YAS)
The Group II sulfide precipitate is treated with warm **Yellow Ammonium Sulfide** (YAS, $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{S}_x$, containing polysulfide ions $\text{S}_2^{2-}$):
- **Group IIA (Insoluble Residue):** $\text{HgS}, \text{PbS}, \text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3, \text{CuS}, \text{CdS}$ do NOT form soluble thiosalts and remain as an insoluble residue.
- **Group IIB (Soluble Thiosalts):** $\text{As}_2\text{S}_3, \text{Sb}_2\text{S}_3, \text{SnS}, \text{SnS}_2$ dissolve completely via oxidation and thiosalt complexation:
  $$\text{As}_2\text{S}_3 + 3(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{S}_2 \to \mathbf{2(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{AsS}_4\quad (\text{Ammonium thioarsenate})} + \text{S}$$
  $$\text{Sb}_2\text{S}_3 + 3(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{S}_2 \to \mathbf{2(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{SbS}_4\quad (\text{Ammonium thioantimonate})} + \text{S}$$
  $$\text{SnS} + (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{S}_2 \to \mathbf{(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SnS}_3\quad (\text{Ammonium thiostannate})}$$
  *(Crucial Note: $\text{SnS}$ is insoluble in colorless ammonium sulfide $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{S}$; it requires the oxidizing yellow polysulfide $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{S}_2$ to oxidize $\text{Sn(II)} \to \text{Sn(IV)}$ to form soluble thiostannate).*
- **Reprecipitation of Group IIB:** When the yellow filtrate is acidified with dilute $\text{HCl}$, the thiosalts decompose, reprecipitating the higher sulfides:
  $$2(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{AsS}_4 + 6\text{HCl} \to \mathbf{\text{As}_2\text{S}_5\downarrow (\text{Yellow})} + 6\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{S}\uparrow$$
  $$2(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{SbS}_4 + 6\text{HCl} \to \mathbf{\text{Sb}_2\text{S}_5\downarrow (\text{Orange})} + 6\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{S}\uparrow$$
  $$(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SnS}_3 + 2\text{HCl} \to \mathbf{\text{SnS}_2\downarrow (\text{Yellow})} + 2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{H}_2\text{S}\uparrow$$

### 4.2 Group IIA Systematic Analysis & Confirmatory Chemistry
The insoluble IIA residue ($\text{HgS, PbS, Bi}_2\text{S}_3, \text{CuS, CdS}$) is boiled with **dilute nitric acid ($50\%\ \text{HNO}_3$, $1:1$ ratio)**:
- **$\text{HgS}$ is completely insoluble in dilute $\text{HNO}_3$** due to its exceptionally low solubility product ($K_{\text{sp}} \approx 10^{-53}$). It remains as a black residue.
- $\text{PbS, Bi}_2\text{S}_3, \text{CuS,}$ and $\text{CdS}$ dissolve in dilute $\text{HNO}_3$, evolving $\text{NO}$ gas:
  $$3\text{CuS} + 8\text{HNO}_3 \to 3\text{Cu(NO}_3)_2 + 3\text{S}\downarrow + 2\text{NO}\uparrow + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

#### 4.2.1 Mercury(II) ($\text{Hg}^{2+}$) Confirmation
- The black $\text{HgS}$ residue dissolves in fresh **aqua regia** ($3\text{ HCl} : 1\text{ HNO}_3$):
  $$3\text{HgS} + 2\text{HNO}_3 + 6\text{HCl} \to 3\text{HgCl}_2 + 3\text{S}\downarrow + 2\text{NO}\uparrow + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
- Dilute with water, boil off excess chlorine, and test:
  - **$\text{SnCl}_2$ Test:** White silky $\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2 \to$ grey/black metallic $\text{Hg}$.
  - **Copper Coin Test:** A clean copper strip immersed in $\text{HgCl}_2$ solution becomes coated with a silvery-white film of mercury amalgam: $\text{HgCl}_2 + \text{Cu} \to \text{CuCl}_2 + \text{Hg}$ (shines like silver when rubbed).
  - **Potassium Iodide Test:** Adding $\text{KI}$ dropwise yields a **scarlet-red precipitate of $\text{HgI}_2$**, which dissolves in excess $\text{KI}$ to form colorless Nessler's complex $[\text{HgI}_4]^{2-}$:
    $$\text{Hg}^{2+} + 2\text{I}^- \to \mathbf{\text{HgI}_2\downarrow \quad (\text{Scarlet Red})}$$
    $$\text{HgI}_2 + 2\text{I}^-(\text{excess}) \to \mathbf{[\text{HgI}_4]^{2-}\quad (\text{Colorless Soluble Complex})}$$

#### 4.2.2 Separation of Lead ($\text{Pb}^{2+}$)
- To the nitric acid solution, add dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ and alcohol. $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ precipitates as white $\text{PbSO}_4$, which is filtered off and confirmed as described in Group I.

#### 4.2.3 Bismuth ($\text{Bi}^{3+}$) Confirmation
- To the filtrate containing $\text{Bi}^{3+}, \text{Cu}^{2+}, \text{Cd}^{2+}$, add excess aqueous ammonia ($\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$):
  - **$\text{Bi}^{3+}$ precipitates as white gelatinous $\text{Bi(OH)}_3$**.
  - $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ and $\text{Cd}^{2+}$ dissolve as soluble tetraammine complexes.
- **Sodium Stannite Reduction:** Filter the white $\text{Bi(OH)}_3$ precipitate and pour freshly prepared sodium stannite solution over it. Instantaneous reduction yields **jet-black metallic bismuth**:
  $$\text{SnCl}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{Sn(OH)}_2\downarrow + 2\text{NaCl}$$
  $$\text{Sn(OH)}_2 + 2\text{NaOH}(\text{excess}) \to \mathbf{\text{Na}_2\text{SnO}_2\quad (\text{Sodium Stannite})} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  $$\mathbf{2\text{Bi(OH)}_3(s) + 3\text{Na}_2\text{SnO}_2(aq) \to 2\text{Bi}(s)\downarrow (\text{Jet Black}) + 3\text{Na}_2\text{SnO}_3 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
- **Water Hydrolysis Test:** Dissolving $\text{Bi(OH)}_3$ in minimum dilute $\text{HCl}$ yields $\text{BiCl}_3$. Pouring this into a large excess of water causes extensive hydrolysis, precipitating **white milky bismuth oxychloride**:
  $$\mathbf{\text{BiCl}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{BiOCl}\downarrow (\text{Bismuthyl Chloride / Pearl White}) + 2\text{HCl}}$$

#### 4.2.4 Separation of Copper ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$) & Cadmium ($\text{Cd}^{2+}$)
The ammoniacal filtrate contains $[\text{Cu(NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$ (intense azure blue) and $[\text{Cd(NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$ (colorless):
6. **Copper Confirmation via Ferrocyanide:**
   Acidify a portion with acetic acid and add potassium ferrocyanide ($\text{K}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]$). An intense **chocolate-brown precipitate of cupric ferrocyanide** forms:
   $$\mathbf{2\text{Cu}^{2+} + [\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-} \to \text{Cu}_2[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]\downarrow \quad (\text{Chocolate Brown})}$$
7. **Cyanide Complexation Separation Method:**
   To the blue ammoniacal solution, add potassium cyanide ($\text{KCN}$) dropwise until the blue color completely discharges:
   $$2[\text{Cu(NH}_3)_4]^{2+} + 10\text{CN}^- \to 2[\text{Cu(CN)}_4]^{3-} + (\text{CN})_2\uparrow (\text{Cyanogen Gas}) + 8\text{NH}_3$$
   $$[\text{Cd(NH}_3)_4]^{2+} + 4\text{CN}^- \to [\text{Cd(CN)}_4]^{2-} + 4\text{NH}_3$$
   - In $[\text{Cu(CN)}_4]^{3-}$, copper has been reduced to $\text{Cu(I)}$; this tetracyanocuprate(I) complex has an astronomical formation constant ($K_f \approx 10^{28}$) and furnishes virtually zero free $\text{Cu}^+$ ions.
   - In $[\text{Cd(CN)}_4]^{2-}$, cadmium remains $\text{Cd(II)}$; this complex is significantly less stable ($K_f \approx 10^{17}$) and maintains an appreciable equilibrium concentration of $\text{Cd}^{2+}$.
   - **Passing $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ Gas:**
     - $[\text{Cu(CN)}_4]^{3-}$ gives **NO PRECIPITATE**.
     - $[\text{Cd(CN)}_4]^{2-}$ decomposes, precipitating a **brilliant canary-yellow precipitate of Cadmium Sulfide ($\text{CdS}$)**:
       $$\mathbf{[\text{Cd(CN)}_4]^{2-} + \text{H}_2\text{S} \to \text{CdS}\downarrow (\text{Canary Yellow}) + 2\text{H}^+ + 4\text{CN}^-}$$

### 5.1 Separation of Group III Hydroxides
The Group III hydroxide precipitate ($\text{Fe(OH)}_3$ reddish-brown, $\text{Al(OH)}_3$ gelatinous white, $\text{Cr(OH)}_3$ green) is treated with **excess $\text{NaOH}$ and $3\%\ \text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ (or sodium peroxide $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$)**, followed by boiling:
- **$\text{Fe(OH)}_3$ is basic:** It does NOT dissolve in $\text{NaOH}$ and remains as a reddish-brown residue.
- **$\text{Al(OH)}_3$ is amphoteric:** It dissolves to form colorless soluble sodium tetrahydroxoaluminate:
  $$\text{Al(OH)}_3(s) + \text{NaOH}(aq) \to \mathbf{\text{Na}[\text{Al(OH)}_4](aq)\quad (\text{Colorless Solution})}$$
- **$\text{Cr(OH)}_3$ is amphoteric and oxidizable:** It oxidizes from $\text{Cr(III)}$ to $\text{Cr(VI)}$, dissolving to form **bright yellow sodium chromate**:
  $$\mathbf{2\text{Cr(OH)}_3(s) + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \to 2\text{Na}_2\text{CrO}_4(aq) (\text{Bright Yellow}) + 8\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$

### 5.2 Iron ($\text{Fe}^{3+}$) Confirmatory Chemistry
Dissolve the reddish-brown $\text{Fe(OH)}_3$ residue in dilute $\text{HCl}$ to obtain yellow $\text{FeCl}_3$:
8. **Prussian Blue Reaction:**
   Adding potassium ferrocyanide ($\text{K}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]$) produces an intense, deep **Prussian Blue precipitate/colloid of ferric ferrocyanide**:
   $$\mathbf{4\text{Fe}^{3+} + 3[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-} \to \text{Fe}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]_3\downarrow \quad (\text{Prussian Blue})}$$
   - Excess alkali decomposes Prussian blue:
     $$\text{Fe}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]_3 + 12\text{NaOH} \to 4\text{Fe(OH)}_3\downarrow (\text{Reddish-Brown}) + 3\text{Na}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]$$
9. **Potassium Thiocyanate (KCNS) Reaction:**
   Adding $\text{KCNS}$ or $\text{NH}_4\text{SCN}$ produces an intense **blood-red coloration** due to pentaaquathiocyanatoiron(III) complex:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{SCN}^- + 5\text{H}_2\text{O} \to [\text{Fe(SCN)}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_5]^{2+}\quad (\text{Blood Red Coloration})}$$
   - The blood-red color is discharged by adding sodium fluoride ($\text{NaF}$) or mercuric chloride ($\text{HgCl}_2$) due to the formation of colorless, highly stable $[\text{FeF}_6]^{3-}$ or un-ionized $\text{Hg(SCN)}_2$.
10. **Turnbull's Blue vs. Prussian Blue Distinction:**
   - $\text{Fe}^{3+} + [\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-} \to \text{Fe}_4[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]_3$ (Prussian Blue).
   - $\text{Fe}^{2+} + [\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{3-} \to \text{Fe}_3[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]_2$ (Turnbull's Blue).
   - Both compounds are now known to be structurally identical coordination polymers containing mixed-valence $\text{Fe(II)}-\text{CN}-\text{Fe(III)}$ networks!

### 5.3 Aluminium ($\text{Al}^{3+}$) Confirmatory Chemistry
11. **Reprecipitation via Ammonium Chloride:**
   Boiling the colorless sodium aluminate filtrate with solid $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ neutralizes the caustic alkali and **reprecipitates gelatinous white $\text{Al(OH)}_3$**:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Na}[\text{Al(OH)}_4] + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Al(OH)}_3\downarrow (\text{Gelatinous White}) + \text{NaCl} + \text{NH}_3\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
12. **The Blue Lake Test:**
   - Dissolve the white $\text{Al(OH)}_3$ precipitate in dilute $\text{HCl}$.
   - Add 1-2 drops of blue litmus solution (which turns red in the acidic solution).
   - Add $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ dropwise down the side of the test tube.
   - A **brilliant blue floating precipitate / lake** forms suspended in the upper alkaline zone with a clear liquid beneath.
   - Mechanism: Freshly precipitated colloidal $\text{Al(OH)}_3$ has an enormous surface area that strongly adsorbs the blue litmus dye anion!
13. **Cobalt Nitrate Test (Thenard's Blue):**
   Heating $\text{Al(OH)}_3$ with a drop of cobalt nitrate ($\text{Co(NO}_3)_2$) on a charcoal block gives an intensely blue, non-fusible mass of cobalt meta-aluminate:
   $$2\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 2\text{Co(NO}_3)_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathbf{2(\text{CoO} \cdot \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3)\quad (\text{Thenard's Blue})} + 4\text{NO}_2\uparrow + \text{O}_2\uparrow$$

### 5.4 Chromium ($\text{Cr}^{3+}$) Confirmatory Chemistry
14. **Lead Chromate Test:**
   Acidify the yellow sodium chromate filtrate with acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$) and add lead acetate solution. A **bright yellow precipitate of lead chromate** forms:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Pb}^{2+} + \text{CrO}_4^{2-} \to \text{PbCrO}_4\downarrow \quad (\text{Chrome Yellow})}$$
15. **Chromium Peroxide Butterfly Test ($\text{CrO}_5$):**
   - Acidify the chromate solution with dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.
   - Add $1-2\text{ mL}$ of amyl alcohol or diethyl ether.
   - Add $3\%\ \text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ and shake gently.
   - The organic layer extracts a magnificent, **intense royal-blue compound: Chromium Peroxide ($\text{CrO}_5$)**:
     $$\mathbf{\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \to 2\text{CrO}_5 + 5\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   - **Molecular Structure:** $\text{CrO}_5$ possesses a **butterfly structure** containing one oxo oxygen ($=\text{O}$) and two peroxo bridges ($-\text{O}-\text{O}-$). The formal oxidation state of chromium is **$+6$**:
     $$\text{Cr}(=\text{O})(\text{O}_2)_2$$
   - In water, $\text{CrO}_5$ rapidly decomposes to green $\text{Cr}^{3+}$ and $\text{O}_2$, but is stabilized in coordinating donor solvents (ether, pyridine, amyl alcohol).

### 6.1 Nickel ($\text{Ni}^{2+}$) Confirmatory Chemistry
Dissolve the black $\text{NiS}$ precipitate in aqua regia, neutralize with $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$, and perform:
- **The Dimethylglyoxime (DMG) Test:**
  Adding an ethanolic solution of dimethylglyoxime (DMG) in the presence of excess $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ produces a voluminous, **brilliant scarlet / rosy-red precipitate**:
  $$\mathbf{\text{Ni}^{2+} + 2\text{DMG} + 2\text{NH}_4\text{OH} \to [\text{Ni(DMG)}_2]\downarrow (\text{Rosy Red}) + 2\text{NH}_4^+ + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
- **Structural Highlights of $[\text{Ni(DMG)}_2]$:**
  - **Geometry:** Square planar, $dsp^2$ hybridized, **diamagnetic** ($d^8$ low spin configuration induced by strong chelation).
  - **Hydrogen Bonding:** Stabilized by **two symmetrical intramolecular $\text{O}-\text{H}\cdots \text{O}$ hydrogen bonds**, creating four fused chelate rings (two 5-membered rings coordinating $\text{Ni}$ to nitrogens, and two 6-membered hydrogen-bonded rings).
  - The complex is neutral, hydrophobic, and exceptionally insoluble in aqueous ammonia.

### 6.2 Cobalt ($\text{Co}^{2+}$) Confirmatory Chemistry
Dissolve the black $\text{CoS}$ precipitate in aqua regia and test:
16. **Vogel's Thiocyanate Test:**
   Add ammonium thiocyanate ($\text{NH}_4\text{SCN}$) and $1\text{ mL}$ of amyl alcohol (or acetone / ether) and shake. An **intense royal-blue color** appears in the organic layer:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Co}^{2+} + 4\text{SCN}^- \rightleftharpoons [\text{Co(SCN)}_4]^{2-}\quad (\text{Tetrathiocyanatocobaltate(II), Royal Blue})}$$
   - The tetrahedral complex $[\text{Co(SCN)}_4]^{2-}$ is unstable in water (pink due to $[\text{Co(H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$), but is stabilized and extracted quantitatively into less polar oxygenated organic solvents.
17. **Fischer's Salt Reaction (Potassium Hexanitrocobaltate(III)):**
   Add potassium nitrite ($\text{KNO}_2$) in excess after acidifying with acetic acid. An intense **canary-yellow crystalline precipitate of Fischer's Salt** forms:
   $$\text{Co}^{2+} + \text{NO}_2^- + 2\text{H}^+ \to \text{Co}^{3+} + \text{NO}\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
   $$\mathbf{\text{Co}^{3+} + 3\text{K}^+ + 6\text{NO}_2^- \to \text{K}_3[\text{Co(NO}_2)_6]\downarrow \quad (\text{Fischer's Salt, Yellow Crystalline})}$$

### 6.3 Zinc ($\text{Zn}^{2+}$) Confirmatory Chemistry
Dissolve the dirty-white $\text{ZnS}$ precipitate in dilute $\text{HCl}$:
18. **Action of Sodium Hydroxide:**
   Adding $\text{NaOH}$ gives a white gelatinous precipitate of $\text{Zn(OH)}_2$, which dissolves completely in excess $\text{NaOH}$ to yield colorless sodium zincate:
   $$\text{Zn}^{2+} + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{Zn(OH)}_2\downarrow + 2\text{Na}^+$$
   $$\text{Zn(OH)}_2 + 2\text{NaOH}(\text{excess}) \to \mathbf{\text{Na}_2[\text{Zn(OH)}_4]\quad (\text{Sodium Zincate})}$$
   - Passing $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ into this alkaline zincate solution reprecipitates white $\text{ZnS}$.
19. **Rinmann's Green Test:**
   Moisten the zinc salt on a charcoal block with a dilute drop of cobalt nitrate ($\text{Co(NO}_3)_2$) and heat strongly in an oxidizing blowpipe flame:
   $$\text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{Co(NO}_3)_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathbf{\text{CoO} \cdot \text{ZnO}\quad (\text{Rinmann's Green / Cobalt Zincate})} + \text{SO}_3 + 2\text{NO}_2 + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2$$

### 6.4 Manganese ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$) Confirmatory Chemistry
Dissolve the flesh/buff-colored $\text{MnS}$ precipitate in dilute $\text{HCl}$ and boil off $\text{H}_2\text{S}$:
20. **Oxidation to Permanganic Acid ($\text{PbO}_2$ Test):**
   Boil the manganese solution with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ and a pinch of solid lead dioxide ($\text{PbO}_2$, or sodium bismuthate $\text{NaBiO}_3$). Allow the excess brown $\text{PbO}_2$ to settle:
   $$\mathbf{2\text{Mn}^{2+} + 5\text{PbO}_2(s) + 4\text{H}^+ \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{HMnO}_4(aq) (\text{Intense Purple / Pink}) + 5\text{Pb}^{2+} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   - The supernatant turns a characteristic **vibrant pink/purple color** due to permanganic acid ($\text{MnO}_4^-$).
   - Note: $\text{HCl}$ must be completely absent because chloride ions reduce $\text{MnO}_4^-$ to $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ while oxidizing to $\text{Cl}_2$.

### 7.1 Separation Cascade in Acetic Acid Medium
The white carbonate precipitate ($\text{BaCO}_3, \text{SrCO}_3, \text{CaCO}_3$) is dissolved in minimum hot dilute **acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$)**:
21. **Barium ($\text{Ba}^{2+}$) Precipitation:**
   Add potassium chromate ($\text{K}_2\text{CrO}_4$) solution. **$\text{BaCrO}_4$ precipitates as a pale yellow solid**, while $\text{Sr}^{2+}$ and $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ remain completely dissolved because strontium and calcium chromates are soluble in dilute acetic acid:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Ba}^{2+} + \text{CrO}_4^{2-} \to \text{BaCrO}_4\downarrow \quad (\text{Yellow Precipitate, Insoluble in Acetic Acid})}$$
22. **Strontium ($\text{Sr}^{2+}$) Precipitation:**
   Filter off $\text{BaCrO}_4$. Make the filtrate ammoniacal and add saturated ammonium sulfate ($(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$) solution and warm. **$\text{SrSO}_4$ precipitates as a white solid**, while $\text{CaSO}_4$ remains in solution:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Sr}^{2+} + \text{SO}_4^{2-} \to \text{SrSO}_4\downarrow \quad (\text{White Precipitate})}$$
23. **Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) Precipitation:**
   Filter off $\text{SrSO}_4$. To the filtrate, add ammonium oxalate ($(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{C}_2\text{O}_4$). **$\text{CaC}_2\text{O}_4$ precipitates as a fine white crystalline solid**:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Ca}^{2+} + \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} \to \text{CaC}_2\text{O}_4\downarrow \quad (\text{Calcium Oxalate, White Precipitate})}$$

#### Trap 1: Interference of Phosphate ($\text{PO}_4^{3-}$) and Interfering Acid Radicals
- Phosphates, borates, oxalates, and fluorides of alkaline earth metals and Group IV metals are insoluble in neutral or alkaline media, but soluble in acidic media.
- When $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ is added to precipitate Group III in the presence of $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$, the medium becomes alkaline, causing **premature precipitation of $\text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2, \text{Ba}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2, \text{Sr}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2, \text{Mg}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ alongside $\text{Fe(OH)}_3$ in Group III**!
- **Remedy (Phosphate Removal Scheme):**
  After Group II, boil off $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, test for phosphate with ammonium molybdate. If present, add $\text{FeCl}_3$ in the presence of sodium acetate buffer ($\text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$). Ferric phosphate ($\text{FePO}_4$) precipitates as an insoluble yellowish-white solid and is filtered off before proceeding to Group III!

#### Trap 2: Why Does $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ Appear in Both Group I and Group II?
- The solubility product of lead chloride ($\text{PbCl}_2$) is moderately high ($K_{\text{sp}} \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-5}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$).
- In Group I, cold dilute $\text{HCl}$ does not precipitate lead quantitatively; an appreciable concentration of $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ remains dissolved in the filtrate.
- When $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ is passed in Group II, because $K_{\text{sp}}[\text{PbS}] \approx 10^{-28}$ is vastly lower than $K_{\text{sp}}[\text{PbCl}_2]$, the residual lead precipitates quantitatively as black $\text{PbS}$.

#### Trap 3: Oxidation State & Disproportionation Traps in Mercury
- $\text{Hg}_2^{2+}$ (mercurous) contains a metal-metal bond with single unpaired electrons paired up: $^+\text{Hg}-\text{Hg}^+$ (diamagnetic).
- When treated with $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{Hg}_2^{2+}$ auto-disproportionates into elemental $\text{Hg}^0$ (black) and $\text{Hg}^{2+}$ (white $\text{Hg(NH}_2)\text{Cl}$).
- When treated with $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{Hg}_2^{2+}$ yields black $\text{HgS} + \text{Hg}$.
