1. Atmospheric Pollution: Troposphere & Stratosphere
![Environmental Chemistry Smog And Ozone Cycles](/media/environmental_chemistry_smog_and_ozone_cycles.webp) Description: Two-panel reference diagram for atmospheric chemistry: (Panel A) Classical vs. Photochemical Smog comparison matrix, detailing climate conditions, primary vs. secondary pollutants, reducing vs. oxidizing character, and the multi-step sunlight-driven radical mechanism forming ozone, acrolein, formaldehyde, and PAN; (Panel B) Stratospheric ozone dynamics, illustrating the Chapman natural equilibrium, CFC catalytic destruction cycle by chlorine radicals, summer chlorine sinks ($\text{ClONO}_2, \text{HCl}$), and Antarctic Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs) activating chlorine species to cause springtime ozone depletion.
1.1 Tropospheric Gaseous Pollutants
The atmosphere is divided into layers: the troposphere (lowest region, $0 - 10\ \text{km}$ from Earth's surface, containing $75\%$ of air mass) and the stratosphere ($10 - 50\ \text{km}$, containing the protective ozone layer).
1. Oxides of Sulphur ($\text{SO}_x$)
* Sources: Combustion of sulphur-containing fossil fuels (coal, petroleum), roasting of sulphide ores ($2\text{ZnS} + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{ZnO} + 2\text{SO}_2$).
* Catalytic Atmospheric Oxidation: In unpolluted air, oxidation of $\text{SO}_2$ to $\text{SO}_3$ is very slow. In polluted air, particulate matter and oxidizing trace gases dramatically accelerate oxidation: $$2\text{SO}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \xrightarrow{\text{Particulate Matter}} 2\text{SO}_3(g)$$ $$\text{SO}_2(g) + \text{O}_3(g) \rightarrow \text{SO}_3(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$ $$\text{SO}_2(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2(l) \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(aq)$$
* Toxicity & Physiological Effects:
   * Even low concentrations ($1 - 2\ \text{ppm}$) induce severe respiratory spasm, eye irritation, asthma, bronchitis, and emphysema.
   * In vegetation, high $\text{SO}_2$ causes stiffness of flower buds, which turn brown and drop prematurely off plants.
2. Oxides of Nitrogen ($\text{NO}_x$)
* Sources: High-temperature combustion in automobile engines and thermal power plants: $$\text{N}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \xrightarrow{1483\ \text{K}} 2\text{NO}(g)$$ $$2\text{NO}(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow 2\text{NO}_2(g) \quad (\text{slow})$$ $$\text{NO}(g) + \text{O}_3(g) \rightarrow \text{NO}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \quad (\text{fast})$$
* Toxicity:
   * $\text{NO}_2$ produces the characteristic irritant reddish-brown haze seen over congested traffic corridors.
   * High levels retard photosynthesis in plants and cause necrotic damage to leaves. It attacks rubber, textiles, and corrodes architectural metals.
3. Oxides of Carbon ($\text{CO}$ and $\text{CO}_2$)
* Carbon Monoxide ($\text{CO}$):
   * Originates from incomplete combustion of carbonaceous fuels, automobile exhausts, and cigarette smoke.
   * Asphyxiation Mechanism: Binds irreversibly to haemoglobin with an affinity $\sim 300$ times higher than oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin: $$\text{Hb} + \text{CO} \rightleftharpoons \text{CO-Hb}$$
   * When carboxyhaemoglobin levels reach $3\% - 4\%$, blood oxygen-carrying capacity drops critically, causing asphyxiation, dizziness, and cardiovascular failure.
   * In pregnant mothers, elevated $\text{CO}$ induces premature delivery, spontaneous abortion, and congenital deformities.
* Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$):
   * Natural atmospheric abundance is approximately $0.03\%$ by volume ($300 - 400\ \text{ppm}$).
   * Non-toxic at baseline, but excessive emissions from fossil fuel burning and deforestation drive the Enhanced Greenhouse Effect.

1.2 Global Warming & The Greenhouse Effect
Solar radiation reaches the Earth primarily as visible and ultraviolet light. The Earth's surface absorbs this energy and re-emits it as long-wavelength infrared (thermal) radiation.


* Greenhouse Mechanism: Infrared radiation is absorbed by greenhouse gases in the troposphere, trapping thermal energy and warming the planet:
   * Natural Greenhouse Effect maintains Earth's average surface temperature at approximately $+15^\circ\text{C}$. Without greenhouse gases, Earth's mean temperature would be $-18^\circ\text{C}$, rendering life impossible.
* Relative Contribution of Greenhouse Gases: $$\mathbf{\text{CO}_2 \approx 50\%}, \quad \mathbf{\text{CH}_4 \approx 19\%}, \quad \mathbf{\text{CFCs} \approx 14\%}, \quad \mathbf{\text{N}_2\text{O} \approx 4\%}, \quad \mathbf{\text{O}_3 \approx 8\%}, \quad \text{Water Vapour}$$
* Global Warming Potential (GWP): On a per-molecule basis, methane ($\text{CH}_4$) is $\sim 25$ times more effective at trapping heat than $\text{CO}_2$, and CFCs are thousands of times more potent.

1.3 Acid Rain Mechanics & Marble Decay
Unpolluted rainwater is naturally slightly acidic with a $\text{pH} \approx 5.6$ due to dissolution of atmospheric carbon dioxide forming carbonic acid: $$\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{CO}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3(aq) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+(aq) + \text{HCO}_3^-(aq)$$


* Definition of Acid Rain: Precipitation is classified as acid rain when its $\mathbf{\text{pH} < 5.6}$.
* Chemical Composition: Typically consists of $\sim 60\% - 70\%\ \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, $\sim 30\% - 35\%\ \text{HNO}_3$, and minor quantities of $\text{HCl}$. $$\text{SO}_3(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(aq)$$ $$2\text{NO}_2(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightarrow \text{HNO}_3(aq) + \text{HNO}_2(aq)$$
* Corrosion of Monuments ("Stone Leprosy" / Marble Cancer): Acid rain dissolves calcium carbonate in marble monuments (such as the Taj Mahal in Agra): $$\mathbf{\text{CaCO}_3(s) + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_4(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{CO}_2(g)}$$ The formed gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4$) crumbles, discoloring and pitting the marble surface.

1.4 Particulate Matter (Viable vs. Non-Viable)
Particulates are minute solid particles or liquid droplets suspended in air:


1. Viable Particulates: Living organisms ($0.2 - 100\ \mu\text{m}$) such as bacteria, fungi, moulds, and algae.
2. Non-Viable Particulates:
   * Smoke: Solid or mixed solid-liquid particles formed during combustion of organic matter ($0.01 - 1\ \mu\text{m}$, e.g., soot, oil smoke, cigarette smoke).
   * Dust: Fine solid particles formed by crushing, grinding, or mechanical abrasion ($> 1\ \mu\text{m}$, e.g., silica, asbestos, fly ash).
   * Mists: Liquid droplets produced by condensation of vapours or liquid spray ($1 - 10\ \mu\text{m}$, e.g., sulphuric acid mist, pesticide sprays).
   * Fumes: Condensed vapors formed by volatilization followed by oxidation or sublimation ($0.03 - 0.3\ \mu\text{m}$, e.g., zinc oxide fumes, metallurgical fumes).
* Particle Size & Human Health:
   * Particles $> 5\ \mu\text{m}$ are trapped by nasal cilia.
   * Particles $\sim 10\ \mu\text{m}$ enter the tracheobronchial tree.
   * Fine particles $\le 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$ ($\text{PM}_{2.5}$) penetrate deeply into pulmonary alveoli, causing irreversible fibrosis and pneumoconiosis.

1.5 Classical Smog vs. Photochemical Smog
Smog is a contraction of the words smoke and fog.


Characteristic
	Classical Smog (London Smog)
	Photochemical Smog (Los Angeles Smog)
	Climate & Conditions
	Cool, humid, foggy winter mornings
	Warm, dry, sunny afternoons
	Primary Sources
	Coal burning, industrial $\text{SO}_2$ emissions
	Automobile exhaust ($\text{NO}_x$ and unburnt hydrocarbons)
	Key Components
	Smoke, fog, $\text{SO}_2$, and $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ droplets
	Ozone ($\text{O}_3$), $\text{NO}_2$, PAN, Acrolein, Formaldehyde
	Chemical Nature
	REDUCING SMOG (due to $\text{SO}_2$)
	OXIDIZING SMOG (due to $\text{O}_3$, $\text{NO}_2$, PAN)
	Atmospheric Appearance
	Dark grey, opaque, sooty haze
	Brownish haze (red-brown color from $\text{NO}_2$)
	Health Hazards
	Bronchial spasm, acute respiratory failure
	Intense eye irritation, coughing, rubber cracking
	Mechanism of Photochemical Smog Formation:
1. Primary automobile emissions release nitric oxide ($\text{NO}$) and unburnt volatile organic compounds (VOCs).
2. Sunlight photolyzes nitrogen dioxide: $$\text{NO}_2(g) \xrightarrow{h\nu} \text{NO}(g) + \text{O}(g)$$
3. Highly reactive nascent oxygen atom combines with molecular oxygen: $$\text{O}(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{O}_3(g)$$
4. Ozone reacts with $\text{NO}$ to regenerate $\text{NO}_2$, establishing dynamic equilibrium: $$\text{NO}(g) + \text{O}_3(g) \rightarrow \text{NO}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$
5. However, ozone also oxidizes unburnt hydrocarbons into free radicals, producing toxic secondary pollutants:
   * Formaldehyde: $\text{HCHO}$
   * Acrolein: $\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{CHO}$ (powerful lachrymator)
   * Peroxyacetyl Nitrate (PAN): $\text{CH}_3\text{COOONO}_2$ (potent eye irritant and phytotoxin)

1.6 Stratospheric Ozone Layer & Antarctic Ozone Hole
The ozone layer in the stratosphere ($15 - 35\ \text{km}$) shields biological life from lethal solar UV-B radiation ($280 - 315\ \text{nm}$).
1. The Natural Chapman Cycle
$$\text{O}_2(g) \xrightarrow{\text{UV } (< 242\ \text{nm})} 2\text{O}(g)$$ $$\text{O}(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{O}_3(g)$$ $$\text{O}_3(g) \xrightarrow{\text{UV } (220 - 320\ \text{nm})} \text{O}_2(g) + \text{O}(g)$$
2. Catalytic Destruction by Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs / Freons)
CFCs like $\text{CF}_2\text{Cl}_2$ (Freon-12) are chemically inert in the troposphere, but drift into the stratosphere where high-energy UV radiation causes homolytic fission: $$\text{CF}_2\text{Cl}_2(g) \xrightarrow{h\nu} \dot{\text{C}}\text{F}_2\text{Cl}(g) + \dot{\text{C}}\text{l}(g)$$


The free chlorine radical acts as a catalyst in a self-sustaining ozone-destroying chain reaction: $$\dot{\text{C}}\text{l}(g) + \text{O}_3(g) \rightarrow \text{ClO}\dot{\text{}}(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$ $$\text{ClO}\dot{\text{}}(g) + \text{O}(g) \rightarrow \dot{\text{C}}\text{l}(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$


* Catalytic Efficiency: A single chlorine radical can catalytically destroy over $100,000$ ozone molecules before being scavenged.
3. Antarctic Ozone Hole & Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs)
In summer over Antarctica, chlorine radicals are sequestered into stable, inert chemical reservoirs: $$\text{ClO}\dot{\text{}}(g) + \text{NO}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{ClONO}_2(g) \quad (\text{Chlorine Nitrate})$$ $$\dot{\text{C}}\text{l}(g) + \text{CH}_4(g) \rightarrow \text{HCl}(g) + \dot{\text{C}}\text{H}_3(g)$$


During the polar winter, a circumpolar vortex seals off Antarctic air at temperatures below $-78^\circ\text{C}$, forming Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs):


* Type I PSC: Nitric acid trihydrate ($\text{HNO}_3 \cdot 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
* Type II PSC: Water ice crystals.


Heterogeneous chemical reactions on PSC ice surfaces release reactive chlorine: $$\text{ClONO}_2(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(s) \rightarrow \text{HOCl}(g) + \text{HNO}_3(s)$$ $$\text{ClONO}_2(g) + \text{HCl}(s) \rightarrow \text{Cl}_2(g) + \text{HNO}_3(s)$$


* Springtime Sunrise (September/October): The return of solar radiation photolyzes $\text{Cl}_2$ and $\text{HOCl}$: $$\text{Cl}_2(g) \xrightarrow{h\nu} 2\dot{\text{C}}\text{l}(g), \quad \text{HOCl}(g) \xrightarrow{h\nu} \dot{\text{O}}\text{H}(g) + \dot{\text{C}}\text{l}(g)$$ The sudden release of massive quantities of free $\dot{\text{C}}\text{l}$ radicals causes the seasonal catastrophic collapse in ozone concentration known as the Antarctic Ozone Hole.

2. Water Pollution, Quality Metrics & Green Chemistry
![Environmental Chemistry Water Quality And Green Principles](/media/environmental_chemistry_water_quality_and_green_principles.webp) Description: Two-panel reference diagram for aquatic metrics and green chemistry: (Panel A) Water quality parameters comparing Dissolved Oxygen (DO) levels, Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) pollution thresholds, the eutrophication cascade, and BIS/WHO drinking water maximum permissible concentrations ($\text{F}^-, \text{Pb}, \text{SO}_4^{2-}, \text{NO}_3^-, \text{Cd}, \text{Hg}$); (Panel B) Principles of Green Chemistry and industrial green innovations, including liquid $\text{CO}_2$ for dry cleaning, $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ for wood pulp bleaching, and aqueous catalytic oxidation of ethene to ethanal.
2.1 Water Quality Metrics: DO, BOD, COD & Eutrophication
1. Dissolved Oxygen (DO)
In clean, cold water, dissolved oxygen concentration reaches about $10\ \text{ppm}$ ($10\ \text{mg/L}$).


* If DO concentration drops below $6\ \text{ppm}$, fish growth and reproduction are inhibited.
* Severe organic pollution triggers aerobic bacterial respiration that consumes DO rapidly. If DO falls to zero, anaerobic bacteria proliferate, producing foul-smelling toxic gases ($\text{H}_2\text{S}, \text{CH}_4, \text{NH}_3$).
2. Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)
BOD is the amount of oxygen required by aerobic microorganisms to biochemically oxidize the organic matter present in a given volume of a water sample incubated for $5$ days at $20^\circ\text{C}$.


* Clean Drinking Water: $\mathbf{\text{BOD} < 5\ \text{ppm}}$.
* Highly Polluted Water (Untreated Sewage / Effluent): $\mathbf{\text{BOD} \ge 17\ \text{ppm}}$.
3. Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD)
COD is the quantity of oxygen consumed to chemically oxidize both biodegradable and non-biodegradable organic matter using strong chemical oxidants (potassium dichromate $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ in boiling dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$).


* Invariant: $\mathbf{\text{COD} > \text{BOD}}$ for any natural or waste water sample.
4. Eutrophication
The natural or accelerated nutrient enrichment of water bodies (primarily by phosphates from detergents and nitrates from fertilizers): $$\text{Fertilizer / Sewage Runoff} \rightarrow \text{Excess Phosphates & Nitrates} \rightarrow \text{Algal Bloom}$$ $$\text{Algal Bloom Blocks Sunlight} \rightarrow \text{Submerged Flora Dies} \rightarrow \text{Bacterial Decomposition}$$ $$\text{Total Depletion of Dissolved Oxygen} \rightarrow \text{Fish Suffocation & Loss of Biodiversity}$$

2.3 Principles of Green Chemistry & Industrial Replacements
Green Chemistry is the design of chemical products and processes that reduce or eliminate the generation and use of hazardous substances.
1. Core Principles of Green Chemistry (Anastas & Warner)
1. Waste Prevention: Better to prevent waste at source than to treat it after formation.
2. Atom Economy: Maximize incorporation of all raw materials into the final desired product: $$\mathbf{\text{Atom Economy} = \frac{\text{Formula Weight of Desired Product}}{\sum \text{Formula Weight of All Reactants}} \times 100\%}$$
3. Safer Solvents: Replace toxic volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and halogenated solvents with benign media (water, supercritical carbon dioxide).
4. Energy Efficiency: Conduct syntheses at ambient temperature and pressure.
5. Catalysis: Prefer selective heterogeneous/homogeneous catalysts over stoichiometric reagents.
2. High-Yield Everyday Industrial Green Chemistry Replacements
* Dry Cleaning of Garments:
   * Traditional Method: Tetrachloroethene ($\text{Cl}_2\text{C}=\text{CCl}_2$), a suspected human carcinogen that contaminates groundwater.
   * Green Alternative: Liquid $\text{CO}_2$ combined with a specialized surfactant, or hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$) for laundry bleaching.
* Bleaching of Paper Pulp:
   * Traditional Method: Chlorine gas ($\text{Cl}_2$), which produced persistent, bioaccumulative chlorinated dioxins.
   * Green Alternative: Hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$) in the presence of a metal activator catalyst.
* Industrial Synthesis of Ethanal (Acetaldehyde):
   * Green Reaction: Direct, single-step aqueous oxidation of ethene: $$\mathbf{\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd(II)/Cu(II) in } \text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{CH}_3\text{CHO}} \quad (\text{Wacker Process}, \sim 90\%\ \text{Yield})$$

3. High-Yield Problem Archetypes & JEE Traps
#
	Topic / Scenario
	Common Mistake / Trap
	Correct Chemical Principle
	1
	Acid Rain pH Threshold
	Setting acid rain threshold at $\text{pH} < 7.0$.
	Normal rainwater is naturally acidic ($\text{pH} \approx 5.6$) from dissolved $\text{CO}_2$. Rain is classified as acid rain only when $\text{pH} < 5.6$.
	2
	Classical vs Photochemical Smog Nature
	Believing Photochemical Smog is reducing.
	Classical smog contains $\text{SO}_2$ and is reducing; Photochemical smog contains $\text{O}_3, \text{NO}_2, \text{PAN}$ and is strongly oxidizing.
	3
	CO vs CO2 Greenhouse Potential
	Confusing $\text{CO}$ with greenhouse warming.
	$\text{CO}$ is a toxic asphyxiant but NOT a greenhouse gas! The major greenhouse gas is $\text{CO}_2$.
	4
	Stratospheric vs Tropospheric Ozone
	Treating all ozone as beneficial.
	Ozone in the stratosphere is a life-protecting shield against UV-B; ozone in the troposphere is a toxic, eye-irritating component of photochemical smog.
	5
	Antarctic Ozone Hole Timing
	Assuming ozone hole develops during the coldest dark winter.
	Winter cold builds up the reservoir and PSCs; the hole forms during early spring (September/October) when sunlight returns to trigger photolysis.
	6
	Blue Baby Syndrome Causative Ion
	Attributing Blue Baby syndrome to sulfate or fluoride.
	Caused specifically by excess nitrate ($\text{NO}_3^- > 50\ \text{ppm}$) in drinking water.
	7
	Dental Fluoride Thresholds
	Thinking fluoride is always harmful.
	Up to $1\ \text{ppm}$, $\text{F}^-$ protects teeth by forming fluorapatite; mottling occurs $> 2\ \text{ppm}$; bone damage occurs $> 10\ \text{ppm}$.
	8
	Carboxyhaemoglobin Stability Factor
	Stating $\text{CO}$ binds 10x or 1000x stronger than $\text{O}_2$.
	Carboxyhaemoglobin is $\sim 300$ times more stable than oxyhaemoglobin.
	9
	BOD vs Clean Water Threshold
	Thinking clean water has high BOD.
	High BOD indicates heavy organic pollution. Clean drinking water has $\text{BOD} < 5\ \text{ppm}$; dirty water has $\text{BOD} \ge 17\ \text{ppm}$.
	10
	Green Chemistry Dry Cleaning Reagent
	Claiming ethanol or benzene is the green solvent.
	The designated green solvent for dry cleaning is liquid $\text{CO}_2$ with suitable detergent.
