	1.2 Key Anomalies & Invariants in Group 1
1. Density Anomaly ($\text{K} < \text{Na}$):
   * Normal trend: Density increases down the group.
   * Anomaly: $\mathbf{\text{K} (0.86\text{ g/cm}^3) < \text{Na} (0.97\text{ g/cm}^3)}$.
   * Cause: The introduction of the $3d$ subshell in potassium causes an abnormal expansion in atomic volume that outweighs the increase in atomic mass.
2. Hydration Enthalpy vs. Ionic Mobility Inversion:
   * Degree of hydration depends inversely on ionic size: $$\mathbf{\text{Li}^+ > \text{Na}^+ > \text{K}^+ > \text{Rb}^+ > \text{Cs}^+} \quad (\text{Hydration Enthalpy & Hydrated Radius})$$
   * Because $\text{Li}^+$ carries a dense hydration sphere of water dipoles, its effective hydrated size is largest, dragging water molecules through solution.
   * Consequently, aqueous ionic mobility and electrical conductance are reversed: $$\mathbf{\text{Li}^+ < \text{Na}^+ < \text{K}^+ < \text{Rb}^+ < \text{Cs}^+} \quad (\text{Aqueous Conductance})$$
3. Reducing Power & Standard Electrode Potential ($E^\circ$):
   * Standard reduction potential $E^\circ$ is governed by the Born-Haber cycle for ionization and hydration: $$\text{M}(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta_{\text{sub}} H} \text{M}(g) \xrightarrow{\Delta_i H} \text{M}^+(g) \xrightarrow{\Delta_{\text{hyd}} H} \text{M}^+(aq)$$
   * Although $\text{Li}$ has the highest ionization energy among alkali metals, its exceptionally large exothermic hydration enthalpy ($\Delta_{\text{hyd}} H^\circ = -506\text{ kJ/mol}$) overcompensates, giving it the most negative reduction potential ($E^\circ = -3.04\text{ V}$).
   * Reducing power in aqueous solution: $\mathbf{\text{Li} > \text{K} \approx \text{Rb} \approx \text{Cs} > \text{Na}}$.
   * Reducing power in gaseous state (vapor phase): $\mathbf{\text{Cs} > \text{Rb} > \text{K} > \text{Na} > \text{Li}}$ (governed strictly by $\text{IE}_1$).
1.3 Solutions of Alkali Metals in Liquid Ammonia
Alkali metals dissolve smoothly in liquid ammonia without hydrogen evolution to yield distinctive solutions depending on concentration:


1. Dilute Solutions ($<3\text{ M}$): $$\text{M} + (x + y)\text{NH}_3 \longrightarrow \underbrace{[\text{M}(\text{NH}_3)x]^+}{\text{Ammoniated Cation}} + \underbrace{[e(\text{NH}_3)y]^-}{\text{Ammoniated Electron}}$$
   * Color: Deep Blue (due to absorption of red photons by ammoniated electrons).
   * Magnetic Nature: Paramagnetic (due to single unpaired ammoniated electrons).
   * Electrical Conductivity: Excellent conductor (both ammoniated cations and ammoniated electrons act as charge carriers).
   * Chemical Character: Powerful reducing agent (Birch reduction converts alkynes to trans-alkenes).
2. Concentrated Solutions ($>3\text{ M}$):
   * Ammoniated electrons pair up: $2[e(\text{NH}_3)_y]^- \to [e_2(\text{NH}3){2y}]^{2-}$.
   * Color: Changes from deep blue to metallic bronze/copper.
   * Magnetic Nature: Diamagnetic (all spins paired).
   * Conductivity decreases toward metallic values.
3. Decomposition upon Standing or Catalysis: $$\text{M} + \text{NH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{Fe catalyst / Impurities}} \text{MNH}_2 \text{ (Amide)} + \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2 \uparrow$$

2. Group 2: The Alkaline Earth Metals
2.1 General Characteristics & Trends
* Valence configuration: $ns^2$. Divalent cations ($\text{M}^{2+}$) form readily.
* Ionization Enthalpies ($\text{IE}_1$ vs. $\text{IE}_2$):
   * $\text{IE}_1(\text{Group 2}) > \text{IE}_1(\text{Group 1})$ due to higher effective nuclear charge and smaller atomic size.
   * $\text{IE}_2(\text{Group 2}) \ll \text{IE}_2(\text{Group 1})$ because removing the second electron in Group 1 disrupts a noble gas core, whereas in Group 2 it achieves an inert gas configuration ($\text{M}^{2+}$).
   * In aqueous solution or solid lattice, the high hydration energy and lattice energy of $\text{M}^{2+}$ overcompensate for $\text{IE}_2$, ensuring that univalent $\text{M}^+$ compounds of Group 2 do not exist.
* Flame Colors:
   * Calcium ($\text{Ca}$): Brick Red ($622\text{ nm}$)
   * Strontium ($\text{Sr}$): Crimson Red ($606\text{ nm}$)
   * Barium ($\text{Ba}$): Apple Green ($524\text{ nm}$)
   * Radium ($\text{Ra}$): Crimson
   * $\text{Be}$ and $\text{Mg}$ do not impart color to the Bunsen flame because their valence electrons are tightly bound by the compact nucleus and cannot be excited by flame thermal energy.
2.2 Anomalous Behavior of Lithium and Beryllium & Diagonal Relationships
1. Lithium vs. Group 1:
   * $\text{Li}$ is much harder and has a higher melting/boiling point.
   * $\text{Li}$ reacts directly with nitrogen to form ionic nitride ($\text{Li}_3\text{N}$); other alkali metals do not form stable nitrides.
   * $\text{LiOH}$ is a weak base and decomposes on heating to $\text{Li}_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$; other alkali hydroxides do not decompose.
   * $\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3$ is thermally unstable, decomposing to $\text{Li}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$; other alkali carbonates are thermally stable.
   * $\text{LiNO}_3$ decomposes on heating to $\text{Li}_2\text{O} + \text{NO}_2 + \text{O}_2$; other alkali nitrates decompose to nitrites $\text{MNO}_2 + \text{O}_2$.
   * $\text{LiF}$ and $\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3$ are sparingly soluble in water due to high lattice energy.
2. Diagonal Relationship: $\text{Li}$ and $\text{Mg}$:
   * Arises from similar ionic potential (charge-to-size ratio): $\phi(\text{Li}^+) \approx \phi(\text{Mg}^{2+})$ ($0.76\text{ \AA}$ vs. $0.72\text{ \AA}$).
   * Both form nitrides ($\text{Li}_3\text{N}$ and $\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2$).
   * Both form normal oxides ($\text{Li}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{MgO}$) rather than peroxides or superoxides.
   * Both chlorides are deliquescent and crystallize as hydrates ($\text{LiCl}\cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{MgCl}_2\cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$); both are soluble in ethanol.
   * Both carbonates decompose on heating to release $\text{CO}_2$.
3. Diagonal Relationship: $\text{Be}$ and $\text{Al}$:
   * Both have similar charge-to-radius ratio ($\phi \approx 64/31 \approx 2.1$).
   * Both metals are rendered passive by concentrated nitric acid due to protective oxide films.
   * Both oxides and hydroxides are amphoteric: $$\text{Be}(\text{OH})_2 + 2\text{OH}^- \longrightarrow [\text{Be}(\text{OH})_4]^{2-} \text{ (Beryllate)}$$ $$\text{Al}(\text{OH})_3 + \text{OH}^- \longrightarrow [\text{Al}(\text{OH})_4]^- \text{ (Aluminate)}$$
   * Both chlorides ($\text{BeCl}_2$ and $\text{Al}_2\text{Cl}_6$) have bridged chloro structures in the vapor/solid phase and act as Lewis acids (catalysts in Friedel-Crafts reactions).
   * Both form carbide methanides ($\text{Be}_2\text{C}$ and $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$) which hydrolyze to give methane gas ($\text{CH}_4$).
   * Beryllium has a maximum coordination number of 4 due to absence of $d$-orbitals, forming $[\text{BeF}_4]^{2-}$.

3. Oxides, Peroxides & Superoxides
3.1 Reaction of Alkali Metals with Oxygen
The nature of the primary oxide formed on burning alkali metals in excess air depends directly on the cation size: $$\mathbf{\text{Li} \longrightarrow \text{Li}_2\text{O} \text{ (Monoxide)}}, \qquad \mathbf{\text{Na} \longrightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{O}_2 \text{ (Peroxide)}}, \qquad \mathbf{\text{K, Rb, Cs} \longrightarrow \text{MO}_2 \text{ (Superoxide)}}$$


* Lattice Energy Stability Rule:
   * Small cations ($\text{Li}^+$) stabilize small anions ($\text{O}^{2-}$ oxide) via high lattice energy.
   * Intermediate cations ($\text{Na}^+$) stabilize intermediate anions ($\text{O}_2^{2-}$ peroxide).
   * Large cations ($\text{K}^+, \text{Rb}^+, \text{Cs}^+$) stabilize large, polarizable polyatomic anions ($\text{O}_2^-$ superoxide) via lattice energy and low polarization.
* Hydrolysis Reactions Matrix:
   * Normal Oxide: $\text{M}_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{M}^+ + 2\text{OH}^-$
   * Peroxide: $\text{M}_2\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{M}^+ + 2\text{OH}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2$
   * Superoxide: $2\text{MO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{M}^+ + 2\text{OH}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2 + \text{O}_2 \uparrow$
* Magnetic & Electronic Nature:
   * Peroxide ($\text{O}_2^{2-}$): Total 18 electrons; $\sigma^* 2p^2$ fully paired $\implies$ Diamagnetic, colorless.
   * Superoxide ($\text{O}_2^-$): Total 17 electrons; $\pi^* 2p^3$ has one unpaired electron $\implies$ Paramagnetic, colored (yellow/orange). $\text{KO}_2$ is used as an oxygen generator and $\text{CO}_2$ scrubber in submarines and spacecraft: $$4\text{KO}_2 + 2\text{CO}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + 3\text{O}_2 \uparrow$$ $$4\text{KO}_2 + 4\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 4\text{KHCO}_3 + 3\text{O}_2 \uparrow$$

4. Industrial Manufacture of Sodium Compounds
![S Block Industrial Cells And Thermal Stability](/media/s_block_industrial_cells_and_thermal_stability.webp) Description: Two-panel industrial reference diagram: (Panel A) Technical layouts of Castner-Kellner cell, Down's electrolytic cell, and Solvay soda ash synthesis; (Panel B) Thermal stability rankings of carbonates and nitrates alongside solubility inversions of hydroxides, sulphates, and fluorides.
4.1 Sodium Hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$ / Caustic Soda)
Commercial manufacture is carried out via the electrolysis of brine ($\text{NaCl}$ solution):


1. Castner-Kellner Cell (Mercury Cathode Process):
   * Anode: Carbon/Graphite blocks.
   * Cathode: Flowing pool of mercury ($\text{Hg}$) at the bottom of the cell.
   * Electrochemical Reactions: $$\text{Anode: } 2\text{Cl}^- \longrightarrow \text{Cl}_2(g) \uparrow + 2e^-$$ $$\text{Cathode: } \text{Na}^+ + e^- + \text{Hg} \longrightarrow \text{Na/Hg (Sodium Amalgam)}$$
   * Denuder Tank: The sodium amalgam is transferred to a separate chamber and treated with water: $$2\text{Na/Hg} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{NaOH} + 2\text{Hg} \downarrow \text{ (recycled)} + \text{H}_2(g) \uparrow$$
   * Yields exceptionally pure $\text{NaOH}$ free from chloride contamination.
2. Reactions of $\text{NaOH}$ with Halogens:
   * With cold, dilute $\text{NaOH}$ (Disproportionation to halide and hypohalite): $$2\text{NaOH} + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Cold, dil.}} \text{NaCl} + \text{NaOCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
   * With hot, concentrated $\text{NaOH}$ (Disproportionation to halide and chlorate): $$6\text{NaOH} + 3\text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Hot, conc.}} 5\text{NaCl} + \text{NaClO}_3 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
3. Reaction with Amphoteric Metals: $$\text{Zn} + 2\text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{ZnO}_2 \text{ (Sodium zincate)} + \text{H}_2 \uparrow$$ $$2\text{Al} + 2\text{NaOH} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{NaAlO}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \uparrow$$ $$\text{Sn} + 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{SnO}_3 \text{ (Sodium stannite)} + 2\text{H}_2 \uparrow$$
4.2 Sodium Carbonate ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$ / Washing Soda) & Solvay Process
1. Reaction Sequence of Solvay (Ammonia-Soda) Process:
   * Carbon dioxide is bubbled through brine saturated with ammonia: $$\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \longrightarrow (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{CO}_3$$ $$(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{NH}_4\text{HCO}_3$$ $$\text{NH}_4\text{HCO}_3 + \text{NaCl} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{NaHCO}_3 \downarrow \text{ (precipitates)}} + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$$
   * Sodium hydrogen carbonate crystals are filtered and calcined: $$2\text{NaHCO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow \text{ (recycled)}$$
   * Ammonia recovery: Ammonium chloride filtrate is boiled with milk of lime: $$2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{Ca}(\text{OH})_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{NH}_3 \uparrow \text{ (recycled)} + \mathbf{\text{CaCl}_2 \text{ (only major byproduct)}}$$
2. Why Potassium Carbonate ($\text{K}_2\text{CO}_3$) Cannot Be Prepared by Solvay Process:
   * Potassium hydrogen carbonate ($\text{KHCO}_3$) is fairly soluble in water and does not precipitate upon adding $\text{NH}_4\text{HCO}_3$ to $\text{KCl}$ solution.
3. Efflorescence of Washing Soda: $$\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Dry air}} \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O} \text{ (Monohydrate)} + 9\text{H}_2\text{O} \uparrow \xrightarrow{>373\text{ K}} \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \text{ (Soda ash, anhydrous)}$$

5. Important Compounds of Alkaline Earth Metals
![Lime Kiln Quicklime Calcination](/media/lime_kiln_quicklime_calcination.webp) Description: Industrial rotary lime kiln used for thermal calcination of calcium carbonate limestone to quicklime ($CaO$) at $1070-1270\text{ K}$.
5.1 Calcium Oxide ($\text{CaO}$ / Quicklime)
* Preparation: Thermal calcination of limestone in a rotary kiln at $1070-1270\text{ K}$: $$\text{CaCO}_3 \rightleftharpoons \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow \quad (\Delta H = +178\text{ kJ/mol})$$
   * Carbon dioxide is continuously vented to shift equilibrium forward.
* Properties:
   * Slaking of Lime (Highly Exothermic): $\text{CaO} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Ca}(\text{OH})_2$ ($\Delta H = -65\text{ kJ/mol}$).
   * Quicklime slaked with caustic soda produces Soda Lime ($\text{NaOH} + \text{CaO}$).
   * Basic flux: $\text{CaO} + \text{SiO}_2 \to \text{CaSiO}_3$ (slag in iron blast furnace).
5.2 Calcium Hydroxide ($\text{Ca}(\text{OH})_2$ / Slaked Lime)
* Clear aqueous solution is called Lime Water; a suspension of slaked lime in water is called Milk of Lime.
* Reaction with Chlorine Gas: $$2\text{Ca}(\text{OH})_2 + 2\text{Cl}_2 \longrightarrow \underbrace{\text{CaCl}_2 + \text{Ca}(\text{OCl})_2 + 2\text{H}2\text{O}}{\mathbf{\text{Bleaching Powder: } \text{Ca}(\text{OCl})_2 \cdot \text{CaCl}_2 \cdot \text{Ca}(\text{OH})_2 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}}}$$
5.3 Calcium Sulphate, Gypsum & Plaster of Paris

![Magnesium Sulphate Epsom Chart](/media/magnesium_sulphate_epsom_chart.webp) Description: Synthesis and hydration equilibria of magnesium sulphate heptahydrate ($MgSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$, Epsom salt) from kieserite, magnesite, and dolomite.


1. Hydration & Heating Cycle: $$\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \text{ (Gypsum)} \xrightarrow{393\text{ K } (120^\circ\text{C})} \mathbf{\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} \text{ (Plaster of Paris)}} + \frac{3}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}$$ $$\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{>393\text{ K}} \mathbf{\text{CaSO}_4 \text{ (Dead Burnt Plaster)}} \xrightarrow{>1373\text{ K}} \text{CaO} + \text{SO}_2 + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2$$
2. Setting of Plaster of Paris:
   * When mixed with water, POP forms a plastic mass that hardens in $10-15$ minutes with an expansion of $\sim 1\%$ in volume (enabling sharp casting of statues and surgical molds): $$\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{+\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \text{ (orthorhombic)} \xrightarrow{\text{Hardening}} \text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \text{ (monoclinic)}$$
   * Retarders: Glycerol, borax, glue retard setting. Accelerators: Alum, $\text{NaCl}$ accelerate setting.
   * Keene's Cement: POP mixed with alum and ignited sets into an extremely hard marble-like finish.
5.4 Portland Cement
* Raw Materials: Limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$) providing $\text{CaO}$ ($50-60\%$) and Clay providing $\text{SiO}_2$ ($20-25\%$), $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ ($5-10\%$), $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ ($1-2\%$).
* Major Ingredients:
   * Dicalcium silicate ($\text{Ca}_2\text{SiO}_4$ or $\text{C}_2\text{S}$): $26\%$ (provides ultimate long-term strength).
   * Tricalcium silicate ($\text{Ca}_3\text{SiO}_5$ or $\text{C}_3\text{S}$): $51\%$ (rapid initial hydration and setting).
   * Tricalcium aluminate ($\text{Ca}_3\text{Al}_2\text{O}_6$ or $\text{C}_3\text{A}$): $11\%$ (fastest setting; initiates flash setting).
   * Tetracalcium aluminoferrite ($\text{Ca}_4\text{Al}_2\text{Fe}2\text{O}{10}$ or $\text{C}_4\text{AF}$): $9\%$.
* Role of Gypsum in Cement:
   * Raw cement clinker is mixed with $2-3\%$ Gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$) before fine grinding.
   * Function: Gypsum reacts with $\text{C}_3\text{A}$ to form insoluble calcium sulfoaluminate ($\text{C}_3\text{A} \cdot 3\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 32\text{H}_2\text{O}$), retarding the initial rapid hydration and preventing flash setting, allowing sufficient working time for mortar placement.

6. Thermal Stability & Solubility Invariants
6.1 Thermal Stability Hierarchy of Polyatomic Salts
For salts containing large oxyanions ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}, \text{SO}_4^{2-}, \text{NO}_3^-$), thermal stability is controlled by the polarizing power (charge/radius ratio) of the cation (Fajan's rule):


* High polarizing power cations ($\text{Be}^{2+}, \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Li}^+$) strongly distort the electron cloud of the oxyanion, pulling electron density into a covalent bond with oxygen and facilitating decomposition into a stable oxide and volatile gas ($\text{CO}_2, \text{SO}_3, \text{NO}_2$).
* Carbonates Stability: $$\mathbf{\text{BeCO}_3 < \text{MgCO}_3 < \text{CaCO}_3 < \text{SrCO}_3 < \text{BaCO}_3}$$ $$\text{BeCO}_3 \text{ decomposes below } 100^\circ\text{C (must be stored in } \text{CO}_2 \text{ atmosphere); } \text{BaCO}_3 \text{ stable up to } 1360^\circ\text{C.}$$
* Sulphates Stability: $$\mathbf{\text{BeSO}_4 < \text{MgSO}_4 < \text{CaSO}_4 < \text{SrSO}_4 < \text{BaSO}_4}$$
6.2 Solubility Trends in Water
Solubility in water depends on the competition between Lattice Energy ($U$) and Hydration Energy ($\Delta_{\text{hyd}} H$):


1. Hydroxides of Group 2: $$\mathbf{\text{Be}(\text{OH})_2 < \text{Mg}(\text{OH})_2 < \text{Ca}(\text{OH})_2 < \text{Sr}(\text{OH})_2 < \text{Ba}(\text{OH})_2} \quad (\text{Solubility Increases Down Group})$$
   * Hydroxide ($\text{OH}^-$) is a small anion. As cation radius increases down the group, lattice energy decreases much faster than hydration energy ($U \propto \frac{1}{r_+ + r_-}$), making $\Delta H_{\text{solution}}$ more favorable.
2. Sulphates of Group 2: $$\mathbf{\text{BeSO}_4 > \text{MgSO}_4 > \text{CaSO}_4 > \text{SrSO}_4 > \text{BaSO}_4} \quad (\text{Solubility Decreases Down Group})$$
   * Sulphate ($\text{SO}4^{2-}$) is a large anion. The lattice energy remains almost constant down the group because $r-$ dominates ($r_+ + r_- \approx r_-$). Meanwhile, hydration energy of the cation drops precipitously ($\Delta_{\text{hyd}} H \propto \frac{1}{r_+}$). Thus, hydration enthalpy is insufficient to overcome lattice energy for $\text{BaSO}_4$.
3. Fluorides vs. Other Halides:
   * $\text{BeF}_2$ is highly soluble in water due to immense hydration energy of tiny $\text{Be}^{2+}$.
   * $\text{MgF}_2$ is the least soluble fluoride of the group due to high lattice energy.

7. High-Yield JEE Traps & Exam Invariants
1. Nitrate Decomposition Duality:
   * Group 1 nitrates (except $\text{Li}$) decompose at $500^\circ\text{C}$ to nitrites without brown fumes: $$2\text{NaNO}_3 \xrightarrow{500^\circ\text{C}} 2\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \uparrow$$
   * $\text{LiNO}_3$ and all Group 2 nitrates decompose to oxides, liberating brown $\text{NO}_2$ fumes: $$4\text{LiNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{Li}_2\text{O} + 4\text{NO}_2 \uparrow + \text{O}_2 \uparrow$$ $$2\text{Mg}(\text{NO}_3)_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{MgO} + 4\text{NO}_2 \uparrow + \text{O}_2 \uparrow$$
2. Carbide Hydrolysis Classification:
   * Methanides ($C^{4-}$): $\text{Be}_2\text{C} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{Be}(\text{OH})_2 + \text{CH}_4 \uparrow$; $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 4\text{Al}(\text{OH})_3 + 3\text{CH}_4 \uparrow$.
   * Acetylides ($C_2^{2-}$): $\text{CaC}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Ca}(\text{OH})_2 + \text{C}_2\text{H}_2 \uparrow$ (Ethyne).
   * Allylides ($C_3^{4-}$): $\text{Mg}_2\text{C}_3 + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{Mg}(\text{OH})_2 + \text{CH}_3-\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} \uparrow$ (Propyne).
3. Potassium Carbonate Solvay Impossibility:
   * $\text{K}_2\text{CO}_3$ cannot be synthesized via Solvay process because intermediate $\text{KHCO}_3$ is too soluble in water to precipitate out upon adding $\text{NH}_4\text{HCO}_3$.
4. Alkali Metal Halides Hydration:
   * Only lithium halides form hydrates ($\text{LiCl}\cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$); other alkali metal halides ($\text{NaCl}, \text{KCl}$) crystallize anhydrously from aqueous solution.
5. $\text{BeCl}_2$ Phase Polymorphism:
   * Solid state: Polymeric infinite chain with coordinate bonds; $\text{Be}$ is $sp^3$ hybridized with coordination number 4.
   * Vapor state ($<1200\text{ K}$): Chloro-bridged dimer $\text{Be}_2\text{Cl}_4$; $\text{Be}$ is $sp^2$ hybridized with coordination number 3.
   * High temperature vapor ($>1200\text{ K}$): Linear monomer $\text{Cl}-\text{Be}-\text{Cl}$; $\text{Be}$ is $sp$ hybridized with coordination number 2.
