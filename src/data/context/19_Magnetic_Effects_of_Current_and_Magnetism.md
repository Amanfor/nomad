# Physics Revision Context: Chapter 19 — Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism

---

### 1.1 Vector Formulation of Biot-Savart Law
- The differential magnetic induction $d\vec{B}$ produced at position vector $\vec{r}$ relative to an infinitesimal current element $I d\vec{l}$ is:
  $$d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I (d\vec{l} \times \hat{r})}{r^2} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I (d\vec{l} \times \vec{r})}{r^3}$$
  where:
  $$\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} = 10^{-7}\text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A} = 10^{-7}\text{ Wb}/(\text{A}\cdot\text{m})$$
  $$\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\text{ H/m}$$
- **Direction:** Determined by the right-hand cross-product rule ($d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}$).

---

#### A. Straight Conductor of Finite Length
For a straight wire carrying current $I$, with perpendicular distance $d$ to observation point $P$, and endpoints subtending angles $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ with the normal:
$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d} (\sin\alpha_1 + \sin\alpha_2)$$

- **Infinitely Long Wire:** $\alpha_1 = 90^\circ, \alpha_2 = 90^\circ$:
  $$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi d}$$
- **Semi-Infinite Wire:** $\alpha_1 = 90^\circ, \alpha_2 = 0^\circ$:
  $$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}$$
- **Point along the Line of Current:** $d\vec{l} \times \hat{r} = 0 \implies B = 0$.

#### B. Circular Arc of Current (Radius $R$, Subtending Angle $\phi$)
At the center of curvature:
$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I \phi}{4\pi R}$$
- **Full Circular Loop ($\phi = 2\pi$):**
  $$B_{\text{center}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2 R}$$
- **Semicircular Loop ($\phi = \pi$):**
  $$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4 R}$$
- **Quadrant Arc ($\phi = \pi/2$):**
  $$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{8 R}$$

#### C. Axial Field of a Circular Current Loop (Radius $R$, Turns $N$)
At distance $x$ along the symmetry axis perpendicular to the plane of the loop:
$$B(x) = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$$
- For $x \gg R$:
  $$B(x) \approx \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2 x^3} = \frac{\mu_0 (N I \pi R^2)}{2\pi x^3} = \frac{\mu_0 M}{2\pi x^3} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{2 M}{x^3}$$
  matching the axial field of a magnetic dipole of magnetic moment $M = N I A$.
- **Helmholtz Coils:** Two identical coaxial coils separated by distance equal to their radius ($d = R$) carrying current in the same direction produce a remarkably uniform magnetic field in the central region:
  $$B_{\text{Helmholtz}} = \frac{8 \mu_0 N I}{5\sqrt{5} R} \approx 0.716 \frac{\mu_0 N I}{R}$$

#### D. Solenoid (Turn Density $n = N/L$)
- **Infinitely Long Solenoid (Interior):**
  $$B = \mu_0 n I$$
- **At Endpoints of Long Solenoid:**
  $$B_{\text{end}} = \frac{1}{2} \mu_0 n I$$
- **Finite Solenoid:** If semi-vertical angles subtended by the ends at an axial point are $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$:
  $$B = \frac{1}{2} \mu_0 n I (\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)$$

#### E. Toroid (Endless Solenoid)
Inside the core of mean radius $R$ with total turns $N$:
$$B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi R} = \mu_0 n I$$
Outside the toroid and in the open interior space: $B = 0$.

### 2.1 Statement of Ampère's Law
- The line integral of magnetic field $\vec{B}$ around any closed Ampèrian loop equals $\mu_0$ times the total net algebraic current threading the surface enclosed by the loop:
  $$\oint_C \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enclosed}}$$

### 2.2 Field of a Solid Cylindrical Conductor (Radius $R$, Total Current $I$)
- **Inside the Conductor ($r \le R$):** Assuming uniform current density $J = \frac{I}{\pi R^2}$:
  $$I_{\text{enclosed}} = J (\pi r^2) = I \frac{r^2}{R^2}$$
  $$\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = B (2\pi r) = \mu_0 I \frac{r^2}{R^2} \implies B(r) = \frac{\mu_0 I r}{2\pi R^2} \quad (B \propto r)$$
- **Outside the Conductor ($r \ge R$):**
  $$B(r) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} \quad \left(B \propto \frac{1}{r}\right)$$
- **At Conductor Surface ($r = R$):** Maximum field $B_{\text{max}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi R}$.

### 3.1 Lorentz Force
When a charge $q$ moves with velocity $\vec{v}$ in simultaneous electric $\vec{E}$ and magnetic $\vec{B}$ fields:
$$\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$$

### 3.2 Pure Magnetic Deflection ($\vec{E} = 0$)
$$\vec{F}_m = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$$
- **Work Done by Magnetic Force is Always Zero:**
  $$P = \vec{F}_m \cdot \vec{v} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B}) \cdot \vec{v} = 0 \implies W = \Delta K = 0$$
  Magnetic fields cannot change the speed or kinetic energy of a charged particle; they solely alter the trajectory direction.

#### A. Motion Perpendicular to Magnetic Field ($\theta = 90^\circ$)
The magnetic force provides exact centripetal acceleration:
$$q v B = \frac{m v^2}{r} \implies r = \frac{m v}{q B} = \frac{p}{q B} = \frac{\sqrt{2 m K}}{q B} = \frac{\sqrt{2 m q V}}{q B}$$
- **Time Period & Frequency (Cyclotron Invariants):**
  $$T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi m}{q B}$$
  $$f_c = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{q B}{2\pi m}, \quad \omega_c = \frac{q B}{m}$$
  *(Crucial Insight: Time period, orbital frequency, and cyclotron resonance are completely independent of speed $v$, energy, and radius $r$).*

#### B. Motion at Arbitrary Angle $\theta$ to Field (Helical Motion)
Resolving velocity into components:
$$v_\parallel = v \cos\theta \quad (\text{parallel to } \vec{B}), \quad v_\perp = v \sin\theta \quad (\text{perpendicular to } \vec{B})$$
- $v_\parallel$ is unaffected by $\vec{B}$ (uniform linear motion along field).
- $v_\perp$ produces uniform circular motion of gyro-radius:
  $$r = \frac{m v_\perp}{q B} = \frac{m v \sin\theta}{q B}$$
- **Pitch of the Helix ($p$):** Linear distance traveled along field lines in one complete orbit:
  $$p = v_\parallel T = (v \cos\theta) \left(\frac{2\pi m}{q B}\right) = \frac{2\pi m v \cos\theta}{q B}$$

### 3.4 Crossed Fields (Velocity Selector & Cyclotron)
- **Velocity Selector ($\vec{E} \perp \vec{B} \perp \vec{v}$):**
  $$q E = q v B \implies v = \frac{E}{B}$$
  Only particles possessing exact speed $v = E/B$ emerge undeflected.
- **Cyclotron Principles:**
  - Alternating RF oscillator tuned to cyclotron frequency: $f_{\text{osc}} = \frac{q B}{2\pi m}$.
  - Maximum kinetic energy upon exiting at Dees radius $R$:
    $$K_{\text{max}} = \frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{max}}^2 = \frac{q^2 B^2 R^2}{2 m}$$

### 4.1 Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor
$$\vec{F} = I (\vec{L} \times \vec{B})$$
- For an arbitrary shaped wire between endpoints $A$ and $B$ in a uniform field:
  $$\vec{F} = I (\vec{L}_{AB} \times \vec{B})$$
  where $\vec{L}_{AB}$ is the direct displacement vector connecting initial and final points.
- **Closed Current Loop in Uniform $\vec{B}$:** $\oint d\vec{l} = 0 \implies \vec{F}_{\text{net}} = 0$.

### 4.2 Force Between Two Parallel Straight Currents
Force per unit length between two long straight conductors separated by distance $d$:
$$\frac{F}{L} = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi d}$$
- **Parallel Currents (same direction):** Attractive force.
- **Antiparallel Currents (opposite direction):** Repulsive force.
- **SI Definition of Ampère:** $1\text{ A}$ is the constant current which, maintained in two straight parallel conductors of infinite length and negligible cross-section placed $1\text{ m}$ apart in vacuum, produces a force of $2 \times 10^{-7}\text{ N/m}$.

### 4.3 Torque on a Current Loop & Magnetic Moment
- **Magnetic Dipole Moment ($\vec{M}$):**
  $$\vec{M} = N I \vec{A}$$
  Direction given by the right-hand grip rule around the current loop.
- **Bohr Magneton ($\mu_B$):** Magnetic moment associated with an electron in the first Bohr orbit of Hydrogen ($L = \hbar$):
  $$\mu_B = \frac{e \hbar}{2 m_e} = \frac{e h}{4\pi m_e} \approx 9.274 \times 10^{-24}\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2 = 9.274 \times 10^{-24}\text{ J/T}$$
- **Gyromagnetic Ratio:** $\frac{M}{L} = \frac{q}{2 m} = \frac{e}{2 m_e}$ (universal for orbital electron motion).
- **Restoring Torque in Uniform $\vec{B}$:**
  $$\vec{\tau} = \vec{M} \times \vec{B} \implies \tau = M B \sin\theta = N I A B \sin\theta$$
- **Potential Energy:**
  $$U = -\vec{M} \cdot \vec{B} = -M B \cos\theta$$

### 5.1 Operating Principle
- Radial magnetic field engineered via cylindrical soft-iron core and concave pole pieces ensures field lines are parallel to the plane of the coil ($\theta = 90^\circ$ everywhere) $\implies \tau = N I A B$.
- Deflecting torque balanced by torsional restoring torque of phosphor-bronze strip:
  $$N I A B = C \theta \implies I = \left( \frac{C}{N A B} \right) \theta = K \theta$$
  where $K = \frac{C}{N A B}$ is the galvanometer reduction factor.

### 5.2 Sensitivities
- **Current Sensitivity ($S_i$):**
  $$S_i = \frac{\theta}{I} = \frac{N A B}{C}$$
- **Voltage Sensitivity ($S_v$):**
  $$S_v = \frac{\theta}{V} = \frac{\theta}{I R_g} = \frac{S_i}{R_g} = \frac{N A B}{C R_g}$$
  *(Doubling turns $N$ doubles $S_i$, but doubles resistance $R_g$, leaving $S_v$ unchanged).*

### 5.3 Conversion of Galvanometer
- **Conversion to Ammeter (Range $0 - I$):** Connected in parallel with a low resistance Shunt ($S$):
  $$S = \frac{I_g R_g}{I - I_g}$$
  Equivalent Ammeter Resistance: $R_A = \frac{R_g S}{R_g + S} \approx S$ (ideal ammeter $R_A = 0$).
- **Conversion to Voltmeter (Range $0 - V$):** Connected in series with a high resistance ($R$):
  $$R = \frac{V}{I_g} - R_g$$
  Equivalent Voltmeter Resistance: $R_V = R_g + R$ (ideal voltmeter $R_V \to \infty$).

### 6.1 Magnetic Elements of Earth
1. **Magnetic Declination ($\theta$):** Acute angle between geographic meridian and magnetic meridian at a place.
2. **Magnetic Dip or Inclination ($\delta$):** Angle made by the total magnetic field of Earth $\vec{B}_e$ with the horizontal in the magnetic meridian:
   $$B_H = B_e \cos\delta, \quad B_V = B_e \sin\delta$$
   $$\tan\delta = \frac{B_V}{B_H}, \quad B_e = \sqrt{B_H^2 + B_V^2}$$
   - At Magnetic Poles: $\delta = 90^\circ \implies B_H = 0, B_V = B_e$.
   - At Magnetic Equator: $\delta = 0^\circ \implies B_V = 0, B_H = B_e$.
3. **Apparent Dip in Vertical Planes:** If apparent dips measured in two mutually perpendicular vertical planes are $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$, the true dip $\delta$ satisfies:
   $$\cot^2\delta = \cot^2\delta_1 + \cot^2\delta_2$$

### 6.2 Tangent Law & Tangent Galvanometer
- **Tangent Law:** When a magnetic needle is suspended under two mutually perpendicular uniform magnetic fields $B$ and $B_H$, it comes to equilibrium at angle $\theta$ with $B_H$:
  $$B = B_H \tan\theta$$
- **Tangent Galvanometer Equation:**
  $$\frac{\mu_0 N I}{2 R} = B_H \tan\theta \implies I = \left( \frac{2 R B_H}{\mu_0 N} \right) \tan\theta = K \tan\theta$$

### 7.1 Magnetostatic Quantities & Relations
- **Intensity of Magnetization ($\vec{I}$):** Magnetic moment per unit volume induced in a specimen:
  $$\vec{I} = \frac{\vec{M}_{\text{net}}}{V} \quad (\text{A/m})$$
- **Magnetizing Field ($\vec{H}$):** Ability of external current to magnetize a medium:
  $$\vec{H} = \frac{\vec{B}}{\mu} = \frac{\vec{B}_0}{\mu_0} \quad (\text{A/m})$$
- **Magnetic Susceptibility ($\chi$):** Dimensionless measure of magnetization responsiveness:
  $$\chi = \frac{I}{H}$$
- **Magnetic Permeability ($\mu$):**
  $$\mu = \frac{B}{H} = \mu_0 \mu_r = \mu_0 (1 + \chi)$$
  $$B = \mu_0(H + I) = \mu_0(1 + \chi)H$$

### 7.2 Classification of Magnetic Materials

| Property | Diamagnetic | Paramagnetic | Ferromagnetic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin** | Orbital paired electrons (Lenz's Law response) | Unpaired spin magnetic moments | Domain exchange coupling |
| **Behavior in Field** | Repelled weakly from stronger to weaker field | Attracted weakly towards stronger field | Strongly attracted into high field |
| **Susceptibility ($\chi$)** | Small and negative ($-1 \le \chi < 0$), e.g., $\chi_{\text{Bi}} \approx -1.6 \times 10^{-5}$ | Small and positive ($0 < \chi < 10^{-3}$) | Extremely large and positive ($\chi \sim 10^2 - 10^5$) |
| **Relative Permeability** | $\mu_r < 1$ | $\mu_r > 1$ (slightly) | $\mu_r \gg 1$ (hundreds to thousands) |
| **Temperature Dependence** | Independent of temperature (except Superconductors) | **Curie's Law:** $\chi = \frac{C}{T}$ | **Curie-Weiss Law:** $\chi = \frac{C}{T - T_c}$ ($T > T_c$) |
| **Superconductor State** | **Perfect Diamagnet:** $\chi = -1, \mu_r = 0, B = 0$ (Meissner Effect) | None | Transitions to Paramagnetic above $T_c$ |
| **Classic Examples** | $\text{Bi, Cu, Ag, Au, Pb, } \text{H}_2\text{O, NaCl, Glass}$ | $\text{Al, Na, Ca, Pt, } \text{O}_2\text{ (gas), } \text{CuSO}_4$ | $\text{Fe, Co, Ni, Steel, Alnico, NdFeB}$ |

### 7.3 Hysteresis Loop ($B-H$ Curve) & Material Selection

![Ferromagnetic Hysteresis and Materials](/media/ferromagnetic_hysteresis_loop_and_materials.webp)
*Description: Structural hysteresis loop tracing ferromagnetic response through a cyclic magnetizing cycle ($H$), marking saturation ($B_s$), residual retentivity ($B_r$), coercive field ($-H_c$), and area energy dissipation. The right panel contrasts the tall, narrow profile of Soft Iron (low coercivity and minimal hysteresis area) against the broad profile of Steel/Alnico (high coercivity and permanent flux retention).*

- **Key Hysteresis Landmarks:**
  1. **Retentivity (Remanence $B_r$):** The value of residual magnetic flux density remaining in the material when the magnetizing field is reduced to zero ($H = 0$).
  2. **Coercivity (Coercive Force $H_c$):** The reverse magnetizing field required to completely demagnetize the material ($B = 0$).
  3. **Hysteresis Loss:** The energy dissipated as heat per unit volume during one complete magnetization cycle equals the enclosed area of the $B-H$ loop:
     $$W = \oint B \, dH = \text{Area of } B-H \text{ loop}$$
- **Engineering Material Selection for JEE:**
  - **Electromagnets & Transformer Cores:** Require **Soft Iron** (High permeability $\mu$, high retentivity $B_r$, very low coercivity $H_c$, and narrow loop area to prevent wasteful heat dissipation during 50 Hz AC cycles).
  - **Permanent Magnets:** Require **Steel or Alnico** (High coercivity $H_c$ so magnetization is immune to stray demagnetizing fields, high retentivity, and high saturation magnetization).

### Archetype 1: Magnetic Field at the Center of Polygon Current Loops
- **Problem:** Find the magnetic field at the center of a regular polygon of $n$ sides with inscribed radius $r$ carrying current $I$.
- **Derivation:**
  - Each side subtends an angle $\frac{2\pi}{n}$ at the center, so $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = \frac{\pi}{n}$.
  - Field due to one side:
    $$B_1 = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi r} \left(\sin\frac{\pi}{n} + \sin\frac{\pi}{n}\right) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)$$
  - Net field for all $n$ sides:
    $$B_{\text{net}} = n B_1 = \frac{\mu_0 n I}{2\pi r} \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)$$
  - *Square ($n=4$):* $B = \frac{2\sqrt{2} \mu_0 I}{\pi a}$ (where side length is $a$).
  - *Equilateral Triangle ($n=3$):* $B = \frac{9 \mu_0 I}{2\pi r} = \frac{18 \mu_0 I}{\sqrt{3} \pi a}$.
  - *Circle ($n \to \infty$):* $\lim_{n \to \infty} n \sin(\pi/n) = \pi \implies B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2r}$.

### Archetype 2: Ratio of Time Periods in a Vibration Magnetometer
- **Problem:** A bar magnet oscillates in a uniform horizontal field $B_H$ with time period $T$. If it is cut into two equal halves along its length, find the new period in the same field.
- **Derivation:**
  - Time period of vibration magnetometer:
    $$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I}{M B_H}}$$
  - Original magnet: mass $m$, length $L \implies I_0 = \frac{m L^2}{12}$, moment $M_0$.
  - When cut longitudinally into two equal halves:
    - Mass of each half: $m' = m/2$.
    - Pole strength of each half: $q_m' = q_m/2 \implies M' = M_0/2$.
    - Moment of inertia: $I' = \frac{(m/2) L^2}{12} = \frac{I_0}{2}$.
  - New period:
    $$T' = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I_0/2}{(M_0/2) B_H}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I_0}{M_0 B_H}} = \mathbf{T} \quad (\text{Unchanged!})$$
  - *(Contrast: If cut transverse to its length into two halves: $m' = m/2, L' = L/2 \implies I' = \frac{(m/2)(L/2)^2}{12} = \frac{I_0}{8}$, and $M' = M_0/2 \implies T' = T \sqrt{\frac{1/8}{1/2}} = \frac{T}{2}$).*

### Archetype 3: Magnetic Deflection in a Sector Field
- **Problem:** A particle of mass $m$, charge $q$, and speed $v$ enters a region of uniform magnetic field of width $d < r$ perpendicularly. Find the deviation angle $\delta$ and lateral displacement $y$.
- **Derivation:**
  - Radius of curvature: $r = \frac{mv}{qB}$.
  - From trajectory geometry:
    $$\sin\delta = \frac{d}{r} = \frac{q B d}{m v} \implies \delta = \arcsin\left(\frac{q B d}{m v}\right)$$
  - Time spent in field:
    $$t = \frac{\delta}{\omega} = \frac{\delta m}{q B}$$
  - Lateral displacement at exit:
    $$y = r(1 - \cos\delta) = r - \sqrt{r^2 - d^2}$$
