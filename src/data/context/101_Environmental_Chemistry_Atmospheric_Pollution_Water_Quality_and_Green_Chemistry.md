1. Atmospheric Pollution, Smog Chemistry & Stratospheric Ozone
![Atmospheric Pollution Smog Mechanisms And Ozone Hole Chemistry](/media/atmospheric_pollution_smog_mechanisms_and_ozone_hole_chemistry.webp) Description: Two-panel reference diagram for atmospheric chemistry: (Panel A) Comparative matrix between classical reducing smog and photochemical oxidizing smog, alongside photochemical reaction cycles and acid rain marble cancer reactions; (Panel B) Stratospheric ozone Chapman equilibrium, CFC catalytic chlorine radical destruction, Antarctic polar stratospheric cloud heterogeneous reactions, and seasonal ozone hole mechanics.
1.1 Atmospheric Stratification & Tropospheric Gaseous Pollutants
The Earth's atmosphere extends hundreds of kilometers, divided into distinct thermal layers:


1. Troposphere ($0 - 10\ \text{km}$): Lowest turbulent atmospheric zone where all living organisms exist, containing $\approx 80\%$ of atmospheric mass and water vapor.
2. Stratosphere ($10 - 50\ \text{km}$): Calm layer containing the protective ozone ($\text{O}_3$) shield.
Major Tropospheric Gaseous Pollutants
* Oxides of Sulphur ($\text{SO}_2, \text{SO}_3$):
   * Generated from burning fossil fuels (coal, petroleum) and roasting of sulfide ores.
   * Toxic effects: Severe respiratory ailments (asthma, bronchitis, emphysema); causes stiffness and premature falling of flower buds; eye irritation.
   * Atmospheric oxidation catalyzed by airborne particulates and atmospheric oxidants: $$2\text{SO}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \xrightarrow{\text{Particulate matter}} 2\text{SO}_3(g)$$ $$\text{SO}_2(g) + \text{O}_3(g) \to \text{SO}_3(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$ $$\text{SO}_2(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2(l) \to \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(aq)$$
* Oxides of Nitrogen ($\text{NO}, \text{NO}_2$):
   * Generated at high temperatures ($1483\ \text{K}$) in automobile internal combustion engines and thermal power plants: $$\text{N}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \xrightarrow{1483\ \text{K}} 2\text{NO}(g)$$ $$2\text{NO}(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \to 2\text{NO}_2(g) \quad (\text{Reddish-brown toxic gas})$$ $$\text{NO}(g) + \text{O}_3(g) \to \text{NO}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$
   * Toxic effects: Reddish-brown pungent haze over urban highways; damages plant leaves, inhibiting photosynthesis; causes acute respiratory problems in children.
* Oxides of Carbon ($\text{CO}$ and $\text{CO}_2$):
   * Carbon Monoxide ($\text{CO}$): Incomplete combustion of carbon fuels.
      * Binds with blood hemoglobin to form carboxyhemoglobin, which is $\approx \mathbf{300\times}$ more stable than oxyhemoglobin!
      * At $3 - 4\%$ carboxyhemoglobin in blood, oxygen-carrying capacity drops critically, causing dizziness, headache, cardiovascular stress, and fatal asphyxiation. In pregnant women, it induces spontaneous abortion or fetal deformities.
   * Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$): Complete combustion, respiration. Natural level $\approx 0.03\% = 300 - 400\ \text{ppm}$. Major greenhouse gas driving global warming.
* Hydrocarbons: Unburnt fuel vapors. Carcinogenic; cause tissue breakdown, leaf shedding, and twig decay in vegetation.

1.2 Particulate Pollutants
Microscopic solid or liquid droplets suspended in air ($0.002 - 500\ \mu\text{m}$):


1. Viable Particulates: Minute living organisms floating in atmosphere (bacteria, fungi, molds, algae spores). Cause human allergies and plant pathology.
2. Non-Viable Particulates:
   * Smoke: Fine solid particles ($0.01 - 1\ \mu\text{m}$) produced by combustion of organic matter (soot, cigarette smoke, combustion of oil).
   * Dust: Solid particles ($> 1\ \mu\text{m}$) produced by mechanical crushing, grinding, and blasting of solid materials (sand, cement, fly ash).
   * Mist: Liquid droplets produced by spray condensation or chemical reactions (sulfuric acid mist, herbicide/insecticide sprays).
   * Fumes: Condensed vapors from metallurgical smelting, sublimation, or distillation of metals and solvents.

1.3 Global Environmental Phenomena: Greenhouse Effect & Acid Rain
1. Greenhouse Effect & Global Warming
Solar shortwave radiation heats the Earth's surface. The warm Earth radiates longwave infrared (thermal) radiation back towards space. Greenhouse gases trap this outgoing IR radiation and re-radiate it back to the ground:


* Relative Contributions of Greenhouse Gases: $$\mathbf{\text{CO}_2 \ (\approx 50\%) > \text{CH}_4 \ (\approx 20\%) > \text{CFCs} \ (\approx 14\%) > \text{O}_3 \ (\approx 8\%) > \text{N}_2\text{O} \ (\approx 6\%) > \text{H}_2\text{O}\text{ (vapor)}}$$
2. Acid Rain
Normal rainwater has a mildly acidic $\text{pH} \approx 5.6$ due to equilibrium with atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$: $$\text{CO}_2(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3(aq) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+(aq) + \text{HCO}_3^-(aq)$$


* Definition of Acid Rain: Precipitation with $\mathbf{\text{pH} < 5.6}$.
* Major Acids: Sulfuric acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) and Nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$).
* Harmful Environmental Effects:
   1. Marble Cancer: Corrodes limestone and marble monuments (e.g., Taj Mahal): $$\mathbf{\text{CaCO}_3(s) + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) \to \text{CaSO}_4(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{CO}_2(g)}$$
   2. Aquatic Ecosystem Collapse: Acidifies lakes and streams. At $\text{pH} < 4.5$, fish reproduction halts and fish kills occur.
   3. Heavy Metal Leaching: Dissolves and leaches toxic metals ($\text{Al}^{3+}, \text{Pb}^{2+}, \text{Cd}^{2+}$) from soil into aquifers and drinking water supplies.

1.4 Smog Chemistry: Classical Smog vs Photochemical Smog
Feature
	Classical (London) Smog
	Photochemical (Los Angeles) Smog
	Historical Origin
	First documented in London (1952)
	First documented in Los Angeles
	Climatic Condition
	Cool, humid climate in winter (early morning)
	Warm, dry, and sunny climate in summer (midday)
	Primary Composition
	Smoke + Fog + $\text{SO}_2$ + carbon soot
	Unsaturated hydrocarbons + $\text{NO}_x$ + Sunlight ($h\nu$)
	Chemical Nature
	REDUCING SMOG (High $\text{SO}_2$ and carbon particles)
	OXIDIZING SMOG (High $\text{O}_3, \text{NO}_2, \text{PAN}$, acrolein)
	Major Toxic Components
	Sulfuric acid mist, $\text{SO}_2$
	Ozone ($\text{O}_3$), PAN, Acrolein, Formaldehyde
	Health Hazards
	Severe bronchitis, chest constriction
	Extreme eye irritation, throat dryness, rubber cracking
	Control Measures
	Desulfurization of industrial fuels
	Catalytic converters in cars; planting Pinus, Pyrus, Quercus
	Mechanism of Photochemical Smog
1. Sunlight photolyzes nitrogen dioxide: $$\text{NO}_2(g) \xrightarrow{h\nu \ (\lambda < 400\ \text{nm})} \text{NO}(g) + \text{O}$$
2. Atomic oxygen combines with molecular oxygen to generate ozone: $$\text{O} + \text{O}_2(g) \to \text{O}_3(g)$$
3. Ozone oxidizes $\text{NO}$ back to $\text{NO}_2$: $$\text{NO}(g) + \text{O}_3(g) \to \text{NO}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$
4. Ozone reacts with unburnt unsaturated hydrocarbons to yield free radicals that oxidize into powerful eye irritants:
   * Formaldehyde: $\text{HCHO}$
   * Acrolein: $\mathbf{\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}-\text{CHO}}$ (severe lachrymator)
   * Peroxyacetyl Nitrate (PAN): $\mathbf{\text{CH}_3\text{CO}-\text{OO}-\text{NO}_2}$ (powerful phytotoxin and eye irritant)

1.5 Stratospheric Ozone Depletion & The Antarctic Ozone Hole
1. Chapman Equilibrium (Ozone Shield)
In the stratosphere, UV radiation ($160 - 240\ \text{nm}$) produces dynamic equilibrium: $$\text{O}_2(g) \xrightarrow{\text{UV}} 2\text{O}; \qquad \text{O} + \text{O}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons \text{O}_3(g)$$
2. Catalytic Destruction by Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs / Freons)
Stable, non-flammable CFCs (e.g., $\text{CF}_2\text{Cl}_2$, Freon-12) drift into the stratosphere where intense UV photolyzes them: $$\text{CF}_2\text{Cl}_2(g) \xrightarrow{\text{UV}} \dot{\text{C}}\text{F}_2\text{Cl}(g) + \dot{\text{Cl}}(g)$$ The liberated chlorine free radical ($\dot{\text{Cl}}$) triggers a catalytic cycle: $$\dot{\text{Cl}}(g) + \text{O}_3(g) \to \text{Cl}\dot{\text{O}}(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$ $$\text{Cl}\dot{\text{O}}(g) + \text{O} \to \dot{\text{Cl}}(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$ A single chlorine free radical catalyzes the destruction of over $100,000$ ozone molecules before termination!
3. Antarctic Ozone Hole & Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs)
* In summer: Chlorine radicals are trapped in inert chlorine sinks: $$\text{Cl}\dot{\text{O}}(g) + \text{NO}_2(g) \to \text{ClONO}_2(g) \quad (\text{Chlorine nitrate})$$ $$\dot{\text{Cl}}(g) + \text{CH}_4(g) \to \dot{\text{C}}\text{H}_3(g) + \text{HCl}(g)$$
* In winter: Isolation over Antarctica creates a polar vortex with temperatures below $-78^\circ\text{C}$, forming Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs) composed of solid nitric acid trihydrate. Heterogeneous reactions on ice surfaces hydrolyze the inert sinks: $$\text{ClONO}_2(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(s) \to \text{HOCl}(g) + \text{HNO}_3(s)$$ $$\text{ClONO}_2(s) + \text{HCl}(s) \to \text{Cl}_2(g) + \text{HNO}_3(s)$$
* In spring (September/October): Sunlight returns, photolyzing $\text{HOCl}$ and $\text{Cl}_2$: $$\text{HOCl} \xrightarrow{h\nu} \dot{\text{O}}\text{H} + \dot{\text{Cl}}, \qquad \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu} 2\dot{\text{Cl}}$$ Unleashing an explosive burst of chlorine radicals that causes the seasonal Ozone Hole.

2. Water Quality Standards, Pollution & Green Chemistry
![Water Quality Standards Bod Eutrophication And Green Chemistry](/media/water_quality_standards_bod_eutrophication_and_green_chemistry.webp) Description: Two-panel reference diagram for aquatic environmental chemistry: (Panel A) International drinking water standards for fluoride, lead, sulfate, nitrate, and heavy metal limits with physiological pathological consequences; (Panel B) Dissolved oxygen metrics, biochemical oxygen demand criteria, fertilizer-driven eutrophication sequence, and daily-life green chemistry processes.
2.1 Water Quality Metrics: DO, BOD, COD & Eutrophication
1. Dissolved Oxygen (DO)
* In cold clean water, dissolved oxygen concentration reaches saturation at $\approx 10\ \text{ppm}$ ($10\ \text{mg/L}$).
* If DO concentration drops below $6\ \text{ppm}$, fish growth and reproduction are inhibited.
* Below $4\ \text{ppm}$, mass fish asphyxiation occurs.
2. Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)
The amount of dissolved oxygen (in $\text{ppm}$ or $\text{mg/L}$) consumed by aerobic bacteria to biochemically oxidize organic waste in a water sample over $5$ days at $20^\circ\text{C}$:


* Clean Potable Water: $\mathbf{\text{BOD} < 5\ \text{ppm}}$
* Highly Polluted Water: $\mathbf{\text{BOD} \ge 17\ \text{ppm}}$
3. Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD)
The total amount of oxygen required to chemically oxidize all organic and inorganic oxidizable matter using acidified potassium dichromate ($\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ / $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$). $$\mathbf{\text{COD} > \text{BOD} \quad \text{always}}$$
4. Eutrophication
The accelerated aging of water bodies caused by nutrient runoff (fertilizer nitrates and detergent phosphates): $$\text{Nutrient Enrichment} \to \text{Dense Algal Bloom} \to \text{Sunlight Blocked} \to \text{Algal Death} \to \text{Bacterial Decay Depletes DO} \to \text{Aquatic Fauna Dies}$$

2.3 Soil Pollution, Agrochemicals & Green Chemistry
1. Pesticides & Herbicides
* Persistent Organochlorines: DDT (Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane), BHC (Benzene hexachloride / Lindane), Aldrin, Dieldrin. Insoluble in water, lipid-soluble; cause severe bioaccumulation in food chains.
* Organophosphates & Carbamates: Malathion, Parathion. Biodegradable but potent neurotoxins (inhibit acetylcholinesterase).
* Herbicides: Sodium chlorate ($\text{NaClO}_3$), Sodium arsenite ($\text{Na}_3\text{AsO}_3$). Cause congenital birth defects.
2. Principles of Green Chemistry in Daily Life
Green chemistry designs chemical products and processes that eliminate the generation of hazardous substances.


1. Dry Cleaning of Clothes:
   * Conventional toxic method: Tetrachloroethene ($\text{Cl}_2\text{C}=\text{CCl}_2$), a suspected carcinogen that contaminates groundwater.
   * Green alternative: Liquefied $\text{CO}_2$ with a suitable detergent (non-toxic, recyclable, benign to aquifers).
2. Bleaching of Paper:
   * Conventional toxic method: Chlorine gas ($\text{Cl}_2$), which produces toxic chlorinated dioxins.
   * Green alternative: Hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$) with a suitable catalyst (decomposes cleanly to $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{O}_2$).
3. One-Step Synthesis of Acetaldehyde (Ethanal):
   * Green Wacker process: Direct catalytic oxidation of ethene with oxygen in water: $$\mathbf{\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd(II)} / \text{Cu(II)} \text{ in water}} \text{CH}_3\text{CHO} \quad (90\% \text{ yield})}$$
   * Replaces multi-step toxic oxidation with a high atom-economy, environmentally benign process.

3. High-Yield Problem Archetypes & Structural JEE Traps
#
	Concept / Scenario
	Common Mistake / Trap
	Correct Chemical Principle
	1
	Chemical Character of Smogs
	Stating Classical smog is oxidizing and Photochemical smog is reducing.
	Classical smog is REDUCING (contains $\text{SO}_2$ and carbon soot); Photochemical smog is OXIDIZING (contains $\text{O}_3, \text{NO}_2, \text{PAN}$).
	2
	Rainwater pH vs Acid Rain Threshold
	Calling normal rain with $\text{pH} = 6.0$ acid rain.
	Natural unpolluted rain has $\text{pH} \approx 5.6$ due to dissolved $\text{CO}_2$. Acid rain is strictly defined as $\mathbf{\text{pH} < 5.6}$.
	3
	Carbon Monoxide Affinity
	Thinking $\text{CO}$ binds with hemoglobin $20\times$ more strongly than $\text{O}_2$.
	Carboxyhemoglobin is $\approx 300\times$ more stable than oxyhemoglobin!
	4
	Fluoride Ion Concentrations
	Confusing mottling limit with fluorosis threshold.
	$\text{F}^-$ up to $1\ \text{ppm}$ protects enamel; $> 2\ \text{ppm}$ causes brown mottling; $> 10\ \text{ppm}$ causes severe fluorosis.
	5
	Nitrate Health Hazard
	Associating blue baby syndrome with nitrite or ammonia.
	Methemoglobinemia ("Blue Baby Syndrome") is specifically caused by excess Nitrate ($\text{NO}_3^- > 50\ \text{ppm}$)!
	6
	Biochemical Oxygen Demand Criteria
	Thinking high BOD indicates pure water.
	High BOD means bacteria require huge amounts of $\text{O}_2$ to decompose waste: Clean water has $\text{BOD} < 5\ \text{ppm}$; Polluted water has $\text{BOD} \ge 17\ \text{ppm}$.
	7
	Stratospheric Ozone Catalytic Agent
	Believing fluorine radicals destroy ozone.
	In CFC photolysis, $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bonds break preferentially because $\text{C}-\text{F}$ bonds are too strong: Chlorine free radicals ($\dot{\text{Cl}}$) catalytically destroy ozone.
	8
	Inert Chlorine Sinks in Antarctica
	Considering $\text{ClONO}_2$ and $\text{HCl}$ as active ozone-depleting agents.
	$\text{ClONO}_2$ and $\text{HCl}$ are inert chlorine sinks that prevent ozone depletion until hydrolyzed on PSC ice surfaces during polar winter.
	9
	Green Dry Cleaning Solvent
	Writing liquid nitrogen or liquid $\text{SO}_2$ for dry cleaning.
	Green dry cleaning replaces $\text{Cl}_2\text{C}=\text{CCl}_2$ with liquefied carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) with a detergent.
	10
	COD vs BOD Magnitude
	Claiming BOD can exceed COD in municipal wastewater.
	COD measures all chemically oxidizable matter (biodegradable + non-biodegradable), so $\mathbf{\text{COD} > \text{BOD}}$ always!
