## 1. Systematic Classification Architecture & Preliminary Dry Tests


### 1.1 The Master Three-Tier Analytical Classification of Anions
Unlike basic radicals (cations) which are separated sequentially in a rigid cascade, acidic radicals (anions) are classified into three major analytical divisions based on their response to mineral acids and specific precipitation/redox reagents:


| Analytical Division | Anions Included | Group Reagent & Reaction Basis | Volatile Products / Diagnostic Phenomena |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Class A**<br>*(Dilute Acid Group)* | $\text{CO}_3^{2-}, \text{HCO}_3^-, \text{SO}_3^{2-}, \text{S}^{2-}, \text{NO}_2^-, \text{S}_2\text{O}_3^{2-}, \text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$ | Dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or dilute $\text{HCl}$ (Cold / Gentle Warm) | Decomposed with effervescence to evolve volatile characteristic gases ($\text{CO}_2, \text{SO}_2, \text{H}_2\text{S}, \text{NO}_2, \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$) |
| **Class B**<br>*(Concentrated Acid Group)* | $\text{Cl}^-, \text{Br}^-, \text{I}^-, \text{NO}_3^-, \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$ | Concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ + Heat | Decomposed via displacement or redox to evolve pungent/colored acid vapors or halogens ($\text{HCl}, \text{Br}_2, \text{I}_2, \text{NO}_2, \text{CO} + \text{CO}_2$) |
| **Class C**<br>*(Special / Precipitation Group)* | $\text{SO}_4^{2-}, \text{PO}_4^{3-}, \text{BO}_3^{3-}$ | Specific precipitation or oxidation/esterification reagents | Non-volatile anions that do NOT evolve gases with dilute or conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$; confirmed by individual precipitation or flame tests |

### 1.2 Preparation & Chemical Function of Sodium Carbonate Extract (Soda Extract)
When an unknown inorganic salt mixture contains water-insoluble salts (e.g., heavy metal carbonates, sulfates, sulfides, phosphates), direct aqueous testing is impossible. Furthermore, colored or amphoteric heavy metal cations ($\text{Fe}^{3+}, \text{Cu}^{2+}, \text{Bi}^{3+}, \text{Pb}^{2+}, \text{Zn}^{2+}$) interfere severely with anion confirmatory tests.
* **Preparation Procedure:**
  1. Mix 1 part of the finely powdered original salt mixture with 3-4 parts of pure, solid anhydrous sodium carbonate ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$).
  2. Add $15-20\text{ mL}$ of distilled water in a porcelain dish.
  3. Boil the suspension vigorously for $10-15\text{ minutes}$, maintaining the volume by adding distilled water.
  4. Filter the hot mixture.
* **Chemical Reaction Principle (Double Decomposition):**
  Boiling with high concentration of $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ forces metathesis of insoluble heavy metal salts:
  $$\mathbf{\text{M}_x\text{A}_y(s) + y\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3(aq) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{M}_x(\text{CO}_3)_y\downarrow (\text{Insoluble Heavy Metal Carbonate}) + 2y\text{Na}^+ + y\text{A}^{2-}(aq)}$$
  * **Residue (on filter paper):** Contains insoluble basic carbonates / hydroxides / carbonates of heavy metals ($\text{PbCO}_3, \text{CuCO}_3, \text{Fe(OH)}_3, \text{BaCO}_3$).
  * **Filtrate (Soda Extract):** A clear, colorless aqueous solution containing all anions present as highly soluble sodium salts ($\text{NaCl}, \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4, \text{NaNO}_3, \text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4$).
* **CRITICAL JEE LIMITATION (The Soda Extract Trap):**
  **Soda Extract CANNOT be used to detect Carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) or Bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_3^-$)** because a massive excess of $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ was intentionally introduced during its preparation! Carbonates and bicarbonates must ALWAYS be tested using the **original dry salt**.

### 1.4 Preliminary Dry Analytical Tests


#### 1.4.1 Dry Heating Phenomena & Residue Thermochromism
Heating a small pinch of the dry salt in a clean, dry ignition tube yields high-yield diagnostic clues:
* **Decrepitation:** Crackling sound during heating due to the explosive mechanical rupture of crystalline lattices enclosing microscopic water droplets: $\text{NaCl}, \text{KI}, \text{Pb(NO}_3)_2, \text{Ba(NO}_3)_2$.
* **Sublimation:** Direct solid-to-vapor phase transition:
  * White sublimate: $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}, \text{NH}_4\text{Br}, \text{HgCl}_2, \text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2, \text{As}_2\text{O}_3$.
  * Yellow sublimate: $\text{As}_2\text{S}_3, \text{HgI}_2$ (turns red on rubbing).
  * Black/Violet sublimate: $\text{I}_2$ (condenses as lustrous violet-black crystals).
* **Swelling / Intumescence:** Salt melts and froths up into a voluminous spongy mass: Borates ($\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$), Alums ($\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4 \cdot \text{Al}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3 \cdot 24\text{H}_2\text{O}$), Phosphates.
* **Residue Thermochromism (Color Changes Hot vs. Cold):**


| Metal Oxide Formed | Color When Hot | Color When Cold | Physicochemical Explanation |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **$\text{ZnO}$** | **Yellow** | **White** | Non-stoichiometric oxygen loss on heating creates trapped electrons in anionic vacancies (F-centers), narrowing the band gap. Reversible upon cooling! |
| **$\text{PbO}$** | **Reddish-Brown** | **Yellow** | Polymorphic structural phase shift between litharge (tetragonal) and massicot (orthorhombic). |
| **$\text{SnO}_2$** | **Dirty Brown** | **Yellow** | Reversible thermal lattice contraction and charge-transfer band broadening. |
| **$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$** | **Dark Red / Brown** | **Light Brown** | Thermal spin-state variation and lattice parameter distortion. |

#### 1.4.2 The Borax Bead Test Chemistry
Performed on a clean platinum wire loop with borax ($\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$):
1. **Bead Synthesis Reaction:**
   $$\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 + 10\text{H}_2\text{O}\uparrow$$
   $$\mathbf{\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 \xrightarrow{\text{ignite}} 2\text{NaBO}_2\ (\text{Sodium Metaborate}) + \text{B}_2\text{O}_3\ (\text{Boric Anhydride})\quad (\text{Glassy Bead})}$$
   The resulting clear, transparent bead contains basic $\text{NaBO}_2$ and acidic boric anhydride $\text{B}_2\text{O}_3$.
2. **Reaction with Metal Salts (Metaborate Formation):**
   When heated with a colored transition metal salt, $\text{B}_2\text{O}_3$ displaces volatile acidic anhydrides to form characteristic colored transition metal metaborates:
   $$\text{CuO} + \text{B}_2\text{O}_3 \to \mathbf{\text{Cu(BO}_2)_2\quad (\text{Cupric Metaborate, Blue})}$$
3. **Redox Flame Chemistry (Oxidizing vs. Reducing Flames):**
   * **In Oxidizing Flame (Luminous Zone Tip):** Transition metals are oxidized to their highest stable oxidation states.
   * **In Reducing Flame (Carbon-Rich Inner Cone):** Carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$) and incandescent carbon reduce the metaborates to lower oxidation states or metallic elements:
     $$2\text{Cu(BO}_2)_2 + 2\text{NaBO}_2 + \text{C} \to 2\text{CuBO}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 + \text{CO}\uparrow$$
     $$2\text{CuBO}_2 + \text{C} \to \mathbf{2\text{Cu}(s)\ (\text{Red Opaque Bead of Metallic Copper})} + \text{B}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{CO}\uparrow$$


| Transition Metal | Oxidizing Flame (Hot) | Oxidizing Flame (Cold) | Reducing Flame (Hot) | Reducing Flame (Cold) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Copper ($\text{Cu}$)** | Green | **Sky Blue** | Colorless | **Opaque Red ($\text{Cu}^0 / \text{Cu}_2\text{O}$)** |
| **Iron ($\text{Fe}$)** | Dark Yellow / Brown | **Yellow** | Dirty Green | **Bottle Green ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$)** |
| **Chromium ($\text{Cr}$)** | Dark Green | **Emerald Green** | Green | **Emerald Green ($\text{Cr}^{3+}$)** |
| **Cobalt ($\text{Co}$)** | **Deep Royal Blue** | **Deep Royal Blue** | **Deep Royal Blue** | **Deep Royal Blue ($\text{Co}^{2+}$)** |
| **Nickel ($\text{Ni}$)** | Violet | **Reddish-Brown** | Grey | **Grey Metallic ($\text{Ni}^0$)** |
| **Manganese ($\text{Mn}$)** | Light Violet | **Amethyst / Violet** | Colorless | **Colorless ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$)** |

#### 1.4.3 Charcoal Cavity & Cobalt Nitrate Bead Tests
* **Charcoal Cavity Test:**
  The salt is mixed with anhydrous $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ and heated in a small depression on a charcoal block using a blowpipe:
  $$\text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \to \text{ZnCO}_3 + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$$
  $$\text{ZnCO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{ZnO} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow \quad (\text{White residue, yellow when hot})$$
* **Cobalt Nitrate Test:**
  If a white non-fusible residue is left in the charcoal cavity, it is moistened with a dilute drop of cobalt nitrate ($\text{Co(NO}_3)_2$) and heated intensely in the oxidizing flame:
  $$2\text{Co(NO}_3)_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{CoO} + 4\text{NO}_2\uparrow + \text{O}_2\uparrow$$
  $$\mathbf{\text{ZnO} + \text{CoO} \to \text{CoO} \cdot \text{ZnO}\quad (\text{Rinmann's Green, Green Mass})}$$
  $$\mathbf{\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{CoO} \to \text{CoO} \cdot \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\quad (\text{Thenard's Blue, Blue Mass})}$$
  $$\mathbf{\text{MgO} + \text{CoO} \to \text{CoO} \cdot \text{MgO}\quad (\text{Pink / Flesh-Colored Mass})}$$
  $$\mathbf{\text{SnO}_2 + 2\text{CoO} \to 2\text{CoO} \cdot \text{SnO}_2\quad (\text{Bluish-Green Mass})}$$

## 2. Class A Anions: Dilute Mineral Acid Group


### 2.1 Carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) & Bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_3^-$)


![Confirmatory Tests for Dilute and Concentrated Acid Anions](/media/confirmatory_tests_dilute_and_concentrated_acid_anions.webp)
*Description: Two-panel diagnostic reaction graphic: (A) Class A (dilute acid) confirmatory transformations illustrating the $\text{MgSO}_4$ differentiation between carbonate (cold white ppt) and bicarbonate (white ppt on boiling), the alkaline sodium nitroprusside purple complex for sulfide, acidified dichromate emerald-green reduction by $\text{SO}_2$, and the multi-stage rainbow decomposition of silver thiosulfate ($\text{Ag}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3 \to \text{Ag}_2\text{S}$); (B) Halide differentiation matrix detailing the stepwise silver nitrate solubilities in ammonia ($\text{AgCl}$ soluble in dilute $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{AgBr}$ soluble in concentrated $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{AgI}$ completely insoluble) and the organic layer extraction in $\text{CCl}_4/\text{CS}_2$ with chlorine water.*


1. **Action of Dilute Acid:**
   Treating the dry salt with dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or dilute $\text{HCl}$ causes brisk effervescence of a colorless, odorless gas ($\text{CO}_2$):
   $$\mathbf{\text{CO}_3^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ \to \text{CO}_2(g)\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   $$\mathbf{\text{HCO}_3^- + \text{H}^+ \to \text{CO}_2(g)\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
2. **Lime Water & Baryta Water Tests:**
   Passing the evolved $\text{CO}_2$ gas into clear lime water ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$) or baryta water ($\text{Ba(OH)}_2$) turns it milky due to insoluble carbonate precipitation:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Ca(OH)}_2(aq) + \text{CO}_2(g) \to \text{CaCO}_3\downarrow (\text{Milkiness}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   * **Excess $\text{CO}_2$ Effect:** Passing $\text{CO}_2$ continuously dissolves the milky precipitate completely, yielding a clear solution of calcium bicarbonate:
     $$\mathbf{\text{CaCO}_3(s) + \text{CO}_2(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightleftharpoons \text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2(aq)\quad (\text{Clear Soluble Solution})}$$
   * **Boiling the Clear Solution:** Heating the clear solution decomposes the bicarbonate, reprecipitating white milky $\text{CaCO}_3$:
     $$\text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2(aq) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathbf{\text{CaCO}_3\downarrow (\text{Milkiness Reappears})} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
3. **Rigorous Differentiation: Carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) vs. Bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_3^-$):**
   * **The Magnesium Sulfate ($\text{MgSO}_4$) Test:**
     * To an aqueous solution of the salt, add $\text{MgSO}_4$ solution:
     * **Carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$):** Yields an **immediate white precipitate of $\text{MgCO}_3$ in the cold**:
       $$\text{CO}_3^{2-} + \text{Mg}^{2+} \to \mathbf{\text{MgCO}_3\downarrow \quad (\text{White Precipitate in Cold})}$$
     * **Bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_3^-$):** Produces **NO PRECIPITATE IN THE COLD** because magnesium bicarbonate is highly soluble. Upon boiling, it decomposes to precipitate white $\text{MgCO}_3$:
       $$\text{Mg}^{2+} + 2\text{HCO}_3^- \xrightarrow{\text{Cold}} \text{Mg(HCO}_3)_2(aq) \quad (\text{Soluble, No Ppt})$$
       $$\text{Mg(HCO}_3)_2(aq) \xrightarrow{\text{Boil}} \mathbf{\text{MgCO}_3\downarrow (\text{White Precipitate on Heating})} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
   * **Phenolphthalein Test:**
     * $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ solutions undergo extensive hydrolysis ($\text{CO}_3^{2-} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{HCO}_3^- + \text{OH}^-$), creating $\text{pH} > 9$, turning phenolphthalein **vibrant pink**.
     * $\text{HCO}_3^-$ solutions have $\text{pH} \approx 8.3$ and remain **colorless**.

### 2.2 Sulfite ($\text{SO}_3^{2-}$) & Bisulfite ($\text{HSO}_3^-$)
1. **Action of Dilute Acid:**
   Evolves sulfur dioxide gas ($\text{SO}_2$) having the characteristic, choking, suffocating odor of burning sulfur:
   $$\mathbf{\text{SO}_3^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ \to \text{SO}_2(g)\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
2. **Acidified Potassium Dichromate ($\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$) Test:**
   Filter paper moistened with acidified $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ turns **brilliant emerald green** when exposed to the gas:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} (\text{Orange}) + 2\text{H}^+ + 3\text{SO}_2 \to 2\text{Cr}^{3+} (\text{Emerald Green}) + 3\text{SO}_4^{2-} + \text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
3. **Acidified Potassium Permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$) Test:**
   $\text{SO}_2$ reduces purple permanganate to colorless $\text{Mn}^{2+}$, decolorizing the solution:
   $$\mathbf{2\text{MnO}_4^- (\text{Purple}) + 5\text{SO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{Mn}^{2+} (\text{Colorless}) + 5\text{SO}_4^{2-} + 4\text{H}^+}$$
4. **Barium Chloride Test:**
   Adding $\text{BaCl}_2$ to a sulfite solution yields a white precipitate of $\text{BaSO}_3$, which dissolves completely in dilute $\text{HCl}$ with the evolution of $\text{SO}_2$ (distinction from $\text{BaSO}_4$):
   $$\text{SO}_3^{2-} + \text{Ba}^{2+} \to \mathbf{\text{BaSO}_3\downarrow (\text{White})}$$
   $$\text{BaSO}_3 + 2\text{HCl} \to \text{BaCl}_2 + \text{SO}_2\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}\quad (\text{Dissolves completely})$$

### 2.3 Sulfide ($\text{S}^{2-}$)
1. **Action of Dilute Acid:**
   Evolves hydrogen sulfide gas ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) with the nauseating stench of rotten eggs:
   $$\mathbf{\text{S}^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ \to \text{H}_2\text{S}(g)\uparrow}$$
2. **Lead Acetate Test:**
   Filter paper moistened with lead acetate solution turns **lustrous silvery-black** due to lead sulfide precipitation:
   $$\mathbf{\text{Pb(CH}_3\text{COO})_2 + \text{H}_2\text{S} \to \text{PbS}\downarrow (\text{Black}) + 2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}}$$
3. **Cadmium Acetate Test:**
   Passing the gas into cadmium acetate solution precipitates **bright canary-yellow cadmium sulfide ($\text{CdS}$)**:
   $$\text{Cd}^{2+} + \text{H}_2\text{S} \to \mathbf{\text{CdS}\downarrow (\text{Canary Yellow})} + 2\text{H}^+$$
4. **The Sodium Nitroprusside Confirmatory Test:**
   Adding a few drops of freshly prepared sodium nitroprusside ($\text{Na}_2[\text{Fe(CN)}_5\text{NO}]$) to an alkaline sulfide solution produces an intense, magnificent **purple / violet coloration**:
   $$\mathbf{\text{S}^{2-} + [\text{Fe(CN)}_5\text{NO}]^{2-} \to [\text{Fe(CN)}_5(\text{NOS})]^{4-}\quad (\text{Sodium Thionitroprusside, Intense Purple / Violet})}$$
   * **Reagent State:** In sodium nitroprusside $[\text{Fe(CN)}_5\text{NO}]^{2-}$, iron is formally $\text{Fe(II)}$ ($d^6$, low spin, diamagnetic) and nitrosyl is coordinated as $\text{NO}^+$.
   * Upon reaction with $\text{S}^{2-}$, sulfide coordinates to the nitrosyl nitrogen to form the thionitrosyl ligand ($\text{NOS}^-$), expanding the complex charge to $-4$.
   * The purple color fades upon standing due to oxidation.

### 2.4 Nitrite ($\text{NO}_2^-$)
1. **Action of Dilute Acid:**
   Dilute acid generates unstable nitrous acid ($\text{HNO}_2$), decomposing to evolve pale blue liquid that emits **pungent reddish-brown fumes of $\text{NO}_2$**:
   $$2\text{NO}_2^- + 2\text{H}^+ \to 2\text{HNO}_2 \to \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{NO}\uparrow + \text{NO}_2\uparrow$$
   $$\mathbf{2\text{NO} + \text{O}_2(\text{air}) \to 2\text{NO}_2\uparrow \quad (\text{Pungent Reddish-Brown Fumes})}$$
2. **Starch-Iodide Test:**
   Nitrite acts as an oxidizing agent, liberating free iodine from acidified potassium iodide:
   $$\mathbf{2\text{NO}_2^- + 2\text{I}^- + 4\text{H}^+ \to \text{I}_2 + 2\text{NO}\uparrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   The liberated $\text{I}_2$ forms an intense **deep blue inclusion complex with starch**.
3. **The Cold Ferrous Sulfate Test:**
   When a nitrite solution is acidified with dilute acetic acid and mixed with $\text{FeSO}_4$ solution, an intense **dark brown coloration / ring forms in the cold WITHOUT needing concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$** (distinction from $\text{NO}_3^-$):
   $$\text{NO}_2^- + \text{Fe}^{2+} + 2\text{H}^+ \to \text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{NO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
   $$[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} + \text{NO} \to \mathbf{[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{NO})]^{2+}\quad (\text{Brown Ring Complex})}$$

### 2.5 Thiosulfate ($\text{S}_2\text{O}_3^{2-}$)
1. **Action of Dilute Acid:**
   Evolves sulfur dioxide gas accompanied by the **slow, gradual precipitation of pale yellow colloidal sulfur**:
   $$\mathbf{\text{S}_2\text{O}_3^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ \to \text{SO}_2(g)\uparrow + \text{S}\downarrow (\text{Pale Yellow Colloidal Sulfur}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   *(Crucial Distinction: Sulfite $\text{SO}_3^{2-}$ gives a clear solution with $\text{SO}_2$, whereas thiosulfate gives a milky yellow turbid suspension of sulfur).*
2. **Silver Nitrate "Rainbow Sequence" Test:**
   Adding $\text{AgNO}_3$ solution dropwise produces a white precipitate of silver thiosulfate ($\text{Ag}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3$), which dissolves in excess thiosulfate to form a soluble complex $[\text{Ag(S}_2\text{O}_3)_2]^{3-}$.
   When allowed to stand or warmed, the precipitate undergoes a **stunning chromatic transformation sequence**:
   $$\mathbf{\text{WHITE} \to \text{YELLOW} \to \text{ORANGE} \to \text{BROWN} \to \text{BLACK}}$$
   $$\text{Ag}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \mathbf{\text{Ag}_2\text{S}\downarrow (\text{Black Silver Sulfide})} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$$
3. **Ferric Chloride ($\text{FeCl}_3$) Test:**
   Adding $\text{FeCl}_3$ yields a transient, **deep violet coloration of bis(thiosulfato)ferrate(III)**, which rapidly fades to colorless as $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ is reduced to $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ with tetrathionate formation:
   $$\text{Fe}^{3+} + 2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3^{2-} \to [\text{Fe(S}_2\text{O}_3)_2]^-\ (\text{Deep Violet Complex, Transient})$$
   $$[\text{Fe(S}_2\text{O}_3)_2]^- + \text{Fe}^{3+} \to \mathbf{2\text{Fe}^{2+} (\text{Colorless})} + \text{S}_4\text{O}_6^{2-}\ (\text{Tetrathionate})$$

### 2.6 Acetate ($\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$)
1. **Action of Dilute Acid:**
   Warming with dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ evolves the characteristic, sharp **vinegar smell of acetic acid**:
   $$\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^- + \text{H}^+ \to \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}(g)\uparrow$$
2. **Ester Test:**
   Warming the salt with absolute ethanol and concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ yields the delightful, **fruity odor of ethyl acetate**:
   $$\mathbf{\text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4, \Delta} \text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5\uparrow (\text{Fruity Odor}) + \text{NaHSO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
3. **Neutral Ferric Chloride Test:**
   Adding neutral $\text{FeCl}_3$ solution to a neutral acetate solution gives an intense **blood-red coloration of basic trinuclear ferric acetate complex**:
   $$6\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^- + 3\text{Fe}^{3+} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \mathbf{[\text{Fe}_3(\text{OH})_2(\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_6]^+\quad (\text{Blood Red Coloration})} + 2\text{H}^+$$
   * When this red solution is diluted with water and boiled, it decomposes into a voluminous **reddish-brown precipitate of basic ferric acetate**:
     $$[\text{Fe}_3(\text{OH})_2(\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_6]^+ + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathbf{3\text{Fe(OH)}_2(\text{CH}_3\text{COO})\downarrow (\text{Reddish-Brown})} + 3\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{H}^+$$

## 3. Class B Anions: Concentrated Acid Group


### 3.1 Chloride ($\text{Cl}^-$)
1. **Action of Concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$:**
   Pungent, colorless fumes of $\text{HCl}$ that fume in moist air and produce **dense white fumes of $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$** when contacted with a glass rod dipped in aqueous ammonia:
   $$\text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{NaHSO}_4 + \text{HCl}\uparrow$$
   $$\text{HCl} + \text{NH}_3 \to \mathbf{\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}(s)\quad (\text{Dense White Fumes})}$$
2. **Silver Nitrate Test:**
   Adding $\text{AgNO}_3$ solution gives a **curdy white precipitate of $\text{AgCl}$**:
   $$\text{Cl}^- + \text{Ag}^+ \to \mathbf{\text{AgCl}\downarrow (\text{Curdy White})}$$
   * **Solubility Behavior:** $\text{AgCl}$ dissolves completely in dilute aqueous ammonia due to the formation of the linear coordination complex **diamminesilver(I)**:
     $$\mathbf{\text{AgCl}(s) + 2\text{NH}_3(aq) \rightleftharpoons [\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]^+ + \text{Cl}^-}$$
   * Acidifying the ammoniacal solution with dilute $\text{HNO}_3$ reprecipitates curdy white $\text{AgCl}$.

### 3.2 The Chromyl Chloride Test ($\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2$)


![Chromyl Chloride, Brown Ring, and Special Anion Tests](/media/chromyl_chloride_brown_ring_and_special_anion_tests.webp)
*Description: Two-panel inorganic mechanism and coordination chemistry graphic: (A) Classical benchmark anion confirmatory reactions detailing the multi-step Chromyl Chloride test ($\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2$ deep red vapors $\to$ bright yellow $\text{Na}_2\text{CrO}_4 \to$ chrome yellow $\text{PbCrO}_4\downarrow$) and its critical exceptions ($\text{HgCl}_2, \text{AgCl}, \text{PbCl}_2$), alongside the Nitrate Brown Ring test highlighting the unique $+1$ oxidation state of iron in $[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{NO})]^{2+}$ ($d^7$, $sp^3d^2$, $\mu = 3.87\text{ BM}$) with nitrosonium $\text{NO}^+$ coordination; (B) Special Group (Class C) confirmatory pathways detailing acid-insoluble $\text{BaSO}_4$, the wooden match stick reduction test on a silver coin ($\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$ black stain), the canary yellow ammonium phosphomolybdate complex, and the ethyl borate green-edged flame test.*


* **Reaction Sequence:**
  1. A solid mixture of the dry chloride salt and powdered potassium dichromate ($\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$) in a $1:2$ ratio is heated with concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$:
     $$\mathbf{4\text{NaCl} + \text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7 + 6\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2\uparrow (\text{Deep Red / Orange Vapors}) + 2\text{KHSO}_4 + 4\text{NaHSO}_4 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
  2. The deep red-orange vapors of **Chromyl Chloride ($\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2$)** are passed into a test tube containing dilute sodium hydroxide solution. The solution turns **bright yellow** due to the formation of sodium chromate:
     $$\mathbf{\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \to \text{Na}_2\text{CrO}_4(aq) (\text{Bright Yellow Solution}) + 2\text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
  3. The yellow solution is acidified with acetic acid and treated with lead acetate solution. A **bright yellow precipitate of lead chromate ($\text{PbCrO}_4$)** forms:
     $$\mathbf{\text{Na}_2\text{CrO}_4 + \text{Pb(CH}_3\text{COO})_2 \to \text{PbCrO}_4\downarrow (\text{Chrome Yellow}) + 2\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}}$$
* **CRITICAL JEE LIMITATIONS OF THE CHROMYL CHLORIDE TEST:**
  1. **Covalent & Insoluble Chlorides Fail:** Chlorides of $\text{Hg}^{2+}, \text{Hg}_2^{2+}, \text{Ag}^+, \text{Pb}^{2+}, \text{Sn}^{2+}$ (e.g., $\text{HgCl}_2, \text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2, \text{AgCl}, \text{PbCl}_2$) do **NOT** give the chromyl chloride test because their bonding is predominantly covalent or their lattice energy is too high, releasing insufficient free chloride ions!
  2. **Bromides and Iodides Do NOT Give Chromyl Halides:**
     * Bromides undergo oxidation to elemental bromine: $6\text{KBr} + \text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7 + 7\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \mathbf{3\text{Br}_2\uparrow (\text{Reddish-Brown})} + \text{Cr}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3 + \dots$
     * When $\text{Br}_2$ vapors are passed into $\text{NaOH}$, they form colorless hypobromite/bromide ($\text{NaBr} + \text{NaOBr}$), which gives **NO yellow color and NO precipitate with lead acetate**!
     * Iodides undergo oxidation to violet iodine vapors ($\text{I}_2$).

### 3.3 Bromide ($\text{Br}^-$) & Iodide ($\text{I}^-$)
1. **Action of Concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$:**
   * **Bromide:** Evolves reddish-brown fumes of bromine ($\text{Br}_2$) mixed with $\text{SO}_2$. Adding $\text{MnO}_2$ intensifies the dense brown fumes:
     $$2\text{KBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{SO}_2\uparrow + \mathbf{\text{Br}_2\uparrow (\text{Reddish-Brown})} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
     $$2\text{KBr} + \text{MnO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{MnSO}_4 + \mathbf{\text{Br}_2\uparrow} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
   * **Iodide:** Evolves magnificent **dense violet vapors of iodine ($\text{I}_2$)** that condense as black metallic crusts on cooler walls:
     $$2\text{KI} + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{SO}_2\uparrow + \mathbf{\text{I}_2\uparrow (\text{Violet Vapors})} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
     $$2\text{KI} + \text{MnO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{MnSO}_4 + \mathbf{\text{I}_2\uparrow} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
2. **Silver Nitrate Precipitation Matrix:**
   * **$\text{Br}^-$:** Pale yellow precipitate of $\text{AgBr}$, sparingly soluble in dilute ammonia, but **dissolves in concentrated aqueous ammonia**:
     $$\text{AgBr}(s) + 2\text{NH}_3(\text{conc}) \rightleftharpoons [\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]^+ + \text{Br}^-$$
   * **$\text{I}^-$:** Bright canary-yellow precipitate of $\text{AgI}$, completely **insoluble in both dilute and concentrated ammonia** ($K_{\text{sp}} \approx 8.3 \times 10^{-17}$).
3. **The Organic Layer Test (Chlorine Water in $\text{CCl}_4$ / $\text{CS}_2$):**
   Acidify the soda extract with dilute $\text{HCl}$, add $1-2\text{ mL}$ of carbon tetrachloride ($\text{CCl}_4$, or $\text{CS}_2 / \text{CHCl}_3$), and add freshly prepared chlorine water dropwise with vigorous shaking:
   * **For Bromide:** Chlorine oxidizes bromide to elemental bromine:
     $$\mathbf{2\text{Br}^- + \text{Cl}_2 \to 2\text{Cl}^- + \text{Br}_2}$$
     Non-polar $\text{Br}_2$ partitions into the lower organic layer, coloring it **orange / reddish-brown**.
   * **For Iodide:** Chlorine oxidizes iodide to elemental iodine:
     $$\mathbf{2\text{I}^- + \text{Cl}_2 \to 2\text{Cl}^- + \text{I}_2}$$
     Non-polar $\text{I}_2$ partitions into the lower organic layer, coloring it **intense violet / purple**.
   * **The Excess Chlorine Bleaching Trap:**
     Adding excess chlorine water oxidizes violet $\text{I}_2$ to colorless iodic acid, **completely discharging the violet color**:
     $$\mathbf{\text{I}_2 + 5\text{Cl}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{HIO}_3 (\text{Iodic Acid, Colorless}) + 10\text{HCl}}$$
     *(Note: The orange-brown color of $\text{Br}_2$ is NOT bleached by excess chlorine water; it merely lightens to yellow $\text{BrCl}$).*

### 3.4 Nitrate ($\text{NO}_3^-$)
1. **Copper Turnings Test:**
   Heating the solid nitrate salt with concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ and bright copper turnings evolves copious **dense reddish-brown fumes of $\text{NO}_2$**, while the solution turns **vibrant blue** due to cupric nitrate:
   $$2\text{NaNO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{HNO}_3 + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$$
   $$\mathbf{\text{Cu} + 4\text{HNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Cu(NO}_3)_2 (\text{Azure Blue}) + 2\text{NO}_2\uparrow (\text{Dense Brown Fumes}) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
2. **The Nitrate Brown Ring Test:**
   * To an aqueous solution of the nitrate salt, add an equal volume of freshly prepared, saturated ferrous sulfate ($\text{FeSO}_4$) solution.
   * Incline the test tube at $45^\circ$ and carefully introduce concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ dropwise down the inner wall of the tube without shaking.
   * The heavy concentrated acid settles to the bottom, and a **dark brown ring** appears at the junction of the two liquid layers.
   * **Chemical Mechanism:**
     $$\text{NO}_3^- + 3\text{Fe}^{2+} + 4\text{H}^+ \to 3\text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{NO} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
     $$\mathbf{[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} + \text{NO} \rightleftharpoons [\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{NO})]^{2+}\quad (\text{Pentaaquanitrosyliron(I) Ion, Brown Ring})} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
3. **Advanced Coordination & Electronic Structure of $[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{NO})]^{2+}$:**
   * **Formal Oxidation State of Iron:** **$+1$**!
   * **Ligand Character:** Nitrosyl transfers one electron to $\text{Fe}^{2+}$, coordinating as the **nitrosonium ion ($\text{NO}^+$)**:
     $$\text{Fe}^{2+} (d^6) + \text{NO}^0 \to \text{Fe}^+ (d^7) + \text{NO}^+$$
   * **Electronic Configuration:** $3d^7$ with high-spin octahedral geometry ($sp^3d^2$ hybridization).
   * **Magnetic Properties:** Contains **3 unpaired electrons** ($t_{2g}^5 e_g^2$); spin-only magnetic moment:
     $$\mathbf{\mu_{\text{eff}} = \sqrt{n(n+2)} = \sqrt{3(5)} = \sqrt{15} \approx 3.87\text{ BM}\quad (\text{Strongly Paramagnetic})}$$
   * **Thermal Lability:** The brown ring complex is unstable; shaking or heating decomposes the complex, releasing $\text{NO}$ and discharging the brown ring:
     $$[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{NO})]^{2+} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Fe}^{2+} + \text{NO}\uparrow + 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
   * **Interfering Anions:** $\text{NO}_2^-$ gives brown coloration throughout the solution with dilute acid. $\text{Br}^-$ and $\text{I}^-$ evolve brown $\text{Br}_2$ and violet $\text{I}_2$ rings at the acid boundary, masking the brown ring.

### 3.5 Oxalate ($\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$)
1. **Action of Concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$:**
   Concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ acts as a dehydrating agent, cleaving oxalic acid into equimolar amounts of carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide:
   $$\mathbf{\text{H}_2\text{C}_2\text{O}_4 \xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4, \Delta} \text{CO}(g)\uparrow + \text{CO}_2(g)\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   * $\text{CO}_2$ turns lime water milky.
   * $\text{CO}$ burns with a characteristic **faint blue flame** at the mouth of the test tube.
2. **Potassium Permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$) Decolorization:**
   Warming an acidified oxalate solution with dilute $\text{KMnO}_4$ at $60-70^\circ\text{C}$ slowly decolorizes the purple permanganate:
   $$\mathbf{5\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} + 2\text{MnO}_4^- + 16\text{H}^+ \xrightarrow{60-70^\circ\text{C}} 2\text{Mn}^{2+} (\text{Colorless}) + 10\text{CO}_2\uparrow + 8\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   *(Autocatalytic Reaction: The reaction is sluggish initially, but accelerates rapidly as generated $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ acts as an autocatalyst).*
3. **Calcium Chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$) Test:**
   Adding $\text{CaCl}_2$ to a neutral oxalate solution gives a **white crystalline precipitate of calcium oxalate**:
   $$\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} + \text{Ca}^{2+} \to \mathbf{\text{CaC}_2\text{O}_4\downarrow (\text{White Precipitate})}$$
   * $\text{CaC}_2\text{O}_4$ is insoluble in acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$), but dissolves readily in dilute mineral acids ($\text{HCl}, \text{HNO}_3$).

## 4. Class C Anions: Special / Precipitation Group


### 4.1 Sulfate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$)
1. **The Insoluble Barium Chloride Test:**
   Adding $\text{BaCl}_2$ solution to an acidified soda extract yields a heavy, **white precipitate of barium sulfate**:
   $$\mathbf{\text{SO}_4^{2-} + \text{Ba}^{2+} \to \text{BaSO}_4\downarrow \quad (\text{Heavy White Precipitate})}$$
   * **Acid Invariance:** **$\text{BaSO}_4$ is completely insoluble in concentrated $\text{HCl}$ and boiling concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$**, clearly distinguishing it from $\text{BaSO}_3, \text{BaCO}_3,$ and $\text{Ba}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ (all of which dissolve in mineral acids).
2. **Lead Acetate Test:**
   Adding lead acetate solution gives a white precipitate of $\text{PbSO}_4$, which dissolves in ammonium acetate:
   $$\text{SO}_4^{2-} + \text{Pb}^{2+} \to \mathbf{\text{PbSO}_4\downarrow (\text{White})}$$
   $$\text{PbSO}_4 + 2\text{CH}_3\text{COONH}_4 \to \text{Pb(CH}_3\text{COO})_2 + (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$$
3. **The Match Stick Test (Sulfate Reduction):**
   * Mix the sulfate salt with solid $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ and powdered charcoal, moisten with water, and place on the head of a charred wooden matchstick.
   * Heat intensely in the reducing zone of a Bunsen flame:
     $$\mathbf{\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + 4\text{C} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Na}_2\text{S} + 4\text{CO}\uparrow}$$
   * Place the fused bead on a clean, shiny silver coin and moisten with a drop of water.
   * An intense **black stain of silver sulfide ($\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$)** forms on the coin surface:
     $$\mathbf{\text{Na}_2\text{S} + 2\text{Ag} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2 \to \text{Ag}_2\text{S}\downarrow (\text{Black Stain on Silver Coin}) + 2\text{NaOH}}$$

### 4.2 Phosphate ($\text{PO}_4^{3-}$)
1. **The Ammonium Molybdate Confirmatory Test:**
   * To the salt solution, add concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ and boil.
   * Add a large excess of ammonium molybdate solution $((\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4)$ and warm gently ($40-50^\circ\text{C}$):
     $$\mathbf{\text{HPO}_4^{2-} + 12(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4 + 23\text{H}^+ \xrightarrow{\Delta} (\text{NH}_4)_3[\text{P(Mo}_3\text{O}_{10})_4]\downarrow (\text{Canary Yellow}) + 21\text{NH}_4^+ + 12\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   * Forms a voluminous, heavy **canary-yellow crystalline precipitate of Ammonium Phosphomolybdate** (also formulated as $(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{PO}_4 \cdot 12\text{MoO}_3$).
   * **Dissolution:** The canary-yellow precipitate dissolves readily in excess alkalis ($\text{NaOH}$) and aqueous ammonia.
2. **Magnesia Mixture Test:**
   Adding magnesia mixture ($\text{MgCl}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{NH}_4\text{OH}$) gives a **white crystalline precipitate of magnesium ammonium phosphate**:
   $$\text{PO}_4^{3-} + \text{Mg}^{2+} + \text{NH}_4^+ \to \mathbf{\text{Mg(NH}_4)\text{PO}_4 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}\downarrow (\text{White Crystalline})}$$

### 4.3 Borate ($\text{BO}_3^{3-}$ / $\text{B}_4\text{O}_7^{2-}$)
1. **The Ethyl Borate Flame Test:**
   Mix a pinch of the borate salt with concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ and $2-3\text{ mL}$ of absolute ethanol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$) in a porcelain dish, stir well, and ignite the vapors:
   $$\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 5\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + 4\text{H}_3\text{BO}_3$$
   $$\mathbf{\text{H}_3\text{BO}_3 + 3\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4} (\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_3\text{BO}_3\uparrow (\text{Triethyl Borate Vapors}) + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}}$$
   * The triethyl borate vapors burn with a spectacular **luminous green-edged flame**!
   * Interference: Copper salts also give a green flame, but copper is non-volatile; performing the test in an ignition tube or using methyl alcohol avoids interference.
2. **The Turmeric Paper Test:**
   * Acidify a borate solution with dilute $\text{HCl}$ and dip a strip of turmeric paper into it.
   * Dry the paper carefully on a steam watch glass: it turns **distinctive cherry-red / reddish-brown** due to the formation of the dye complex **rosocyanin**.
   * Moisten the red paper with dilute $\text{NaOH}$ or ammonia solution: the color instantly transforms into **inky greenish-black**!

### 5.2 High-Yield Exam Traps & Common Diagnostic Errors


#### Trap 1: Nitrate vs. Nitrite in the Brown Ring Test
* **Nitrite ($\text{NO}_2^-$):** Forms $[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{NO})]^{2+}$ with dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ in the cold, producing dark brown color throughout the solution.
* **Nitrate ($\text{NO}_3^-$):** Does NOT react with dilute acid; strictly requires concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ as a dehydrating/protonating agent to form a localized brown ring at the acid junction.
* **Removal of Nitrite:** If $\text{NO}_2^-$ is present alongside $\text{NO}_3^-$, boil the mixture with urea or sulfamic acid in dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ to destroy $\text{NO}_2^-$ before testing for $\text{NO}_3^-$:
  $$\text{NH}_2\text{CONH}_2 + 2\text{HNO}_2 \to 2\text{N}_2\uparrow + \text{CO}_2\uparrow + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$$


#### Trap 2: Sulfite ($\text{SO}_3^{2-}$) vs. Thiosulfate ($\text{S}_2\text{O}_3^{2-}$) Acidification
* Both evolve $\text{SO}_2$ gas that turns acidified dichromate paper green.
* **Key Discrimination:** Sulfite yields a completely clear, transparent solution. Thiosulfate yields an immediate or progressive **milky pale-yellow turbidity of colloidal elemental sulfur ($\text{S}\downarrow$)**.


#### Trap 3: The Chromyl Chloride Test Trapdoor
* Remember: $\text{HgCl}_2, \text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2, \text{AgCl}, \text{PbCl}_2, \text{SnCl}_2$ do NOT give the chromyl chloride test!
* If a question asks *"A white crystalline chloride salt gives a white precipitate with $\text{AgNO}_3$ but fails the chromyl chloride test"*, the cation must be $\text{Hg}^{2+}, \text{Hg}_2^{2+}, \text{Ag}^+,$ or $\text{Pb}^{2+}$.


#### Trap 4: Halide Interference in the Brown Ring Test
* Bromides ($\text{Br}^-$) and Iodides ($\text{I}^-$) react with concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ at the junction to evolve reddish-brown $\text{Br}_2$ and violet $\text{I}_2$ vapor rings, obscuring the delicate brown ring of nitrate.
* **Removal of Halides:** Halides must be precipitated by adding silver sulfate ($\text{Ag}_2\text{SO}_4$) solution; after filtering off the silver halides, the clear filtrate is tested for nitrate.
